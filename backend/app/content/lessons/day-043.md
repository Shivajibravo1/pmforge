## An agile transformation, anonymised

You are asked to review an agile transformation at a company. This is a composite, built to teach recurring patterns, so there is no real company behind it. Treat it as illustrative.

```note
This case is fictional and anonymised. It combines patterns commonly described in published accounts of agile transformations. It is not a record of one real organisation and contains no real figures.
```

```chat
# Review kickoff, as imagined
Chief executive | Company | We spent a year and a lot of money on agile. Delivery is no faster and morale is lower. Tell me why.
Programme director | Transformation office | We trained everyone and renamed the teams. We reported the transformation as complete.
Team lead | Delivery team | We do sprints. We still need a signature from three managers for any change.
```

## What the review found

```cards
users | Training without change | Everyone attended courses, but roles, decisions and incentives stayed as they were. | New words for old behaviour.

chart | Measuring the wrong thing | Success was reported as the number of teams trained and sprints run. | Activity measured, outcomes ignored.
clock | Fixed plans stayed | Annual plans with fixed scope and dates continued, now with sprint labels. | Waterfall with stand ups.
```

## How it unfolded

```timeline
Month 1 | Launch with large training programme | ok
Month 4 | Teams renamed, sprints started, approvals unchanged | warn
Month 8 | Reports show full adoption, delivery times unchanged | warn
Month 12 | Staff survey shows lower trust, some key people leave | bad
Month 13 | Review commissioned | info
```

## Symptoms and root causes

```flow
Training only | Skills added, structure unchanged
Control kept | Decisions stay at the top
Wrong measures | Adoption counted, outcomes not
Plan unchanged | Fixed scope and date remain
Result | Agile in name, no benefit
```

The symptoms, such as slow delivery and low morale, are visible. The root causes lie in decision rights, incentives, measurement and planning. Training alone does not change them.

```ref

```


## Your decision as reviewer

```decision
? Which recommendation goes first in your report?
- Provide more training to the middle managers | Useful, but it treats the symptom. The structure that blocks teams stays in place.
- Replace the transformation office | Personal, and it may not fix anything if the incentives stay the same.
+ Change decision rights and measures: give teams authority within clear boundaries, measure outcomes such as lead time and customer value, and align planning and funding with them | It attacks the root causes and gives the other actions something to stand on.
= Fix the system that shapes behaviour: who decides, what is measured and how money flows. Training supports it and does not replace it.
```

## What a better version looks like

Reviews of real transformations often point to the same conditions for success. Treat these as common patterns to test, not guarantees.

```accordion
Leaders change first | Leaders change how they decide, fund and measure before asking teams to change.
Start small and learn | A few teams start, the lessons are shared, and the approach spreads when it works.
Measure outcomes | Lead time, customer feedback and quality, not the number of sprints.
Remove blockers | A visible list of organisational obstacles, with owners and dates.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 guiding principles: start where you are, progress iteratively with feedback | Better transformations start from the real current state and move in steps.
```
