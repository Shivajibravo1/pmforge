## A chatbot answers a grieving customer

In 2024, a Canadian tribunal ruled on a dispute where an airline's website chatbot gave a customer wrong information about bereavement fares. The airline argued it was not responsible for what its chatbot said. The tribunal disagreed. The case is one of the most cited examples of a company being held to account for the words of its own AI assistant.

```note
This lesson summarises widely reported public facts about Moffatt v. Air Canada, decided by the British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal in February 2024. Check the tribunal's published decision and reputable reports before you quote any detail. The lesson deliberately avoids precise figures. It is education, not legal advice.
```

## What the tribunal reportedly decided

```timeline
Before travel | A passenger asks the chatbot about bereavement fares after a family death | info
Chatbot answer | The answer reportedly said the discount could be claimed after travel | warn
Afterwards | The airline declined the claim, pointing to the policy page | bad
Dispute | The passenger brings a claim to the tribunal | info
Decision | The tribunal reportedly found the airline responsible for the information on its site, including the chatbot, and awarded the passenger a refund of the difference and some costs | ok
```

## Why it matters to a project manager

```cards
scale | Accountability stays with the organisation | A company cannot treat its chatbot as a separate entity responsible for itself. | Launching AI with no owner for what it says.
file | Two sources, one truth | The chatbot and the policy page disagreed. Customers cannot be expected to know which to trust. | Content and policy maintained in different places.
users | Vulnerable moments | Bereavement is a high stakes, emotional moment. | Applying the same low care to every topic.
eye | Monitoring | Wrong answers need to be found by you before your customers find them. | No sampling and no review.
```

## What a better delivery plan looks like

```flow
Scope | Limit the assistant to topics it can answer from approved sources
Sources | One authoritative policy, owned and dated
Grounding | Answers cite the source and quote the rule
Escalate | Sensitive topics go to a person
Monitor | Sample conversations, track complaints and corrections
Own | A named owner who can switch it off
```

## Controls to ask for

```accordion
Grounded answers with citations | The assistant answers from approved documents and shows the source link.
Refusal and handover | When unsure or on sensitive topics such as refunds, bereavement or legal matters, hand over to a person.
Disclosure | Tell users they are talking to an AI and where official policy lives.
Testing on real questions | An evaluation set that includes high stakes policies and tricky phrasing.
Content governance | When policy changes, the knowledge base is updated and the assistant tested before the change is live.
Complaint path | A simple way for users to report wrong answers, and a process to learn from them.
```

## Your decision

Your own assistant is about to go live. An executive suggests adding a line in the terms that the assistant "may be inaccurate and the company is not responsible for its statements".

```decision
? What do you recommend?
- Rely on the disclaimer, since it protects the company | The case suggests a disclaimer is not a substitute for accuracy, and customers still rely on answers.
- Remove the assistant entirely | An overreaction that loses a useful service.
+ Keep a clear disclosure, but also ground answers in approved policy with citations, hand over sensitive topics, monitor and correct wrong answers quickly, and ask legal counsel to review the wording | Controls reduce the harm, and legal protection is a supplement, not the plan.
= A disclaimer does not replace controls. Reduce the chance and impact of wrong answers.
```

## What not to conclude

```cards
alert | Not a case about bad technology alone | It is about governance, ownership and customer reliance. | Blaming the tool and ignoring the process.
check | Not a ban on chatbots | Many organisations run assistants responsibly with the right controls. | Overcorrecting into doing nothing.
scale | Not a general legal rule | Decisions in one place do not bind all others. Seek local legal advice. | Treating one ruling as universal law.
```
