## Five minutes with the executive committee

Daniel has one slot on Thursday. Ten projects compete for the same budget. He turns to you.

```chat
# Prep call, Wednesday, 6:10 PM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | The committee will ask why Lighthouse and not the warehouse upgrade. I have five minutes. What do I say?
Finance partner | Finance | Give me costs, benefits and who owns each benefit. Not adjectives.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | Contact centre calls about order status are our best hard number.
```

If your answer is vague, your project loses priority the first time it hits trouble. A business case is the clear answer, written down.

```key
A business case answers one question: why spend money and attention on this instead of something else?
```

## What goes in it

```cards
search | Problem or opportunity | What is wrong or possible, with evidence. | A solution looking for a problem.
layers | Options | At least three: do nothing, your proposal, and a smaller or cheaper alternative. | Only one option, so the decision looks rigged.
money | Costs | One-off (build, licences, migration, training) and recurring (support, hosting, subscriptions). | Running costs are forgotten.
chart | Benefits | Money saved, revenue gained, risk reduced, time saved. Separate hard from soft. | Soft benefits are sold as hard savings.
alert | Risks and assumptions | What could change the numbers. | The case collapses when one assumption fails.
check | Recommendation | The option you advise and why. | No clear ask, so no decision.
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, business case and benefits management | Know that a business case is checked against benefits after delivery, not only before approval.
COBIT | COBIT 2019 governance objective on benefits delivery | If the client uses COBIT, tie your business case to benefits delivery governance.
```


## ROI in plain words

Return on investment asks: for every unit of money we put in, how much extra do we get back?

```key
ROI = (total benefit minus total cost) divided by total cost
```

Worked example with illustrative numbers. A project costs 200,000 over three years and saves 90,000 each year.

```stats
200,000 | Total cost
270,000 | Total benefit
35% | ROI
Year 3 | Payback point
```

Total benefit is 3 x 90,000 = 270,000. ROI is (270,000 minus 200,000) divided by 200,000 = 35 percent. Payback is where cumulative savings catch up with cost, here in year three.

## Your decision

Finance asks you to pick the headline benefit for the slide.

```decision
? Which benefit goes at the top of the slide?
- "Better customer experience", because it is the biggest idea | It cannot be measured, so Finance discounts it to zero and the committee hears marketing.
- "Fewer calls, 40 percent fewer", with no source | A number nobody can trace. The first question from the room is "where does that come from?" and you have no answer.
+ "Fewer order status calls to the contact centre, with last quarter's call volume as the baseline and Marcus as the benefit owner" | A hard, measurable benefit with a baseline and an owner. Finance can check it, and the committee can hold someone to it.
= Lead with a benefit that is measurable, has a baseline and has a named owner. Soft benefits support the case but never carry it.
```

## The hidden cost line in AI projects

An AI feature usually has costs that repeat every month. Leave them out and the case looks better than it is.

```cards
money | Usage costs | Many AI services charge by how much they are used. | A popular feature quietly becomes expensive.
users | Human review time | People check the AI's work, especially at the start. | The savings disappear into review time.
chart | Measuring the benefit | Compare before and after with real numbers. | Benefit is claimed from a demo, not from measured results.
```

```ai
Show a range, not one number. Say which assumptions matter most, such as how many questions per month and how many need a human check.
```

```ref
NIST | AI RMF Measure function | Measure both value and risk of an AI feature, then put both in the business case.
Azure | Well-Architected Framework, cost optimization pillar | Running cost of cloud and AI services belongs in the case. Cite the cost pillar for ongoing spend.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Estimates model usage, evaluation effort and tuning. | Forecasts based on the pilot only.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Time on site for deployment and tuning. | Effort not in the business case.
shield | AI security consultant | Testing, review and ongoing monitoring effort. | Treated as free.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Build and run of logging, guardrails and evaluation. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Run cost missing.
money | Finance partner | Needs a forecast with ranges and clear assumptions. | A single number that hides uncertainty.
```

```incident
# Usage based costs exceeded the forecast (illustrative)
What happened | An AI assistant was priced by usage. Adoption was higher than planned, and monthly costs rose above the business case.
Impact | The sponsor had to approve extra budget, and the benefit case looked weaker.
Root cause | The business case counted build cost but treated running cost as fixed and based it on the pilot only.
Resolution | The team added usage caps, cost alerts and a range based forecast, and reviewed the case with finance each quarter.
PM move | Put run costs, usage assumptions and caps in the business case, and review them after launch.
```

## Three cautions

```warn
Benefits with no owner are fiction. Always include running costs, because a cheap build with an expensive operation is not cheap. When estimates are uncertain, show a range, not a single number.
```

## Check your understanding

```quiz
? A project costs 100,000 and delivers 150,000 in benefit. What is the ROI?
- 150 percent
+ 50 percent
- 33 percent
= (150,000 minus 100,000) divided by 100,000 is 0.5, so 50 percent.
```

```answer
? How do you build a business case for an AI project?
- Show the technology is impressive | Executives approve outcomes and risk, not technology.
- Give one ROI number | A single figure hides assumptions and uncertainty.
+ I start with the problem and the measurable benefit, then list all costs: build, run, data, security, training and change. I include usage based costs and a range for benefits, state assumptions, show payback and risks, and describe what we will measure after launch. I review the case at gates so the sponsor can stop or continue with evidence.
= Mention run costs. Many candidates only list build costs.
= Use ranges and assumptions, not a single number.
= Say how and when you will check the benefit after launch.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
Give at least three options, including do nothing
Separate one-off costs from running costs
Hard benefits are measurable in money, soft ones are not
Every benefit needs a named owner and a baseline
ROI = (benefit minus cost) divided by cost
```


```mindmap
# Business case
Benefit | Problem, measure, range, timing
Cost | Build, run, data, security, training
Risk | Assumptions, uncertainty, dependencies
Governance | Gates, review, post launch measure
```

```flashcards
ROI in plain words | The gain from the project compared with what it costs, over a stated period.
Hidden AI costs | Usage based fees, evaluation, monitoring, data preparation and change management.
Why use ranges | They show uncertainty honestly and build trust.
Post launch check | Measure the benefit after go live and compare it with the case.
```
