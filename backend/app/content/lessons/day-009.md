## A business case answers one question

"Why should we spend money and attention on this instead of something else?" If the answer is not clear, the project will lose priority the first time it hits trouble.

## The parts of a simple business case

- **Problem or opportunity.** What is wrong or possible, with evidence.
- **Options.** At least three: do nothing, the proposed solution, and a cheaper or smaller alternative.
- **Costs.** One-off (build, licences, migration, training) and recurring (support, hosting, subscriptions).
- **Benefits.** Cost saved, revenue gained, risk reduced, time saved. Separate hard benefits (measurable in money) from soft ones.
- **Risks and assumptions.**
- **Recommendation.**

## ROI basics

Return on investment compares benefit with cost over a period.

ROI = (total benefit minus total cost) divided by total cost

Example with illustrative numbers: a project costs 200,000 over three years and saves 90,000 per year. Total benefit is 270,000. ROI = (270,000 minus 200,000) divided by 200,000 = 35 percent. Payback is the point where cumulative savings equal cost, here in year three.

## Cautions

- Benefits claimed with no owner are fiction. Name who is responsible for realising each benefit.
- Include run costs. A cheap build with an expensive operation is not cheap.
- Show a range, not a single number, when estimates are uncertain.
