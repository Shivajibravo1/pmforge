## A business case answers one question

```key
Why should we spend money and attention on this instead of something else? If the answer is not clear, the project loses priority the first time it hits trouble.
```

## The parts of a simple business case

```cards
search | Problem or opportunity | What is wrong or possible, with evidence.
layers | Options | At least three: do nothing, the proposed solution, and a cheaper or smaller alternative.
money | Costs | One-off (build, licences, migration, training) and recurring (support, hosting, subscriptions).
chart | Benefits | Cost saved, revenue gained, risk reduced, time saved. Separate hard from soft.
alert | Risks and assumptions | What could change the numbers.
check | Recommendation | The option you advise and why.
```

## ROI basics

Return on investment compares benefit with cost over a period.

```key
ROI = (total benefit minus total cost) divided by total cost
```

Worked example with illustrative numbers: a project costs 200,000 over three years and saves 90,000 per year.

```stats
200,000 | Total cost
270,000 | Total benefit
35% | ROI
Year 3 | Payback point
```

Total benefit is 270,000. ROI = (270,000 minus 200,000) divided by 200,000 = 35 percent. Payback is where cumulative savings equal cost, here in year three.

## Cautions

```warn
Benefits claimed with no owner are fiction. Include run costs, because a cheap build with an expensive operation is not cheap. Show a range, not a single number, when estimates are uncertain.
```

```quiz
? A project costs 100,000 and delivers 150,000 of benefit. What is the ROI?
- 150 percent
+ 50 percent
- 33 percent
= (150,000 minus 100,000) divided by 100,000 is 0.5, so 50 percent.
```
