## "Show me the plan"

The infrastructure lead has finished the inventory. Victoria wants a migration plan for one real three tier application, the Nimbus order portal, by Friday. It has a web front end, an application tier and a database. Three teams and one vendor touch it.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 9:30 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | I need a plan I can approve. Waves, dates, cost, risks and what happens if it goes wrong.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | The database is the hard part. Large, busy and full of customer orders.
Elena Rossi | CISO | I need to see the security controls before data moves.
```

A migration plan for a three tier application is a good first deliverable, because it forces you to think about data, dependencies, cutover and rollback. A plan that shows only dates is a calendar. A plan that shows approach, risk, cost and a way back is a plan.

```key
A migration plan answers six questions: what moves, in which order, how, who does what, what it costs and how you go back if it fails.
```

## Anatomy of a three tier migration

```arch
Users | Browsers and mobile app
Web tier | Front end servers or a managed web service
Application tier | Business logic, APIs and integrations
Data tier | Database with orders and customers
Connections | Identity, network, monitoring and backup
```

## What the plan should contain

```cards
list | Scope and strategy | Which of the 7 Rs for each tier, with reasons. | Moving everything the same way.
workflow | Sequence and waves | Order of moves, dependencies and test points. | Moving the front end before the data is ready.
database | Data migration approach | Copy, sync, cutover window and checks. | Lost or duplicated orders.
shield | Security and compliance | Controls in place before data moves. | Retrofitting security after launch.
clock | Cutover and rollback | A timed runbook and a clear point of no return. | No way back.
coins | Cost and benefit | One time and running costs against savings. | Only the migration cost is counted.
```

```ref
AWS | Prescriptive guidance for migration planning | A migration plan includes discovery, dependencies, sequence, cutover and rollback. Use the guidance as a checklist.
```


## A typical sequence

```flow
Prepare | Landing zone, access, network, monitoring
Replicate | Copy data and keep it in sync
Test | Functional, performance, security and failover tests
Cutover | Short freeze, final sync, switch traffic
Hypercare | Close watch, fix issues, then decommission
```

## Your decision

Rahul says the database cutover needs a four hour freeze. The business wants no downtime.

```decision
? What do you propose?
- Do a hidden cutover in the night with no announcement | A problem during the window would hit unprepared customers and staff.
- Refuse any freeze and delay the migration | The date and lease are at risk.
+ Offer a planned low traffic window of one to two hours using continuous replication, announce it, rehearse the cutover twice, and agree a rollback decision time with the sponsor | The business gets a short, known window, and the team gets rehearsed steps and a clear fallback.
= Replace "no downtime" with a short rehearsed window and a rollback point.
```

## Risk register extract

```rag
Data loss | R | Mitigated with replication, checksums and a rehearsed rollback
Performance | A | Load test before cutover, then adjust sizing
Security | A | CISO approval before data moves
Vendor dependency | A | Vendor support confirmed for the window
Skills | A | Pair vendor and internal engineers
```

```incident
# Cutover plan from a generic AI draft (illustrative)
What happened | A PM used an AI draft as the migration plan for a three tier application. It missed a batch job and a licence dependency.
Impact | The batch job failed after cutover and the team rolled back.
Root cause | The draft was generic and was not checked against the actual application inventory.
Resolution | The PM validated each step against the inventory with owners, added the missing items and rehearsed the rollback.
PM move | Check any AI drafted plan against the real inventory and rehearse rollback.
```

```answer
? What should a migration plan for a three tier application contain?
- A list of servers | It lacks sequence and risk.
- A date only | A date without steps is a hope.
+ Scope and inventory, target design, sequence for data, application and web tiers, cutover and rollback steps, testing and parity checks, risks, owners, communication and a decision on go or no go. I validate it against the real inventory with owners and rehearse rollback. For AI features I add quality checks after the move.
= Sequence and rollback.
= Inventory checked.
= Go or no go criteria.
```
