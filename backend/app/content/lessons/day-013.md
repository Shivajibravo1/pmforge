## You are the independent reviewer

It is April 2018. A British bank, TSB, moves millions of customer accounts from an old banking platform to a new one over a single weekend. When customers log in on Monday, many cannot get in, and some reported seeing other people's accounts. You are brought in as an independent reviewer. Your job is to separate what customers saw from why it happened.

```chat
Regulator | Financial regulator | We need to understand what was known before go-live, and who decided it was ready.
```

## What was reported

```cards
alert | Customers locked out | Many could not access accounts for days or weeks, and the bank's own service channels were overwhelmed. | A migration judged by what customers experience, not by weekend completion.
layers | A very large move at once | A big cutover with a single migration event concentrated risk. | When it fails, there is no partial success to fall back on.
clock | Readiness questioned | The independent review concluded the new platform was not ready for service when the migration went ahead. | Readiness criteria that were not tested honestly.
users | Governance and oversight | Reviews pointed to weaknesses in how readiness was challenged and how suppliers were overseen. | Confidence reported upward that the evidence did not support.
```

## How the pressure builds

```timeline
Before | A large, complex migration is planned around a single big weekend | warn
Build | Testing happens, but gaps in realistic, full scale testing are later criticised | warn
Cutover | The migration weekend goes ahead | info
Next days | Customers locked out, incorrect data seen by some, service channels swamped | bad
Weeks after | Prolonged stabilisation and independent review | bad
```

```flow
Large single cutover | One event holds all the risk
Readiness not proven | Testing and challenge fall short
Go decision | Taken on confidence, not evidence
Customer impact | Public and prolonged
```

No single mistake explains it. A large all at once cutover, readiness that was not proven, and a go decision that did not rest on hard evidence together made a serious failure much more likely.

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, governance and schedule pressure | A fixed date with growing scope is a governance problem, not a team problem. Escalate it with options.
```


## Your decision as reviewer

The review team has two possible findings to lead with.

```decision
? Which finding is the stronger root cause to lead with?
- Customers could not log in on Monday | That is the symptom. It describes what happened, not why it was allowed to happen.
- A few specific technical defects in the new platform | Defects happen on every project. Focusing on them lets the real cause hide.
+ The go decision was taken without proven readiness, because the governance did not demand independent evidence before a single large cutover | This explains why the failure was possible. It points to controls you can add to any project.
= Lead with the decision structure, not the defect list. Defects are inevitable. A system that lets an unready change go live is the root cause you can fix.
```

## What you can reuse

```cards
check | A written go or no go standard | Agreed before the date is fixed, with evidence required. | Opinion decides, and hope replaces proof.
layers | Smaller, reversible steps | Migrate in waves, with a rollback plan tested in rehearsal. | One big weekend with no way back.
eye | Independent challenge | Someone outside the team tests whether readiness claims are true. | Everyone inside reports green.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 release management and change enablement practices | Staged release, pilot and rollback are standard release controls. List them as lessons you reuse.
NIST | SP 800-34 contingency planning guide | A rollback and recovery plan is a contingency plan. Cite it when asked how you plan for a failed cut-over.
ISO | ISO 22301 business continuity management | If a migration touches customer service, ask for the continuity plan.
```

```answer
? What lessons would you take from a large, failed IT migration?
- Pick a better vendor next time | Treats it as a supplier problem. Reviews usually show governance and testing gaps as well.
- It was bad luck | Similar patterns recur and can be managed.
+ I look for patterns that repeat: pressure on a date that skips rehearsal, end to end testing that does not match real conditions, unclear ownership of the whole, and optimistic reporting. I would use phased cutover, rehearsed rollback, independent assurance and clear go live criteria. For the specific public facts of a case I would check the current record.
= Name patterns, not people.
= Say you would verify the details of any real case.
= Add one control you would use: phased cutover or rehearsed rollback.
```

```mindmap
# Migration failure patterns
Pressure | Fixed dates, optimistic reporting
Testing | End to end, load, rollback rehearsal
Ownership | One owner of the whole, independent assurance
Controls | Phased cutover, go live criteria, support readiness
```

```flashcards
Big bang versus phased | Big bang moves everything at once. Phased moves groups in steps so problems stay small.
Rollback rehearsal | Practising the return path before cutover so it works under pressure.
Independent assurance | A review by someone outside the delivery team who can report to leaders.
Go live criteria | Conditions agreed in advance that must be met before cutover.
```

```incident
# Big bang cutover with an untested rollback (illustrative)
What happened | A bank style platform migration moved all customers in one weekend. Parts had been tested separately, and the rollback plan had not been rehearsed end to end.
Impact | Many customers could not access services for days, and the support team was overwhelmed.
Root cause | Pressure to hit a date led to skipped end to end rehearsals and an untested rollback.
Resolution | Cutover was paused, fixes were made and customers moved in smaller groups with rehearsed rollback.
PM move | Prefer phased cutover, rehearse rollback, and give leaders a clear choice between date and readiness.
```
