## The governance pack for a vendor you cannot lose

The vendor's assistant platform is now critical to Lighthouse. The contract is signed, the service is live, and the first incident has taught everyone something. Victoria asks for a pack that sets out how the vendor will be managed for the next two years.

```chat
# Message, Friday, 11:00 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | I want to see how we know the vendor is performing, how we find out early if it is not, and what we do about it.
Procurement | Procurement | The pack must connect to the contract. I do not want promises that the contract does not support.
Elena Rossi | CISO | And the security review cycle, with evidence.
```

Vendor governance is not suspicion. It is a routine that keeps both sides honest, spots problems early and manages change. It is also one of the clearest signs of a mature project manager, because most failures with suppliers come from absent governance, not from bad contracts.

```key
A governance pack states who meets, how often, what they review, what evidence they see and what happens when performance falls short.
```

## What goes in the pack

```cards
users | Roles and meetings | Named owners on both sides, with an operational, a service and an executive review. | Meetings with no agenda or owner.
chart | Performance measures | SLAs, quality measures and trend reports. | Measures chosen by the vendor only.
alert | Risks and issues | A shared log reviewed at every meeting. | Issues discussed but never recorded.
file | Change and commercial control | How changes, invoices and credits are handled. | Disputes at renewal time.
shield | Security and compliance | Audits, certificates and incident notification. | Evidence collected only when asked.
```

## A review rhythm

| Forum | Frequency | Who attends | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Operational review | Weekly | Delivery leads | Incidents, changes, near term plans |
| Service review | Monthly | Service owners and vendor manager | SLA performance, trends, risks |
| Executive review | Quarterly | Sponsor, vendor executive | Value, strategy, relationship |

## Reporting that matters

```rag
Availability | A | 99.6 percent against a 99.9 target this month
Response times | G | Within agreed levels
Answer quality | A | Accuracy on the test set fell two points
Security | G | Audit report received on time
Commercial | G | Invoices match the contract
```

Illustrative figures. The pattern to copy is that every tile has a measure, a target and a reason for its colour.

## Your decision

The vendor suggests replacing the monthly service review with a quarterly one to reduce overhead.

```decision
? How do you respond?
- Agree, since it saves time | Problems are found three months later, and the vendor controls the story.
- Refuse any change in the rhythm | Rigid, and it ignores a fair wish to reduce overhead if performance is strong.
+ Offer a lighter monthly review with a dashboard and exception reporting, and agree to move to quarterly reviews if measures stay on target for six months, with a return to monthly if they fall | You reward good performance with less overhead and keep the early warning.
= Tie the intensity of governance to performance. Reduce overhead as trust is earned, and keep the right to increase it.
```

## A healthy exit

```accordion
Exit plan | Describe how data is returned, in what format and how long the vendor supports a transition.
Knowledge transfer | Document key configuration and prompts, not only code.
Alternatives | Keep a view of other suppliers, in case you need one.
Termination triggers | Agree in advance what level of failure allows exit.
```

## AI vendor governance

```cards
brain | Model changes | Require advance notice and a chance to test before the vendor changes a model. | Silent changes to behaviour.
```
