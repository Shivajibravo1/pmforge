## The backlog that nobody could read

Sprint one is one week away. Marcus has produced a backlog of seventy items. Rahul scrolls through it on the shared screen and winces.

```chat
# Backlog review, Monday, 10:00 AM
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | "Improve order experience". What do I build on Tuesday morning?
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | "Support the service desk". Support them how?
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | I wrote them quickly. I can add detail.
Elena Rossi | CISO | "Make it secure" is not something I can test.
```

A backlog is only useful if each item can be understood, estimated and finished within a sprint. A vague item is a small hidden project. A good user story is a short, specific piece of value that a team can discuss, size and test.

```key
A user story captures who wants what and why. Acceptance criteria say how you will know it is done. The INVEST checklist tells you if it is ready.
```

## The shape of a story

```accordion
Format | As a type of user, I want a goal, so that I get a benefit.
Example (illustrative) | As a customer, I want to see the current status of my order, so that I do not need to phone the contact centre.
Acceptance criteria | Given a shipped order, when I open the tracking page, then I see the status and the expected delivery date. Given no shipment scan, then I see a preparing message.
```

```ref
IIBA | Agile Extension to the BABOK Guide, user stories and acceptance criteria | Use for story format and the link between a story, its acceptance criteria and the business need.
```


## INVEST: is it ready?

```cards
link | Independent | Can be built without waiting for many others. | A tangled chain that blocks the sprint.
users | Negotiable | A conversation starter, not a contract. | Rigid detail written too early.
flag | Valuable | Worth something to a user or the business. | Technical tasks with no visible value.
clock | Estimable | The team can size it. | Too vague or too new to estimate.
layers | Small | Fits within a sprint, ideally a few days. | An epic that never finishes.
check | Testable | You can say clearly when it passes. | "Make it fast" with no measure.
```

## Slicing a large item

Big items, often called epics, are sliced vertically. A vertical slice cuts through every layer and delivers something usable, rather than a horizontal layer, such as "build the database", that nobody can use.

```flow
Epic | Customers can track orders
Slice 1 | See the latest status of one order
Slice 2 | See a list of all my orders
Slice 3 | Get a notification when status changes
```

## Your decision

Marcus has an item, "Build the order database". It is large and has no visible value on its own.

```decision
? What do you suggest?
- Keep it as it is, since databases are necessary | The team works two sprints and nobody can demonstrate anything at the review.
- Delete it, because it is not a user story | The work is still needed, and its cost disappears from the plan.
+ Slice the work into the thinnest vertical story that uses the database, such as "As a customer, I see the status of my latest order", and let the database work happen inside that story | The work is still done, and every sprint ends with something a user can see.
= Prefer thin vertical slices. They give feedback early and expose risk while it is still cheap.
```

## Backlog hygiene

```timeline
Before sprint | Top items refined, small and clear | ok
During sprint | New ideas added at the bottom, not the top | ok
Review | Feedback becomes new items | info
Next sprint | Order changed by the Product Owner based on value | ok
```

```ref
Scrum | Scrum Guide, Product Backlog refinement | Refinement is ongoing, not a meeting you skip. Say who is involved and what makes an item ready to pull.
```


## AI and backlogs

```cards
file | AI drafts stories | Turn notes or requirements into a first set of stories and criteria. | Plausible but generic, and it invents users that do not exist.
search | AI checks INVEST | Ask it to flag stories that are too big or untestable. | It cannot know your team's capacity.
brain | AI features need stories too | Accuracy, human fallback, logging and monitoring each become stories. | The assistant is a single huge story.
```

```ai
Shift | For AI features, a backlog needs stories for the controls as well as the feature.
Example (illustrative) | The backlog has a story for an answering assistant and none for handing off to a person. The team adds one with acceptance criteria.
Use AI to | Draft stories, suggest INVEST improvements and generate acceptance criteria.
Keep human | Making each story small, testable and valuable.
Risk | A feature story with no story for human handoff, so users get stuck.
Control | Add stories such as as a customer I can reach a person when the assistant cannot help, with acceptance criteria.
Interview line | For an AI feature I write stories for the controls too, such as a route to a person, so users are not stuck.
```

```ref
OWASP | Top 10 for LLM Applications, overreliance and misinformation | AI can write a story that reads well and is wrong. The PO and team still check the intent.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
A story says who, what and why
Acceptance criteria give pass or fail
INVEST: independent, negotiable, valuable, estimable, small, testable
Slice vertically, not by technical layer
AI features need stories for controls, not only features
```
