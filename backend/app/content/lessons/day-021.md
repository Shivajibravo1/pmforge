## Everything on one page

Week six. Daniel has the charter, the WBS, the schedule and the budget in four separate documents. The steering committee meets next week. He wants one integrated plan.

```chat
# Message, Tuesday, 10:05 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Does the budget match the schedule? Does the schedule match the scope? I cannot tell from here.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | I found one problem already. The WBS has a data reconciliation task that the schedule does not.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | And the budget still uses my first estimate, not the three-point one.
```

A plan is integrated when changing one part visibly changes the others. If you change scope, the schedule and budget move. If you move the date, the cost and resources shift. If the three disagree, the plan is a collection of documents, not a plan.

```key
An integrated plan answers four questions with one consistent set of numbers: what, when, how much and who.
```

## What goes in the integrated plan

```cards
layers | Scope | Scope statement, WBS and out of scope list. | Deliverables with no owner or date.
clock | Schedule | Milestones, critical path and dependencies, with a baseline. | Dates that do not match the effort.
money | Cost | Cost baseline, contingency and running costs. | A budget built from stale estimates.
users | Resources and responsibility | RACI, team roles and key assumptions. | Capacity assumed, not checked.
alert | Risks and assumptions | The top risks with owners and the assumptions the plan depends on. | A plan that looks certain.
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, project management plan and baselines | One plan with scope, schedule and cost baselines plus subsidiary plans. Know the names.
```


## The cross check

```flow
WBS | Every work package appears in the schedule
Schedule | Every task has an estimate and an owner
Estimates | Match the cost baseline
Cost | Includes contingency tied to named risks
Risks | Link back to the tasks they could affect
```

## Your decision

Two weeks before the committee, you find that the schedule needs four developers, but the plan shows three.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Quietly stretch the schedule to match three developers | The date moves without anyone deciding, and the sponsor discovers the slip later.
- Show three developers and hope the team works harder | Hidden overtime, then burnout and defects.
+ State the gap, show two options, add a fourth developer for the critical weeks with the cost, or move the date, and let the sponsor choose | The plan is honest, the trade-off is visible, and the decision is made by the person who owns the budget and the date.
= An integrated plan exposes conflicts. That is its job. Surface them early with options.
```

## Assumptions are part of the plan

Every plan stands on assumptions. Write them down with an owner and a date to confirm, because an assumption that turns out false is a risk that has already happened.

```accordion
The vendor interface is ready by week 6 | Owner: vendor manager. Confirm in the week 4 review. If false, the critical path moves by two weeks.
Staff are released for user testing | Owner: Priya Nair. Confirm in the week 5 sync. If false, testing is delayed or thinner.
Order data quality is acceptable | Owner: Marcus Webb. Confirm after the first data profile. If false, data cleansing is added to scope.
Security gates are met first time | Owner: Elena Rossi. If false, remediation and retest add time.
```

Each line links an assumption to a plan consequence. When one fails, you know which dates and costs to revisit.

```ref
PMI | Assumption log | Assumptions are risks in waiting. Record owner and check date for each.
```

## AI integration risk

```cards
brain | AI assistant in the plan | Evaluation, human handover, monitoring and data preparation each appear as work packages. | Missing work that appears later as a surprise.
file | AI drafts the plan summary | A one page summary from the detail. | It can hide a conflict by smoothing wording.
```

```ai
When you present the plan, say how you would know the AI part is working: an agreed accuracy standard, who reviews answers and what happens when it is wrong.
```
