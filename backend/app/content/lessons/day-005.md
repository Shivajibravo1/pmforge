## Your build today: a project charter

A charter is the short document that authorises the project and states what everyone has agreed. It is your most useful early artifact because it forces the hard questions while they are still cheap.

## Scenario

Your company will migrate its public marketing website from an ageing on-premises server to a managed cloud platform. The site serves product information and a contact form. It must not lose search ranking, break existing links or lose form submissions during the move.

## What a good charter contains

1. **Purpose and business case.** Why this project exists, in two or three sentences.
2. **Objectives.** Measurable outcomes (for example, zero broken inbound links, page load time target, cutover within an agreed window).
3. **Scope.** What is in, and just as important, what is out.
4. **Deliverables.** The concrete things you will hand over.
5. **Key stakeholders and roles.** Sponsor, owner, delivery lead, security, operations.
6. **High-level timeline and milestones.**
7. **Budget summary and assumptions.**
8. **Top risks and constraints.**
9. **Success criteria and approval.** How the sponsor will judge completion.

## Tips

- Use numbers and dates where you can. "Fast" is not a requirement; "main pages load in under 3 seconds on mobile" is.
- Write assumptions explicitly. Hidden assumptions become late surprises.
- Keep it to two pages. If it is longer, nobody will read it.

## Your task

Write the charter for this scenario using the structure above. Invent realistic specifics (names of roles, dates, budget range) and state them as assumptions. Submit it for mentor review against the rubric.
