## One page that prevents six months of argument

Imagine starting a project with no written agreement on what it is. Three months in, the sponsor says "I thought it included the mobile app." You say "that was never in scope." Both of you are right, and the project is in trouble.

A project charter is the short document that stops this. It is the first thing you write, and it is worth getting right.

```key
A charter authorises the project and records what everyone agreed, while the hard questions are still cheap to ask.
```

## Your scenario

Your company will move its public marketing website from an ageing on-premises server to a managed cloud platform. The site shows product information and has a contact form.

```stats
0 | Lost form submissions
0 | Broken inbound links
3s | Mobile load target
1 | Agreed cutover window
```

```warn
The site must not lose its search ranking, break existing links or lose contact form submissions during the move.
```

## What goes in a charter

```cards
target | 1. Purpose and business case | Why this project exists, in two or three sentences.
flag | 2. Objectives | Measurable outcomes, such as zero broken inbound links.
layers | 3. Scope | What is in and, just as important, what is out.
file | 4. Deliverables | The concrete things you will hand over.
users | 5. Stakeholders and roles | Sponsor, owner, delivery lead, security, operations.
clock | 6. Timeline and milestones | High level, with dates.
money | 7. Budget and assumptions | A summary range and what you assumed.
alert | 8. Risks and constraints | The top few, each with an owner.
check | 9. Success criteria and approval | How the sponsor will judge that you are done.
```

## Write it so people actually read it

```tip
Use numbers and dates. "Fast" is not a requirement. "Main pages load in under 3 seconds on mobile" is. State assumptions out loud, because hidden assumptions become late surprises. Keep it to two pages.
```

```accordion
Weak: "The site should be fast." | Nobody can test it, so nobody can agree it is done.
Strong: "Main pages load in under 3 seconds on a mobile connection." | Measurable, so you can prove it.
Weak: "Scope: migrate the website." | Silent on what is excluded, so everything is arguable.
Strong: "In: all current pages and the contact form. Out: redesign, new features, blog migration." | The boundary is written down.
```

## Before you submit

```checklist
Business case in two or three sentences
Measurable objectives with numbers
Scope with an explicit out-of-scope list
Named sponsor and owner
Milestones with dates
Assumptions stated openly
Top risks with owners
Success criteria the sponsor can sign
```
