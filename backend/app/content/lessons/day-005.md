## Monday, the sponsor wants a page

Week two. Daniel Okafor stops you in the corridor. "Before the portal goes live, the public marketing site has to move off the old on-premises server. It is falling over. Can you write me a charter for that? One page. I will sign it Friday."

You have written project plans. You have never written the page that comes before the plan.

```chat
# Corridor, Monday, 8:40 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Just keep it simple. Move the website to the cloud. Done by quarter end.
Elena Rossi | CISO | If the contact form leaks anything during the move, I will stop it.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | And please do not break the links. We get hundreds of calls when a page disappears.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | Does "the website" include the blog? Last time that argument cost us a month.
```

Imagine starting with no written agreement. Three months in, the sponsor says "I thought it included the mobile app." You say "that was never in scope." Both of you are right, and the project is in trouble. The charter is the short document that stops this.

```key
A charter authorises the project and records what everyone agreed, while the hard questions are still cheap to ask.
```

## Your scenario

The company will move its public marketing website from an ageing on-premises server to a managed cloud platform. The site shows product information and has a contact form.

## What goes in a charter

```cards
target | 1. Purpose and business case | Why this project exists, in two or three sentences.
flag | 2. Objectives | Measurable outcomes, such as zero broken inbound links.
layers | 3. Scope | What is in and, just as important, what is out.
file | 4. Deliverables | The concrete things you will hand over.
users | 5. Stakeholders and roles | Sponsor, owner, delivery lead, security, operations.
clock | 6. Timeline and milestones | High level, with dates.
money | 7. Budget and assumptions | A summary range and what you assumed.
alert | 8. Risks and constraints | The top few, each with an owner.
check | 9. Success criteria and approval | How the sponsor will judge that you are done.
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, project charter | Know what a charter does: it names the project, authorizes it and gives the PM authority. Interview line: no charter, no mandate.
PRINCE2 | Project brief and project initiation documentation | If the client runs PRINCE2, translate your charter into a project brief. Showing you can map between methods is a plus.
```


## Why each voice in the corridor matters

Look at the four messages again. Each one is a charter section waiting to be written.

```flow
Marcus asks about the blog | Scope: in and out, in writing
Priya fears broken links | Objective: zero broken inbound links
Elena fears form leaks | Risk: contact form data, with an owner
Daniel says quarter end | Timeline: milestones with dates
```

## Write it so people actually read it

```tip
Use numbers and dates. "Fast" is not a requirement. "Main pages load in under 3 seconds on mobile" is. State assumptions out loud, because hidden assumptions become late surprises. Keep it to two pages.
```

```accordion
Weak: "The site should be fast." | Nobody can test it, so nobody can agree it is done.
Strong: "Main pages load in under 3 seconds on a mobile connection." | Measurable, so you can prove it.
Weak: "Scope: migrate the website." | Silent on what is excluded, so everything is arguable.
Strong: "In: all current pages and the contact form. Out: redesign, new features, blog migration." | The boundary is written down.
```

## Your decision

Friday is the signing meeting. Marcus's blog question is still open.

```decision
? Daniel is in a hurry and the blog is undecided. What do you put in the charter?
- Leave the blog out of the document and sort it out later | Everyone assumes their own answer. In month three the blog is "obviously in" for Daniel and "obviously out" for the team.
- Include the blog silently to be safe | Scope grows by a third with no budget change and your team learns about it from the sponsor.
+ List the blog under out of scope, with a note that a change request can add it, and flag it to Daniel before Friday | The boundary is written, the sponsor chooses with full information, and nobody is surprised in month three.
= A charter is where you make ambiguity visible. The cheapest time to disagree about scope is before anyone has signed.
```

## Let AI draft, you decide

Many project managers now ask an AI assistant to draft the first version of a charter. That is fine, if you use it the right way.

```cards
file | A first draft in minutes | Structure, headings and starter wording from a short description. | Generic text that fits any project, not yours.
search | Gap spotting | Ask it what assumptions or risks are missing from your draft. | It lists plausible risks, not the real ones in your company.
target | The numbers stay yours | Dates, budget and owners must come from you. | Invented figures end up in a document your sponsor signs.
```

```ai
Shift | AI can produce a polished charter in a minute. That makes it easier to hide weak thinking behind good prose.
Example (illustrative) | An AI draft of a charter lists a go live date and a budget the PM never provided. The PM removes both and replaces them with agreed figures.
Use AI to | Draft sections, challenge assumptions, find missing items such as out of scope and tighten the wording.
Keep human | Every number, date, name and commitment. The sponsor signs a promise that you must be able to defend.
Risk | Invented figures or stakeholders, and confidential details in a public tool.
Control | Mark each AI suggestion, check it against a real source, and keep a short assumptions list the sponsor can read.
Interview line | AI gives me the first draft and I own the facts.
```

```ref
NIST | AI RMF Govern function: accountability for AI use | Draft with AI, sign as the owner. Say that you remain accountable for every claim in a charter.
OWASP | Top 10 for LLM Applications, overreliance and misinformation risks | Cite it when explaining why a human reviews every AI draft before it goes to a sponsor.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Helps define the AI parts of scope, data and success measures in the charter. | Charter lists AI as a feature with no target.
shield | AI security consultant | Reviews data and access assumptions before approval. | Asked to review after sign off.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Confirms what is possible in the customer's environment. | Promises made on site that exceed the charter.

```

```incident
# Charter approved with AI scope left vague (illustrative)
What happened | A charter for a website migration added the line add an AI chat feature. There were no data, security or accuracy details.
Impact | Scope expanded mid project and the launch date came under pressure.
Root cause | The charter allowed a feature with unknown cost, data and risk to enter scope without a defined target or owner.
Resolution | The AI feature was split into a separate, phased initiative with its own charter, owner and measures.
PM move | Challenge vague AI lines in a charter and separate them from committed scope.
```

## Before you submit

```checklist
Business case in two or three sentences
Measurable objectives with numbers
Scope with an explicit out-of-scope list
Named sponsor and owner
Milestones with dates
Assumptions stated openly
Top risks with owners
Success criteria the sponsor can sign
```

```answer
? What goes in a project charter, and why does it matter?
- A detailed plan with every task | That is a plan, not a charter. A charter is short and gives authority.
- Whatever the sponsor wants to see | A charter must state limits and trade offs, not only ambitions.
+ A charter gives the project its purpose and authority: the problem, objectives, scope and what is out of scope, success measures, key stakeholders, high level risks and assumptions, budget and timeline range, and the named sponsor and project manager. It gets the sponsor to agree the limits before work starts. For AI features I add the data, owner and measure that define enough.
= Mention the out of scope list. It shows discipline.
= Keep it to one page when you describe your own.
= Say who signs it and why that matters.
```

```mindmap
# Project charter
Purpose | Problem, objectives, success measures
Boundaries | In scope, out of scope, assumptions
People | Sponsor, project manager, key stakeholders
Constraints | Budget range, timeline, risks, dependencies
```

```flashcards
What a charter does | Gives the project its purpose, boundaries and authority.
Why list out of scope | It stops silent scope growth and sets expectations.
Who signs it | The sponsor, who commits to support and resources.
AI line in a charter | Needs data, owner and measure, or it belongs outside committed scope.
```
