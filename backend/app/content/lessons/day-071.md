## "Walk me through the pipeline"

Victoria has asked for a release plan for the assistant launch. Elena wants to see the pipeline and the controls. Daniel wants to be able to explain it to the board in five minutes. You have to deliver both a plan and a walkthrough.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 9:00 AM
Elena Rossi | CISO | Show me the pipeline, stage by stage, with the security checks and who approves what.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | And I want one page I can use at the board. Dates, risks, what could go wrong.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | I will walk you through Azure DevOps. Bring your questions.
```

A release plan is a short document that tells everyone when, how and by whom a change reaches users, and what happens if it does not go well. A pipeline walkthrough is the evidence that the plan is real: you can show the stages, the gates and the logs. Together they turn trust into verification.

```key
A release plan answers who, what, when, how, how to check and how to go back. A pipeline walkthrough proves that the controls exist and work.
```

## What goes in the release plan

```cards
list | Scope | What is in the release, what is not and the linked work items. | Unclear contents, surprise features.
calendar | Timing | Dates, windows and freezes, with time zones. | Clashes with other releases or peak business periods.
workflow | Method | Strategy, steps and owners for each. | Steps that live in one person's head.
check | Go and no go criteria | Evidence needed to proceed, and signals that stop it. | Decisions made by pressure.
undo | Rollback | Triggers, steps, decision owner and time limit. | A vague plan to fix forward.
chat | Communication | Who is told what, and when. | Support learning about the release from customers.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 release management and deployment management practices | Release plan covers what, when, who approves and how to roll back.
```


## The walkthrough

```flow
Repository | Show branch policies, reviews and linked work items
Build | Show the build, tests and results history
Scans | Show code, dependency and secret scans and the blocking rules
Environments | Show test and production, with approvals and checks
Release | Show the deployment strategy and the rollback step
Evidence | Show logs, approvals and traceability from requirement to release
```

## Your decision

During the walkthrough, Elena finds that security scans run but do not block a release when they find high severity issues.

```decision
? How do you respond?
- Explain that scans are advisory and the team reviews the results | You have no enforcement, and she will notice.
- Switch off the scans to avoid blocking the release | You remove a control to meet a date.
+ Agree to make high and critical findings blocking, with a documented exception route owned by the CISO, and fix the current findings or agree dated exceptions before launch | The control becomes real, and the release has a path forward.
= A control that does not block is a report. Make it enforceable and define the exception route.
```

## Release plan on a page

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Release | Assistant version one for staff and a small share of customers |
| Window | Friday afternoon for staff, Monday for the wider group |
| Owner | Release manager named, with a deputy |
| Go criteria | Tests pass, scans clean or excepted, support ready |
| Stop criteria | Error or wrong answer rate above the agreed limit |
| Rollback | Feature flag off, previous version ready, decision within thirty minutes |
| Communication | Staff note, customer message, support brief |
