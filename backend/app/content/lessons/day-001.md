## Tuesday, 9:47 AM

You are the project manager on Project Lighthouse. It is a fictional retailer's push to launch a customer portal with an AI assistant that answers order questions. The launch date is nine weeks away. You have been in the role for eleven days.

At 9:47 AM your phone shows four messages at once. This is the job.

```chat
# Team chat, Tuesday, 9:47 AM
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | The new login is rejecting about one in ten staff. Can you find out why before the 11:00 call?
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | We cannot start user testing until the test environment has realistic data in it.
Elena Rossi | CISO | I still have not seen the penetration test report. No report, no go-live approval.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Finance wants a one page spend update by end of day. Are we still on budget?
```

You have not opened your project plan yet. It is 10:14 AM. Four people want four different things and every one of them believes theirs is the urgent one.

```note
Project Lighthouse and everyone in it are fictional. The situations are patterns that happen on real projects. You will follow this one project for the next ten days.
```

## The one sentence that defines your job

Strip away the noise and the job fits in one sentence.

```key
You turn a business need into a working, supported technology change, on an agreed date and budget, without breaking what already runs.
```

Picture a bridge being built while traffic keeps crossing it. The business needs the new bridge. Engineers build it. Operations will maintain it. Security checks it is safe. You make sure people, money and calendar line up, and that nobody drives into a gap.

```flow
Business need | Daniel needs customers to track orders without calling the contact centre
Plan | Scope, budget, date and risks agreed in writing
Build | Developers, testers and the vendor deliver the portal
Prove | Testing, security review and a working demo
Run | Operations supports it live and you hand over
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, definition of a project and the Talent Triangle (ways of working, power skills, business acumen) | Interview line: a project is temporary and creates a unique result, and an IT PM is judged on delivery skill, people skill and business sense together.
```


## Your first decision

It is 10:20. You can only do one thing in the next forty minutes. Priya's login problem hits staff right now. Elena's report blocks go-live. Marcus is waiting. Daniel wants numbers by tonight.

```decision
? You have 40 minutes before the 11:00 call. What do you do first?
- Reply to Daniel with a spend update, since the sponsor is the most senior | Daniel gets his numbers, but staff are still locked out, and the 11:00 call opens with Priya asking why nobody looked at her issue.
- Open the plan and re-sequence everything before replying to anyone | You spend 40 minutes on a plan nobody is waiting for. Four people feel ignored and the real login fault is still unknown.
+ Triage: ask Priya for two failing examples, get a named owner for each of the other three, and tell all four when they will hear back | Priya's engineer gets a clear lead on the login fault, Marcus and Elena get dated answers, and Daniel gets a time for his update. Nobody is ignored and nothing is guessed.
= Senior PMs triage before they act. You rarely fix the fault yourself. You make sure the right person owns each problem, with a time attached, and every requester knows what happens next.
```

## Why IT projects behave differently

In a building project you can walk the site and see progress. In IT the work is invisible: code, settings, data, connections between systems, access rights. You cannot judge progress by looking. You judge it by evidence.

```timeline
Week 1 | Portal team reports the login screen as done | ok
Week 2 | A staff member cannot log in. The team says it works on their laptop | warn
Week 3 | The fault is traced to single sign-on settings that differ between test and live | bad
Week 3 | Marcus asks what else differs between test and live. Nobody knows | info
```

That is what "done" without evidence looks like. Three reasons IT work surprises people:

```cards
server | Production is shared | Your change goes into a live system other teams rely on. | A careless change takes down other teams' systems and your name is on the incident.
link | Dependencies are hidden | An easy looking change often touches login, networking, a database, a vendor system and a security review. | A late surprise blocks go-live and the calendar slips.
eye | Done is not obvious | A feature can look finished and still fail under load, leak data or be impossible to support. | You report green while the system fails under real use.
```

```tip
When someone says "it's done", ask: done where, and what proves it? That one question will save you more often than any template.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 four dimensions: organizations and people, information and technology, partners and suppliers, value streams and processes | Use the four dimensions as a checklist when a plan covers only the technology. Good answer to the question of what you look at beyond the build.
```


## Who is on your project

Every IT project is a negotiation between people who want different things. Learn what each one cares about and you can predict where the friction will come from.

| Person on Lighthouse | Role | What they care about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Outcome, cost, date |
| Elena Rossi | CISO | Data protection, compliance, vulnerabilities |
| Priya Nair | Service desk lead | Stability, supportability, fewer tickets |
| Marcus Webb | Business analyst | Clear requirements, usable test data |
| The AI vendor | Supplier | Scope clarity, change requests, payment |

Notice that Elena and Daniel will clash. Daniel wants the date held. Elena wants nothing live until it is safe. Neither is wrong. Your job is to put the trade-off in front of both of them early, with facts.

## Project health at a glance

By Friday you owe Daniel a status. Senior PMs use a simple colour code so a busy executive can read it in ten seconds. Here is Lighthouse after your first triage.

```rag
Scope | G | Agreed in the project charter
Schedule | A | Test environment late by about a week
Budget | G | Spend in line with plan so far
Security | R | Penetration test report not yet received
```

Red, amber and green only work if you give the reason beside each colour. A red with no plan is a panic. A red with an owner and a date is management.

## AI in your Tuesday

It is 10:30. You paste your stand-up notes into an AI assistant and a clean status report comes back in seconds. That is the new normal for project managers, and it brings new ways to get hurt.

```cards
spark | AI drafts your status report | It summarises notes, lists decisions and open actions in your format. | It can state something confidently that nobody actually said. You sign the report, so you check it.
search | AI explains the unknown | Ask it to explain an error message or a term from the meeting in plain words. | A plausible but wrong explanation. Confirm with the engineer before you repeat it.
lock | AI and confidential data | Meeting notes often hold client names and security details. | Pasting them into a public tool leaks them. Use only tools your organisation has approved.
```

```ai
Interview line: "AI makes me faster at drafting. I stay accountable for the evidence, so I verify before I send." The same habit works at home: check an AI's answer before acting on it, whether it is a status report or a travel tip.
```

```ref
NIST | AI Risk Management Framework, four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage | Name the four functions when asked how you would bring AI into a project safely. It shows you treat AI as a governed change, not a tool.
```

```cards
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Embedded with the customer to deploy and tune the product. You translate their findings into plan changes. | Fixes made on site that never reach the plan.
cpu | AI engineer | Builds retrieval, prompts and evaluations. Needs a clear outcome and a measurable target from you. | Asked to build an assistant with no success measure.
shield | AI security consultant | Reviews data flows and abuse cases. Needs to be in the room before the design is fixed. | Brought in the week before launch.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Builds the tools, guardrails and logging around an agent. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Nobody owns what the agent may do.
code | Traditional developers | Build the integrations and interfaces the AI depends on. | Told about AI changes last.
```

```incident
# An AI assistant was promised without an owner (illustrative)
What happened | A sponsor asked for an AI assistant by quarter end. Two teams started building in parallel because nobody had defined the outcome or named a single accountable owner.
Impact | Two prototypes, overlapping spend and a launch date nobody could defend.
Root cause | The project manager started work before writing the outcome, the owner and the success measure in one sentence.
Resolution | The sponsor named one owner, the two builds were merged, and the outcome was written as a measurable target before work resumed.
PM move | Write the one sentence that defines the project, and get the sponsor to confirm it before any build starts.
```

## Check your understanding

```quiz
? Marcus says "the demo looked perfect" but nobody said which environment it ran in. What do you ask first?
- Can we add more features before launch?
+ Is this a test copy or production, and what has not been tested yet?
- Who designed the screens?
= In IT you manage through evidence. The environment and the untested gaps tell you how much the demo really proves.
```

```answer
? What does an enterprise IT project manager actually do?
- I manage the schedule and chase tasks | Describes a coordinator. It ignores outcomes, risk and decisions.
- I manage the developers | A PM usually does not manage people technically. It misses the stakeholders and the business.
+ I turn a business need into delivered outcomes within time, cost and risk limits. That means agreeing the outcome with the sponsor, bringing the right stakeholders in early, making risks and decisions visible, and keeping a mixed team of developers, AI engineers, security and operations pointed at the same goal. I keep the plan honest so bad news arrives early.
= Lead with outcomes, then risk, then people.
= Add one real example of a decision you made, using your own project.
= For AI projects, mention that you coordinate AI engineers, security and customer facing engineers as well as developers.
```
