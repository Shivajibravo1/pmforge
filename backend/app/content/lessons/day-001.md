## A real Tuesday

You are an IT project manager at a mid-sized company. It is 9:47 AM on a Tuesday. Here is what has landed in your inbox and chat in the last hour:

```quote
The new login system is rejecting about one in ten staff. Can you find out why before the 11:00 call? | Service desk lead
We cannot start the user testing until the test environment has real data in it. | Business analyst
Security needs the penetration test report before we can approve the go-live. | CISO's office
Finance wants a one-page update on spend by end of day. | Your sponsor
```

It is 10:14 AM and you have not opened your project plan yet. Welcome to the job.

```note
This scenario is illustrative. It shows the pattern, not a real company.
```

## What an IT PM actually does

Strip away the noise and the job is one sentence:

```key
You turn a business need into a working, supported technology change, on an agreed date and budget, without breaking what already runs.
```

Think of yourself as the person who builds a bridge while traffic keeps crossing it. The business needs the bridge. Engineers build it. Operations will maintain it. Security checks it is safe. You make sure all of them, and the money, and the calendar, line up.

```flow
Business need | Someone needs an outcome
Plan | Scope, budget, date, risks
Build | The team delivers the change
Prove | Test, demo, security review
Run | Operations supports it live
```

## Why IT projects feel different

In a building project you can walk the site and see progress. In IT the work is invisible: code, settings, data, connections between systems, access rights. So you cannot judge progress by looking. You judge it by evidence: a working demo, passed tests, a clean environment.

```cards
server | Production is shared | Your change goes into a live system other teams rely on. A good plan protects them. | A careless change takes down other teams' systems.
link | Dependencies are hidden | An easy change often touches login, networking, a database, a vendor system and a security review. | A late surprise blocks go-live.
eye | Done is not obvious | A feature can look finished and still fail under load, leak data or be impossible to support. | You report green while the system fails under real use.
```

```tip
When someone says "it's done", ask: done where, and what proves it? That one question will save you more often than any template.
```

## AI in your Tuesday

It is 10:30. You paste your stand-up notes into an AI assistant and a clean status report comes back in seconds. That is the new normal for project managers, and it comes with new ways to get hurt.

```cards
spark | AI drafts your status report | Summarises notes, lists decisions and open actions in your format. | It can state something confidently that nobody actually said. You sign the report, so you check it.
search | AI explains the unknown | Ask it to explain an error message or a term from the meeting in plain words. | A plausible but wrong explanation. Confirm with the engineer before you repeat it.
lock | AI and confidential data | Meeting notes often hold client names and security details. | Pasting them into a public tool leaks them. Use only tools your organisation has approved.
```

```ai
Interview line: "AI makes me faster at drafting. I stay accountable for the evidence, so I verify before I send." The same habit works at home: check an AI's answer before acting on it, whether it is a status report or a travel tip.
```

## Who you work with

Every IT project is a negotiation between people who want different things. Learn what each one cares about and you can predict where the friction will come from.

| Role | What they care about |
| --- | --- |
| Sponsor / business owner | Outcome, cost, date |
| CIO / IT head | Fit with architecture, run cost, risk |
| CISO / security | Data protection, compliance, vulnerabilities |
| Operations / support | Stability, monitoring, handover |
| Vendors | Scope clarity, change requests, payment |
| Delivery team | Clear priorities, fewer interruptions |

## Check your understanding

```quiz
? A demo looks perfect, but nobody has said which environment it ran in. What do you ask first?
- Can we add more features before launch?
+ Is this a test copy or production, and what has not been tested yet?
- Who designed the screens?
= In IT you manage through evidence. The environment and the untested gaps tell you how much the demo really proves.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
My job: business need to working, supported change, without breaking what runs
Progress in IT is proven by evidence, not by looking
Production is shared, dependencies are hidden, done is not obvious
Every stakeholder cares about something different
I can name the sponsor, the system owner and the riskiest dependency
```
