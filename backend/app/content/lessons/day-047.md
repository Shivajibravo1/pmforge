## The vendor wants to know how you will pay

The assistant is built on a vendor's model and platform. The procurement team has a draft contract on the table and asks you which pricing structure to choose.

```chat
# Procurement meeting, Wednesday, 2:00 PM
Procurement | Procurement | The vendor offers a fixed price or a time and materials rate. Which do we choose?
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | The scope will change. We do not yet know how accurate the assistant will be.
Finance partner | Finance | I need a number I can budget. I cannot approve an open ended spend.
Elena Rossi | CISO | Whatever you choose, security terms must be in the contract.
```

The contract is the project's risk allocation. A fixed price transfers cost risk to the vendor and rewards a precise scope. Time and materials share cost risk with the buyer and suit uncertain work. Neither is better in general. The right choice depends on how well you can describe what you want.

```key
Choose the contract type by how stable the scope is. The less you know, the more you need flexibility, and the more you need controls to protect the budget.
```

## Two common structures

| Feature | Fixed price | Time and materials |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who bears cost risk | Mostly the vendor | Mostly the buyer |
| Best when | Scope is clear and stable | Scope is uncertain or evolving |
| Vendor incentive | Finish with minimum effort, resist change | Spend more time, unless capped |
| Typical control | Change requests, acceptance criteria | Caps, milestones, regular reviews |
| Common trap | Disputes over what was included | Costs drift upward |

Other structures exist, such as a capped time and materials, or outcome based pricing. Both of the above can be adapted with caps, milestones and incentives.

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, contract types: fixed price, cost reimbursable, time and material | Interview line: fixed price shifts cost risk to the vendor, time and material shifts it to the buyer.
```


## Service level agreements

An SLA, or service level agreement, states the level of service the vendor promises, how it is measured and what happens when it is missed.

```cards
clock | Availability | The percentage of time the service is up, measured over a period. | A number that sounds high but allows long outages.
zap | Response and resolution times | How fast the vendor responds and fixes, by severity. | A response time that is not a fix time.
money | Service credits | A reduction in fees when targets are missed. | A credit smaller than the damage.
eye | Reporting | How and how often performance is reported. | Self reported figures nobody checks.
```

Illustrative: 99.9 percent availability in a 30 day month allows about 43 minutes of downtime, because 30 days x 24 hours x 60 minutes = 43,200 minutes, and 0.1 percent of that is 43.2 minutes. By contrast 99 percent allows about 432 minutes, or just over seven hours.

```stats
99.9% | Availability target
43 | Minutes of downtime per month
99% | A lower target
432 | Minutes of downtime per month
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 service level management practice | SLAs need measurable targets, measurement method and review rhythm.
ISO | ISO/IEC 20000-1 service management system | If the vendor claims it, ask for scope and certificate details.
```


## Your decision

The vendor offers a fixed price for the assistant. You worry that the behaviour of the model is not understood well enough to fix the scope.

```decision
? What do you propose?
- Accept the fixed price, since Finance prefers certainty | When tuning takes longer than expected, change requests multiply and the relationship sours.
- Choose time and materials with no cap | The budget is open ended, and Finance is unhappy.
+ Propose a phased deal: a fixed price for a defined discovery and evaluation phase, then a capped time and materials or a fixed price for the build, with acceptance criteria based on measured accuracy | Uncertainty is reduced before the large commitment, and Finance gets a ceiling.
= Match the contract to what you know. Split uncertain work into phases and let evidence decide the next commitment.
```

## Clauses a PM should look for

```accordion
Acceptance criteria | Payments linked to agreed, testable criteria, including accuracy thresholds where relevant.
Change control | A clear process and price list for changes.
Data protection | Where data is processed, what the vendor may do with it, and breach notification terms.
Exit and transition | How you leave, how data is returned and how knowledge transfers.
Liability and service credits | Limits and remedies, reviewed by legal.
```

You are not a lawyer. Raise these with legal and procurement, and do not sign advice that only they can give.

## AI vendor specifics

```cards
lock | Data use | Whether your prompts or data may be used to train the vendor's models. | Confidential data leaves your control.
brain | Model changes | Whether the vendor may change the model without notice. | Behaviour drifts under a fixed contract.
check | Evaluation rights | Your right to test and audit the service. | You cannot verify the claims.
```

```incident
# Vendor model update changes behaviour mid contract (illustrative)
What happened | The vendor updated its hosted model without notice. The assistant began refusing valid requests and its answers changed in tone.
Impact | Service desk tickets rose and the sponsor questioned the vendor relationship.
Root cause | The contract had no model change notice clause, and acceptance had been based on a demo, not a measured test set.
Resolution | The vendor restored the earlier version for an agreed period, the team re ran its evaluation set, and the contract was amended with change notice and evaluation rights.
PM move | Link payment to measured accuracy on your own test set, and add model change notice to every AI vendor contract.
```

```checklist

```
