## Your RAID log, live

Week eight of Lighthouse. Daniel asks you to share the log with the steering committee. He wants to see that risks are being managed, not just recorded.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 9:00 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Bring the RAID log on Thursday. I want to see owners, dates and what you are doing about the top five.
Elena Rossi | CISO | Please include the data flow to the AI vendor. It is the one I worry about.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | And please make it readable. The last one I saw had 80 rows and nothing I could act on.
```

A log with 80 rows nobody reads is worse than a log with 15 rows reviewed every week. This is the artifact that proves you can run risk management as a habit, using the structure from Day 22.

```key
A good RAID log is short, current and owned. Every entry has an owner, a response and a date, and the top five are reviewed every week.
```

## What a good log contains

```cards
alert | Risks | Event, cause, impact, probability, impact score, owner, response, review date. | Risks with no owner or date.
check | Assumptions | The assumption, who confirms it, by when and what changes if false. | An assumption carried for months unchecked.
zap | Issues | What happened, impact, owner, action and due date. | Issues left to grow.
link | Dependencies | What you need, from whom, by when and the fallback. | Hope used as a plan.
```

```ref
PMI | Risk register fields: cause, event, effect, probability, impact, owner, response | Use these fields in your capstone log.
ISO | ISO 31000 | Add a review frequency and risk appetite note if the sponsor wants a formal tone.
```


## A risk written clearly

Weak and strong versions of the same entry.

```accordion
Weak: "Vendor may be late." | There is no cause, impact, owner or date, so nobody acts.
Strong: "If the vendor interface is not available by week 6, order tracking cannot be tested, delaying go-live by about two weeks. Owner: vendor manager. Response: weekly checkpoint, escalate to account director at week 5." | Cause, effect, owner, response and review point are all clear.
Weak: "Security issues." | Unclear and unfalsifiable.
Strong: "If customer data sent to the AI vendor contains personal data not covered by contract, privacy approval may be refused, blocking launch. Owner: CISO. Response: agree data flow and redaction rules by week 8." | Specific, owned and dated.
```

## Your decision

Your log has 40 risks. Daniel's committee will not read them all.

```decision
? How do you present it?
- Send all 40 and let the committee choose | Nothing stands out, so the dangerous risks get no attention.
- Show only the top three so it looks controlled | The committee will not see what else is pending, and a hidden risk surprises everyone later.
+ Show the top five with owners, responses and dates on one page, include counts for the rest by rating, and keep the full log available | The committee focuses on what matters and can see the log is complete.
= Prioritise, summarise and keep the detail one click away. The goal is attention on the risks that matter.
```

## Review habit

```flow
Weekly | PM reviews top risks with owners
Fortnightly | Team reviews all open items
Monthly | Steering sees top five and trend
At each gate | Re-score everything
```
