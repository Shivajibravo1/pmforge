## "Does the European law apply to us?"

Lighthouse sells to a customer in Germany. Their legal team has sent a short letter: they will ask for evidence about how the support assistant meets the EU Artificial Intelligence Act. Legal counsel says "It depends". The sponsor says "Depends on what?".

```chat
# Legal review, Thursday, 11:30 AM
Legal counsel | Legal | The Act can apply to organisations outside the EU if their AI system is used in the EU.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Our assistant answers delivery questions. Is it high risk?
Legal counsel | Legal | Probably not, but the classification depends on the use. And there are transparency duties in any case.
```

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, is the first broad legal framework for AI. It applies a risk based approach: the higher the risk of an AI use, the stricter the duties. It entered into force in 2024, with obligations applying in phases over the following years. Dates and guidance continue to develop, so always check the official EU sources and take legal advice.

```key
The Act regulates uses of AI by risk level. The first job for a project manager is to classify each AI system accurately, with legal advice, and to plan the duties that follow.
```

## Risk tiers

```cards
x | Unacceptable risk | Practices that are prohibited, such as certain manipulative techniques or social scoring. | Building something that is banned.
alert | High risk | AI in listed areas, for example some uses in employment, education, essential services, law enforcement or safety components. Strict duties apply. | Missing that a use falls into the list.
eye | Limited risk | Mainly transparency duties, such as telling people they are talking to an AI. | Hiding that users are dealing with a machine.
check | Minimal risk | Most uses, with no specific duties under the Act. | Assuming minimal without checking.
```

General purpose AI models also have specific obligations, which mainly fall on the providers of those models. Organisations that use them still need to understand their own duties as deployers.

## Roles

```accordion
Provider | Develops an AI system or model, or has one developed, and places it on the market under its own name.
Deployer | Uses an AI system under its authority in a professional capacity.
Importer and distributor | Bring systems into the EU market or make them available.
Your project | You may be a provider, a deployer or both. Check with legal.
```

## Typical duties for high risk systems

```cards
list | Risk management | A continuous process across the lifecycle. | A one time assessment.
database | Data governance | Relevant, representative and as error free as possible data. | Unexamined training data.
file | Technical documentation | Records that show how the system works and was assessed. | Documents written after the fact.
users | Human oversight | Measures so people can oversee and intervene. | Oversight in name only.
chart | Accuracy, robustness and security | Testing and appropriate levels. | Untested claims.
```

## A path for a PM

```flow
Inventory | List AI systems and intended uses
Classify | Decide the risk tier with legal, and record reasons
Role | Decide if you are provider, deployer or both
Gap | Compare duties with what you have
Plan | Add work items, owners and dates
Monitor | Track guidance, dates and changes in the system
```

## Your decision

A product manager wants to add a feature that uses the assistant to screen job applicants for the company's own hiring.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Add it, since it is an internal tool | Employment is a listed high risk area, and internal does not exempt it.
- Ban all use of AI in HR | An overreaction, without understanding the options.
+ Pause the feature, ask legal to classify the use, treat it as potentially high risk in planning, identify duties such as risk management, human oversight and documentation, and decide whether to proceed after the assessment | Early classification protects people and avoids rework.
= Classify before you build. Some uses carry heavy duties.
```

## Transparency basics

```accordion
Tell people | Users should know when they interact with an AI system, unless it is obvious.
Label content | Some AI generated content needs to be identified.
Provide a route | Allow people to reach a human for important matters.
Keep records | Log enough to investigate issues and respond to authorities.
```

Check the Act's current text and guidance for exact duties.

**Timing**

Obligations apply on different dates, and the timeline has been the subject of discussion and proposed changes. Do not rely on any date in this lesson. Take current dates from the official EU Official Journal and your legal advisers.
