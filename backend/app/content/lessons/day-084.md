## Same fault, third time

For the third time this quarter, the Lighthouse portal slows to a crawl at noon on Mondays. Each time, someone restarts a service, the problem disappears and the incident is closed. Nobody finds out why it happens.

```chat
# Operations meeting, Tuesday, 9:30 AM
Operations lead | Operations | Another Monday slowdown. We restarted the service. Closed in twenty minutes.
Victoria Hale | COO | That is the third one. Is somebody fixing the cause?
Operations lead | Operations | We closed the incident. The cause is not our process.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Nobody has asked us to look.
```

Three practices are often confused: incident management, problem management and change enablement. Each answers a different question, and each has a different owner and a different measure of success. Understanding the difference helps you ask for the right thing at the right time.

```key
Incident management restores service. Problem management removes the cause. Change enablement makes sure fixes and improvements are made safely.
```

## Three practices

```cards
alert | Incident | An unplanned interruption or reduction in quality. Goal: restore service as fast as possible. | Closing the ticket without learning why.
search | Problem | The cause of one or more incidents. Goal: find root cause, and remove or reduce it. | A problem with no owner and no deadline.
settings | Change | Adding, modifying or removing something that could affect services. Goal: maximise successful changes with proper risk assessment. | A bureaucratic process that slows low risk changes.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 incident, problem and change enablement practices | Incident restores service, problem finds the cause, change applies the fix. State the roles of each.
```


## How they connect

```flow
Incident | Service degrades, users report or monitoring alerts
Workaround | Restore service, record what worked
Problem | Investigate root cause across incidents
Fix | Create a change to remove the cause
Change | Assess, approve, implement and review
Learn | Update knowledge and prevent recurrence
```

## Priority

```accordion
Impact | How many users or how much of the business is affected.
Urgency | How quickly it must be fixed.
Priority | Impact and urgency together decide the order of work and target times.
Severity levels | Organisations define levels, for example P1 to P4, with response and resolution targets.
```

```ref
ITIL | Priority as impact and urgency | Know the matrix. It is a classic ITIL question.
```


## Types of change

```cards
check | Standard | Pre approved, low risk and repeatable. | Treating routine tasks as risky.
settings | Normal | Assessed and approved by the right authority. | Approvals by people with no context.
alert | Emergency | Needed urgently to restore service or prevent serious harm. | Overusing the emergency route.
```

## Your decision

The Monday slowdown has caused three incidents in three weeks. The operations lead proposes to keep closing them as incidents.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Keep closing each incident | The cost of recurring outages keeps growing and no one learns.
- Ask developers to drop everything and find it today | You disrupt delivery without a plan or data.
+ Open a problem record linked to the three incidents with a named owner, gather data from monitoring for the next Monday, schedule a root cause review, and agree a date for a fix or permanent workaround through change enablement | The cause is investigated systematically, with owner and timing.
= Repeated incidents are a signal. Open a problem, give it an owner and a date.
```

## Root cause techniques

```accordion
Five whys | Ask why repeatedly until you reach a cause you can act on.
Timeline analysis | Line up events, changes and metrics to find correlations.
Fishbone | Group causes into categories such as people, process, technology and environment.
Change review | Check what changed before the incident began.
```

```ref
PMI | Quality tools: five whys and cause and effect diagram | Use them for root cause analysis. A known error record keeps workarounds visible.
```

```incident
# Same wrong answer, third time (illustrative)
What happened | An AI assistant gave the same wrong policy answer in three incidents. Each was closed by a prompt tweak.
Impact | Customers kept receiving wrong guidance and support effort grew.
Root cause | No problem record existed and the cause was an outdated source document.
Resolution | The PM opened a problem record, fixed the source, added a regression test for the answer and tracked the change.
PM move | Open a problem record when an AI fault repeats, and add a regression test.
```

```answer
? Explain incident, problem and change management with an example.
- They are the same process | Each has a different purpose.
- Problems are big incidents | A problem is the cause of one or more incidents.
+ Incident management restores service quickly. Problem management finds and removes the underlying cause. Change management controls how fixes and other changes are made safely. If the same fault returns, I open a problem record, find the root cause, raise a change for the fix and verify. For AI I add regression tests for repeated wrong answers.
= Restore, find cause, control change.
= Problem record for repeats.
= Regression test.
```
