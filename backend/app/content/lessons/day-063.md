## The foundation nobody sees

Three teams on Lighthouse have created cloud accounts on their own credit cards. Each has different naming, different network ranges and different security settings. The cloud bill arrives in four separate emails. Elena finds two accounts with no logging at all.

```chat
# Message, Wednesday, 9:20 AM
Elena Rossi | CISO | I found accounts nobody told me about. One has no logging and a wide open network rule.
Cloud engineer | Platform team | We needed to move fast and there was no standard setup.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Why did we let this happen?
```

A landing zone is a prepared, secure, governed cloud environment that new workloads can be placed into. It sets up the accounts, network, identity, logging, policies and cost controls once, so every team inherits them. Without it, every team builds its own foundation, and the organisation pays for it later.

```key
A landing zone is the platform on which workloads run. Build guardrails once, so teams can move fast safely instead of slowly by permission.
```

## What a landing zone contains

```cards
layers | Account structure | A hierarchy of subscriptions or accounts for production, test and shared services. | Everything in one account with shared admin rights.
network | Network design | Hub and spoke networks, segmentation and controlled internet access. | Flat networks with open rules.
key | Identity and access | Central identity, least privilege and multi factor authentication. | Shared admin accounts.
eye | Logging and monitoring | Central logs and alerts for all accounts. | Gaps nobody sees.
shield | Policies | Rules that block risky settings, such as public storage. | Rules written in documents but not enforced.
coins | Cost controls | Tags, budgets and alerts. | Surprise bills.
```

## The Well-Architected idea

Major providers publish frameworks for good design, often described through pillars such as reliability, security, cost optimisation, operational excellence and performance efficiency. Azure's Well-Architected Framework and the other providers' equivalents use similar themes. Check the current list of pillars on the provider's documentation, because it is updated.

```accordion
Reliability | Can the workload recover and keep working when parts fail.
Security | Is data protected, access limited and activity monitored.
Cost optimisation | Are you paying for what you need and no more.
Operational excellence | Can you deploy, observe and improve with confidence.
Performance efficiency | Does it use resources well as demand changes.
```

## Build order

```flow
Design | Agree account structure, naming and network
Secure | Identity, policies, logging, encryption
Automate | Build the landing zone as code
Onboard | Give teams a standard way to request an environment
Review | Check compliance and improve regularly
```

## Your decision

A product team asks to skip the landing zone, because they have a deadline in three weeks and the platform team's queue is long.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Let them go ahead, since the deadline is real | You create a fourth unmanaged account and a future clean up project.
- Refuse and make them wait | The deadline is missed, and teams learn to work around the platform.
+ Provide a minimum viable landing zone path for them within the week with mandatory policies and logging, and agree what they will add later | Speed and governance are both served, and the platform team prioritises by demand.
= A platform that says yes safely beats a platform that says no.
```

## Questions a PM can ask in a review

```checklist
Is there a standard way to create a new environment
Are policies enforced by the platform, not only documented
Are all accounts logged centrally
Are costs tagged by project and owner
Is the landing zone defined as code and version controlled
Who owns the platform, and what is the request process
```

## AI workloads on the platform

```cards
brain | Separate AI environment | A segmented space for AI workloads, with data access rules. | Experiments with production data in an unmanaged account.
key | Model access control | Who can call a model and with which keys. | One shared key for all teams.
eye | Prompt and response logging | Logs help investigation and must be protected. | Logs full of personal data nobody reviews.
```

```ai
Include AI workloads in the landing zone: approved regions and models, secrets in a vault, network limits for outbound calls, and logging with retention and access rules.
```
