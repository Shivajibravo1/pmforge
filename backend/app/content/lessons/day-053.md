## The argument in the steering room

Week thirty. The assistant is live for staff. Elena wants to restrict the data it can see. Daniel wants the assistant to answer more questions. They are in your meeting room, and the voices are rising.

```chat
# Meeting room, Thursday, 3:30 PM
Elena Rossi | CISO | If the assistant sees full order details, one mistake exposes customers. I will not approve it.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | If it cannot see order details it cannot help customers. Then why did we build it?
Elena Rossi | CISO | That is your problem, not mine.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | It is our problem. You are on this project.
```

You have two senior people with opposite interests and both are right. Conflict is not a sign that the project is failing. It is a sign that it matters. What matters is whether you can turn a fight about positions into a conversation about interests.

```key
Positions are what people say they want. Interests are why they want it. Negotiate on interests, and positions often turn out to be compatible.
```

## Positions and interests

| Person | Position | Interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elena | Restrict what the assistant sees | Avoid a customer data breach and keep her job and the company's licence |
| Daniel | Let it see everything | Deliver the benefit he promised the board |

The interests are not opposed. Both want a safe, useful assistant. Options such as showing only the last order, masking sensitive fields or asking the customer to confirm identity serve both.

## Five ways people handle conflict

```cards
zap | Compete | Win at the other's expense. | Damages the relationship, and wins only the battle.
users | Collaborate | Work together to meet both sets of interests. | Takes time and trust.
check | Compromise | Each gives up something. | Neither gets what they need.
clock | Avoid | Postpone the issue. | The problem grows.
heart | Accommodate | Give way to keep the peace. | Resentment and hidden costs.
```

Collaboration is best when the issue matters to both and the relationship matters. Compromise is quicker when time is short. Avoiding can be right for trivial issues, or when emotions are too high to talk.

```ref

```


## A structure for the conversation

```flow
Calm it | Lower the temperature, agree the goal both share
Listen | Each states their interest, the other summarises it
Options | Generate several solutions without judging
Test | Check each against both sets of interests
Agree | One option, with owners, dates and a review
```

## Your decision

The room is tense. Elena says "I will not approve it", and Daniel looks at you.

```decision
? What do you do first?
- Tell them both to calm down and decide for them | You use authority you do not have, and both resent it.
- Side with Daniel, because the business pays | Elena feels overruled on her core responsibility, and may block you later.
+ Pause, restate the shared goal of a safe and useful assistant, ask each to say what they are afraid of, and then ask for three options that would meet both | You convert a fight into a problem solving session, and you remain neutral.
= Neutral process beats a quick decision. Surface fears, find shared goals and ask for options.
```

## Negotiation basics

```accordion
Prepare | Know your interests, your alternatives and theirs, before you start.
Ask questions | Why do you need that? What would make it acceptable?
Use objective standards | Agreed criteria, such as risk ratings or test results, replace opinion.
Separate people from the problem | Be firm on the problem and gentle with people.
```
