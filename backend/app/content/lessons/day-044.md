## Your hybrid delivery model on paper

You have followed Lighthouse through sprints, flow, governance and a demanding executive. Daniel now asks for one thing: a clear description of how the project is actually run, so the PMO, the team and the next project manager can read it and agree.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 9:00 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | When you leave, or when Victoria asks how we deliver, I want to hand over a page that says what we do and why. Not a methodology book.
PMO lead | Programme office | And it must show where our gates fit.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | And please do not describe a process we do not follow.
```

A delivery model is a short, honest description of how work flows from idea to production, who decides at each step and what evidence proves progress. It is the artifact that shows you can design a way of working, not just follow one.

```key
A hybrid delivery model names its layers, its roles, its events, its controls and its metrics, and says what is fixed and what flexes.
```

## What to include

```cards
layers | Layers | Governance, release planning and sprint delivery, with the link between them. | A diagram with no explanation.
users | Roles | Sponsor, Product Owner, delivery lead, team, security and operations, with their decisions. | Roles listed with no decision rights.
workflow | Events and gates | Sprint events, monthly reviews and stage gates, with dates. | Gates that appear nowhere in the plan.
chart | Measures | Outcomes and flow measures that show progress and risk. | Activity counts.
shield | Controls | Security, quality and change controls that cut across layers. | Controls left to the end.
```

## A structure for the page

```flow
Purpose | Why this model, in two sentences
Layers | What happens at governance, release and sprint level
Roles | Who decides what
Rhythm | Events, gates and their dates
Evidence | What each gate expects from each sprint
Measures | What you report and to whom
```

## A short example of a good paragraph

Compare two ways to describe a gate. The strong one names evidence and a decision, so a reviewer knows what to look for.

```accordion
Weak | "Security gate before go-live."
Strong | "Gate 3, week 20. Evidence: penetration test report with no open high findings, data flow approved by the CISO, rollback rehearsed. Decision by the sponsor and the CISO: proceed, proceed with conditions, or stop."
```

## Your decision

The PMO asks you to describe the model as agile. Rahul asks you to describe it as waterfall with sprints. Neither is quite right.

```decision
? How do you name it?
- Call it agile to please the team | The PMO notices fixed gates and budgets and distrusts the label.
- Call it waterfall with sprints | The team feels misrepresented, and the benefits of iteration disappear from the description.
+ Describe what is actually fixed and what flexes, call it a hybrid model, and show how sprint evidence feeds each gate | The label follows the facts, and each side can recognise itself in the description.
= Describe behaviour, not fashion. A precise description is more useful than a label.
```
