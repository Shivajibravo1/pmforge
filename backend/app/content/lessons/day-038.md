## Three charts, three different stories

After eight sprints Daniel has started to look at charts. He sends you a screenshot.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 7:55 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | This burndown has gone flat for a week. Is the team stuck?
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | We were finishing a big story. Nothing closed until Thursday.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | And what is this coloured chart that keeps getting wider?
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | That is the cumulative flow diagram. The band in the middle means work is piling up.
```

Charts are useful because they tell you something is wrong earlier than a conversation would. They are also easy to misread, and easy to misuse as a way of judging people. A project manager needs to know what each one actually says.

```key
Metrics are for the team to see how work flows and to spot problems early. They are not a scorecard for individuals, and a number without its context misleads.
```

## Velocity

```cards
chart | What it is | Story points finished per sprint, averaged over recent sprints. | Used to compare teams, which have different scales.
clock | What it tells you | How much this team usually finishes, so you can forecast. | Pushing it up by inflating estimates.
alert | The trap | When velocity becomes a target, points inflate and quality falls. | A lovely number and a worse product.
```

```ref
PMI | Agile Practice Guide, velocity, burndown and burnup charts | Know what each chart shows and what it hides. Burnup shows scope change, burndown can hide it.
```


## Burndown

A sprint burndown shows remaining work against time. A release burndown shows remaining points in the backlog against sprints.

```flow
Start of sprint | All planned points remaining
Mid sprint | A steady fall means work is closing
Flat line | Work is in progress but nothing is done
Late cliff | Everything closes at the end, a sign of big batches
```

Illustrative: a sprint plans 20 points over 10 days. A steady burn closes 2 points a day. If after 5 days 15 points remain, the team has closed 5 points, only half of the expected 10, so the sprint is at risk. The numbers are illustrative.

## Cumulative flow diagram

A cumulative flow diagram, or CFD, stacks the count of items in each state over time: to do, in progress, in review, done. Parallel, evenly spaced bands mean smooth flow.

```cards
eye | A widening middle band | Work is entering faster than it leaves, a bottleneck. | Items sit in review for days.
link | Flat Done band | Nothing is being finished. | Many items started, none completed.
check | Steady, parallel bands | Smooth, predictable flow. | Complacency, since flow can still be slow.
```

```ref
Kanban | Kanban Guide, flow metrics: WIP, cycle time, work item age, throughput | Cumulative flow shows these over time. Widening bands mean queues are building.
DORA | Delivery performance metrics | Use outcome metrics, not only activity, to talk about delivery health.
```


## Your decision

Daniel sees the flat burndown and asks you to push the team to speed up.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Push the team to work longer hours | Quality drops and the next sprint is worse, so the line goes flat again.
- Ignore the chart because "metrics are not important" | You miss a real signal about large batches of work.
+ Ask the team what the flat period means, look for large stories or review delays, and agree with Daniel how the chart should be read | You use the chart as a question, not a verdict, and you find the cause.
= Treat a chart as the start of a conversation. Ask what happened before you decide what to do.
```

## Using the three together

| Question | Best chart |
| --- | --- |
| How much can we do next sprint? | Velocity |
| Are we on track this sprint? | Sprint burndown |
| When will the release finish? | Release burndown or forecast |
| Where does work get stuck? | Cumulative flow diagram |

## What to remember

```checklist
Velocity forecasts, it does not judge
Burndown shows progress against time
A CFD shows where work piles up
A chart is a question, not a verdict
Never use metrics to rank individuals
```
