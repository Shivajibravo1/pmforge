## Two documents the committee will read

After the steering committee, Daniel gives you two deadlines. He wants a clean RAID log and a one page status report he can show his own directors.

```chat
# Message, Friday, 11:15 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | The committee approved option B. Now I need the RAID log and the status report, and they must agree with each other.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | If the status says amber for risk but the log shows three red items, someone will notice.
Elena Rossi | CISO | Please keep the AI data flow risk visible. It is not closed.
```

Two documents describe the same project. If they disagree, the reader stops trusting both. This is the first portfolio piece that shows your communication under real conditions.

```key
The status report is the headline. The RAID log is the evidence. A reader should be able to move from one to the other and find the same story.
```

## What each document does

```cards
file | Status report | One page. Colour, progress, decisions needed, top risks, next milestones. | Nine pages that nobody reads.
alert | RAID log | The detailed list with owners, responses and dates. | A list that is not reviewed and soon wrong.
link | The link between them | Every risk named in the status exists in the log with the same rating. | Colours that do not match the evidence.
```

## A one page status report

```flow
Overall | One colour and one sentence
Progress | What was achieved this period
Next | What happens in the next period
Decisions | What you need, from whom, by when
Risks | Top three, with owners and dates
```

## A consistency check

```rag
Status colour | A | Matches the log, which has two high risks with active mitigation
Top risks | G | Same three appear in both documents
Dates | G | Milestones in the report match the baseline
Owners | G | Every named action has an owner in the log
```

## Your decision

The log shows a red risk on the AI data flow. The status report would look better if you left it off the one page.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Leave it off the status report to keep it clean | The committee finds it later in the log and asks why it was hidden.
- Show every risk on the one page | The page becomes unreadable and the key message is lost.
+ Show the top three, including the AI data flow if it is top three, with owner and date, and refer to the log for the rest | The page stays readable and nothing important is hidden.
= Select risks by rating, not by comfort. The one page is a summary, never a filter for good news.
```

## A worked example: one risk, two documents

The same risk must read the same way in both places. Here is the AI data flow risk as it appears in each.

```accordion
In the RAID log | If customer data sent to the AI vendor includes personal data not covered by contract, privacy approval may be refused, blocking launch. Probability 3, impact 5, score 15. Owner: CISO. Response: agree redaction rules and contract terms by week 12. Review: weekly.
In the status report | Top risk 2: AI data flow and privacy approval. Score 15, owner Elena Rossi, mitigation in progress, decision point week 12.
```

The status line is shorter, but every fact in it, the score, the owner and the date, can be found in the log. When a director asks a follow up question, you answer from the log in seconds.

## AI in your reporting

```cards
file | AI drafts the one page | A fast first version from notes and the log. | It can restate a red risk in softer words.
search | AI checks consistency | Ask it to compare the report and the log for contradictions. | It misses implied conflicts.
lock | Sensitive content | Names and financials stay in approved tools. | A leak through a pasted report.
```

```ai
Use AI to check the report against the log for contradictions, then verify its findings yourself. A second reader improves quality, and it does not replace accountability.
```
