## The interview room

You have applied for the role of Programme Manager, AI Delivery, at a company that is starting its first large AI programme. The panel has three people: a hiring manager, a head of security and a business director. They have read your capstone plan. They will not ask for definitions. They will ask what you would do.

This is a simulation. Your answers will be reviewed against a rubric by a mentor and by an AI grader. The AI grader looks for structure, specifics and judgement. It cannot tell if a claim about your experience is true, so be accurate, and use your capstone as the example.

```chat
# Interview panel, 10:00 AM
Hiring manager | Panel | Thanks for coming. We have a pilot assistant at 80 percent accuracy and a launch date in two weeks. The business wants to go. Walk us through what you would do.
Head of security | Panel | And what would worry you most in the first month of live use?
Business director | Panel | And how would you prove to me that it was worth the money?
```

```key
A strong answer states the decision, gives two or three reasons tied to evidence, names the trade-off and says what would change your mind. Keep it to ninety seconds and use one concrete example.
```

## A structure for answers

```flow
Headline | Your decision or position in one sentence
Evidence | Two or three facts or measures you would rely on
Action | What you would do, in order
Risk | The main trade-off and how you would manage it
Example (illustrative) | A short example from your capstone or your experience
```

## Question one: the 80 percent assistant

```decision
? How would you answer the hiring manager?
- Launch on time, because eighty percent is good and the business wants it | You ignore the threshold and the unknown risks, and the panel hears no judgement.
- Say you would need more information, and stop there | Honest but unhelpful. You must still give a position.
+ Say you would not simply launch or simply delay: you would check which topics fail and how serious the failures are, fix any privacy or legal risks first, launch a narrower scope that meets the threshold with human handover for weak areas, define criteria to widen, and brief the sponsor on the options | A clear position, with evidence, controls and a route that serves the business.
= Give a position. Show you use evidence, narrow scope and agree criteria to widen.
```

## Practising the other two

```incident
# Practised answer ignored the question (illustrative)
What happened | A candidate delivered a memorised answer about a different case when asked about a model at 80 percent against a 95 percent target.
Impact | The panel marked the answer low for judgement.
Root cause | Practice focused on scripts, not on listening and adapting to the scenario.
Resolution | The candidate practised a structure: restate, facts, options, recommendation, risks and next steps, and used feedback, human or AI, to improve and not to script.
PM move | Practise a structure, not a script, and listen to the question.
```

```answer
? In an interview, the model scores 80 percent against a 95 percent target and the sponsor wants to launch. How do you answer?
- Say you would delay | It gives no reasoning.
- Say you would launch | It ignores the gap.
+ I restate the situation, ask what the gap is by category and what failure costs, then give options: limited launch, human review for weak areas, delay or an improvement sprint with retest. I recommend one with reasons, name the risks and controls, say who decides and how I communicate, and say what I would measure next.
= Restate and ask.
= Options and recommendation.
= Risks and next steps.
```

## Your task

Write your answers to the three panel questions above, each in 150 to 250 words, using the five part structure. Then answer these two follow ups: "Tell us about a time you disagreed with a sponsor or senior colleague" and "What would you do differently on your capstone". Use your capstone as the main example, and be accurate about anything you present as experience. Submit all five answers for review.
