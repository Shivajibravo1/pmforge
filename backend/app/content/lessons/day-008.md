## The person who said yes and then blocked you

You have approval from the sponsor. The team is ready. Two days before go-live, the security lead says: "I was never consulted. This cannot go live."

Nobody did anything wrong on paper. You simply never mapped who could stop the project.

```key
Most IT projects fail because of people and decisions, not technology. Knowing who matters, and what each person needs, is the cheapest risk control you have.
```

## The usual cast

| Stakeholder | Influence | What they need from you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sponsor | Funds and decides | Clear outcomes, honest status, early warning |
| CIO | Sets technology direction | Alignment with strategy and architecture |
| CISO | Can block release | Evidence of security and compliance |
| Enterprise architect | Approves design | Fit with standards, integration plans |
| Operations / service desk | Runs it after you | Runbooks, monitoring, training, handover time |
| Vendors | Deliver parts | Clear scope, timely decisions, payment on milestones |
| Business users | Adopt it | Involvement, training, a say in priorities |
| Finance / procurement | Controls spend | Accurate forecasts, contract compliance |

## Map them on two axes

Place each person by how much influence they have and how interested they are. Redo the map whenever scope or leadership changes.

```cards
users | High influence, high interest | Manage closely. Involve them in decisions and give early warning.
eye | High influence, low interest | Keep satisfied. These are the people who surprise you late.
zap | Low influence, high interest | Keep informed. They are often your adopters and advocates.
book | Low influence, low interest | Monitor with light updates.
```

## Mistakes to avoid

```warn
Meeting operations only at go-live. Treating security as a final approval step instead of an early partner. Assuming the sponsor and the real decision-maker are the same person.
```

## Check your understanding

```quiz
? The CISO can block your release but rarely attends your meetings. Where do they sit on the map?
- Monitor
+ High influence, low interest: keep satisfied and bring them in early
- Keep informed
= High influence with low engagement is exactly where late surprises come from.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
Map stakeholders by influence and interest
High influence and low interest means keep satisfied, engage early
Bring operations and security in at the start, not at go-live
The sponsor is not always the real decision-maker
```
