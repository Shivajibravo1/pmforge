## The first cloud bill

Two months after the first Lighthouse workloads moved, the invoice arrives. It is three times the estimate. Victoria forwards it with one line: "Explain."

```chat
# Message, Friday, 8:05 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | The cloud bill is three times the forecast. Explain.
Cloud engineer | Platform team | Test environments run all night. Some virtual machines are oversized. A forgotten experiment is still running.
Finance partner | Finance | I cannot allocate this to any team. Half the resources have no tags.
```

Cloud turns a fixed cost into a variable cost. That is a benefit, because you pay for what you use. It is also a risk, because spend can rise as quickly as usage. FinOps is the practice of bringing finance, engineering and business together to manage cloud spend with accountability.

```key
FinOps is a shared responsibility. Engineers see the cost of what they build, finance sees where it goes and business leaders decide what is worth paying for.
```

## Three phases

```flow
Inform | Make spend visible, tagged and allocated
Optimise | Remove waste, right size, commit where it makes sense
Operate | Build cost into planning, reviews and governance
```

```ref
FinOps | FinOps Framework phases: inform, optimize, operate | Interview line: first make cost visible, then optimise, then run it continuously with shared accountability.
```


## Where the money goes

```cards
tag | Missing tags | No one knows who owns the cost. | Unallocated spend nobody manages.
server | Idle and oversized resources | Servers running at five percent load. | Paying for capacity never used.
clock | Always on test environments | Development running at night and weekends. | Large avoidable cost.
database | Data transfer and storage | Data leaving the cloud or kept forever. | Surprise charges in the second month.
coins | Unused commitments | Reserved capacity that nobody uses. | Prepaid money wasted.
```

## Levers a PM should know

```accordion
Right sizing | Match resources to real use.
Scheduling | Switch off non production at night and weekends.
Commitments | Reserved or committed use discounts for steady workloads, with the risk of paying for unused capacity.
Autoscaling | Scale up and down with demand.
Storage tiers | Move old data to cheaper storage and delete what is not needed.
Budgets and alerts | Notify owners before a limit is crossed.
```

Check your provider's current discount models and rules, because they change.

## Your decision

The overspend is real, and the sponsor wants immediate cuts. Rahul warns that cutting resources in the wrong place could damage the pilot.

```decision
? What is your plan?
- Cut every environment by half immediately | You may break the pilot and lose trust in the platform.
- Explain it as normal, and wait for next month | Spend continues and you lose credibility.
+ Tag and allocate spend this week, switch off non production outside working hours, right size the three biggest items after checking utilisation with Rahul, and set budget alerts for owners | Quick savings with low risk, and ownership that stops the problem returning.
= Visibility first, then low risk savings, then structural changes.
```

## Unit economics

Total cost is useful. Cost per outcome is better. A cost per order processed, or cost per resolved ticket, shows if the cloud is delivering value. If cost doubles and orders triple, that is good. If cost doubles and orders do not change, it is waste.

## AI costs

```cards
coins | Usage based pricing | Cost grows with the number of tokens and calls. | A popular feature with an unexpected bill.
chart | Cost per resolved query | The unit measure that connects spend to value. | Counting only the total.
shield | Spending limits | Budgets and rate limits per team and per key. | One runaway loop or abuse.
```

```ai
Shift | AI features create variable, usage based cloud costs that surprise teams.
Example (illustrative) | An assistant goes viral internally and token costs triple in a week. Rate limits and alerts, set before launch, hold the spend within budget.
Use AI to | Forecast scenarios and draft cost reports.
Keep human | Setting budgets and decisions on trade offs.
Risk | A popular feature that multiplies cost overnight.
Control | Set a monthly budget, per user rate limits and alerts before launch. Track cost per resolved query and review provider pricing, which changes.
Interview line | I set a budget, rate limits and alerts for every AI feature before launch and track cost per resolved query.
```

```ref

```

```incident
# AI feature cost grew with use (illustrative)
What happened | A pilot AI assistant had a small monthly cost. After launch to all staff, usage rose sharply and the bill exceeded the forecast.
Impact | The sponsor questioned the business case and froze expansion.
Root cause | The estimate used pilot usage, with no unit cost, usage limits or alerts.
Resolution | The PM added cost per use, a usage forecast with ranges, budget alerts, limits and a review of model choice.
PM move | Estimate AI cost per use and forecast usage as a range with alerts.
```

```answer
? How do you control cloud cost on a project, including AI usage?
- Review the bill at month end | Problems are found too late.
- Cut spend when it is high | Blind cuts can harm service.
+ I follow the FinOps cycle of inform, optimise and operate. I tag resources, set budgets and alerts, estimate before building with ranges, and track unit cost such as cost per transaction. For AI I track cost per use, set limits, choose models by need and review monthly with owners.
= Tag and alert.
= Unit cost.
= AI per use cost.
```
