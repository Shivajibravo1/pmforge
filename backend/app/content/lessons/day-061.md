## "Whose problem is this?"

Lighthouse moves to the cloud. In the first week, a storage bucket holding customer order exports is found open to the internet. Nobody changed a firewall. Nobody hacked anything. Everybody assumed someone else was responsible.

```chat
# Incident channel, Monday, 7:50 AM
Elena Rossi | CISO | An order export bucket is public. Who owns it?
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | We built the pipeline. The cloud provider hosts it. Surely they secure it.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | Our team only reads the exports. We did not create it.
Cloud engineer | Platform team | The default was private. Someone changed a setting during a test.
```

The question "whose problem is this?" is the heart of cloud governance. The answer is the shared responsibility model: the provider secures the cloud itself, and you secure what you put in it and how you configure it. The line moves depending on the service model.

```key
Providers are responsible for security OF the cloud. Customers are responsible for security IN the cloud. The more managed the service, the more the provider owns, but data, identities and configuration are always yours.
```

## Three service models

```cards
server | IaaS | Virtual machines, networks and storage. You manage the operating system, patches, applications and data. | Unpatched servers and open ports.
layers | PaaS | A managed platform for your code and data. The provider runs the operating system and runtime. | Weak application configuration and secrets in code.
cloud | SaaS | A finished application. You manage users, data and settings. | Excess permissions and unmanaged sharing.
```

```ref
NIST | SP 800-145 cloud definition: IaaS, PaaS, SaaS and deployment models | Use the exact definitions to answer who manages what in each model. A frequent opening question in cloud project interviews.
```


## Who owns what

| Layer | IaaS | PaaS | SaaS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Data and its classification | You | You | You |
| Identities and access | You | You | You |
| Application | You | You | Provider |
| Operating system | You | Provider | Provider |
| Physical and network infrastructure | Provider | Provider | Provider |

Check the exact split in your provider's published model, because it differs by service.

```ref
Azure | Shared responsibility in the cloud | The provider secures the cloud, the customer secures what runs in it. AWS publishes the same idea as the Shared Responsibility Model. Know that the split moves with the service model.
CIS | Benchmarks and the Controls v8 cloud companion guide | Ask which CIS benchmark the team uses to harden cloud services.
```


## Deployment models

```accordion
Public cloud | Shared infrastructure operated by a provider such as Azure. Fast, elastic and pay as you use.
Private cloud | Dedicated environment, often on premises. More control and more cost.
Hybrid | A mix, often during migration or for data that must stay on premises.
Multi cloud | Several providers, chosen for fit or resilience, with more complexity to govern.
```

## Your decision

The bucket is public. Daniel asks who is to blame.

```decision
? What do you say?
- The cloud provider, because they hosted it | They did not change the setting. The model puts configuration in your hands.
- The engineer who changed it, and they should be disciplined | A person made a mistake, but a system allowed it and nothing detected it.
+ Close the exposure now, check access logs for misuse, then fix the system: policy that blocks public buckets, alerts on change and clear ownership of each resource | You contain the risk, assess the impact and remove the cause.
= Contain first, then fix the guardrails. Blame a person only after you have fixed the system.
```

## What a PM must ask

```checklist
Which service model is each component, and who owns what
Where is data stored, and which country and law apply
Who owns each resource, with a tag and a named owner
How are identities and access reviewed
What is logged, and who watches the logs
What is the exit plan if the provider changes terms
```

## Risks in plain language

```rag
Misconfiguration | R | The most common cause of cloud data exposure
Identity | A | Excess permissions and shared accounts
Lock in | A | Proprietary services make leaving costly
Data residency | A | Check the location against the law
Provider outage | A | Design for failure, and test it
```

## AI services in the cloud

```cards
brain | Managed AI service | The provider runs the model, you own the prompts, data and access. | Sensitive data sent without a contract or retention check.
database | Your data in AI | Documents used to ground answers stay your responsibility. | Assistants that can read files the user cannot.
key | Keys and secrets | AI service keys must be stored in a vault, rotated and scoped. | Keys in code or in chat messages.
```

```ai
Shift | Cloud AI services move your data across a shared responsibility line, so contracts and risk registers must say where it goes.
Example (illustrative) | A team picks an AI service and finds prompts are logged in another region. The PM records the finding in the risk register and asks legal for contract changes.
Use AI to | Summarise provider terms and prepare vendor questions.
Keep human | Accepting or rejecting the terms with legal and security.
Risk | Prompt data stored, logged or used for training without anyone noticing.
Control | Ask where the prompt data goes, whether it is stored or used for training, which region processes it and who can access the logs. Record the answers in the contract and risk register.
Interview line | For every AI service I ask where prompt data goes, whether it is stored or used for training, which region and who can access logs.
```

```ref
NIST | AI RMF Govern and Map functions | A managed AI service still needs your own policy, data rules and monitoring. Provider responsibility does not remove yours.
OWASP | Top 10 for LLM Applications | Check which risks the provider covers and which remain yours, such as prompt injection and data exposure.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Chooses between a managed model service and self hosting, and owns the model configuration. | Assumes the provider secures everything.
shield | AI security consultant | Maps what the customer secures: identity, data, prompts, logging. | Reads "managed" as "safe".
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Owns guardrails, logging and keys around the service. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Keys and logs left to defaults.
money | Procurement | Reads data use, retention and region terms. | Terms accepted from a template.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Confirms what customer data may reach the service. | Data flow assumed.
```

```incident
# Managed AI service, customer data exposed (illustrative)
What happened | A team used a managed AI service and assumed the provider handled all security. Prompts containing customer data were stored in application logs readable by many staff.
Impact | A privacy review found the exposure and the feature was paused.
Root cause | Shared responsibility was not mapped. Logging and access to prompts sat on the customer side.
Resolution | The PM produced a responsibility table, restricted log access, masked sensitive fields and added it to the go live checklist.
PM move | Map who owns what for each AI service before build.
```

```answer
? Explain the shared responsibility model and how it applies to an AI service.
- The cloud provider secures everything | Customers still own identity, data and configuration.
- The customer secures everything | The provider secures the underlying platform.
+ The provider secures the underlying infrastructure and the customer secures what it puts on it: identity, data, configuration and usage. The split shifts with the service model. For an AI service the customer still owns access control, what data goes in, prompt and output logging and human review. I put this in a table and assign owners.
= Provider versus customer.
= Depends on service model.
= Name AI data and logs.
```
