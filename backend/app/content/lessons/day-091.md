## "It passed every test, and it is still wrong"

Lighthouse's board approves a new project: an AI assistant for customer support. Victoria asks you to plan it like the portal migration. In the first review, the lead engineer says something that changes the plan.

```chat
# Kickoff, Monday, 10:00 AM
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | I cannot tell you in advance that it will be right ninety five percent of the time. I can only find out by testing it on real data.
Victoria Hale | COO | Then how do I approve a date and a budget?
Data lead | Data team | And our order history has gaps. The assistant will only be as good as what it can read.
Elena Rossi | CISO | And it can be wrong in ways we have not tested.
```

Most IT projects build deterministic systems. Given the same input, they give the same output, and you can test them against a specification. AI projects, especially those using machine learning and generative models, are probabilistic and data dependent. The output varies, quality is a measured range and the same system can behave differently when the data or the world changes. A project manager has to change how they plan, test, report and govern.

```key
AI projects are managed by experiments, evidence and thresholds, not by fixed specifications and pass or fail tests. Plan for learning, and agree what good enough means before you build.
```

## How AI projects differ

```cards
dice | Probabilistic output | The same question can get different answers. Quality is a rate, not a yes or no. | Promising exact behaviour.
database | Data dependence | Quality depends on the data used for training, retrieval and testing. | Starting build before checking the data.
flask | Experimental nature | Feasibility is unknown until you try. | A fixed plan for an uncertain outcome.
refresh | Change over time | Data, users and models change, so performance can drift. | Treating launch as the finish line.
users | Human impact | Wrong outputs affect people, and there are ethical and legal duties. | Treating ethics as a later task.
```

## Traditional and AI delivery compared

| Aspect | Traditional software | AI project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requirements | Specified behaviour | Target performance and acceptable errors |
| Testing | Pass or fail cases | Evaluation sets and thresholds |
| Plan | Scope fixed, effort estimated | Staged experiments, scope adjusted by evidence |
| Done | Features delivered | Quality threshold met and monitored |
| After launch | Maintenance | Monitoring, drift and retraining or tuning |
| Risk | Defects | Bias, error, privacy, security and misuse |

## A staged approach

```flow
Frame | Define the problem, success measure and constraints
Feasibility | Quick test of data and approach on a sample
Prototype | Build a small version, evaluate on real cases
Pilot | Limited users, human oversight, measure
Scale | Harden, secure, integrate and govern
Operate | Monitor quality, cost and drift, improve
```

Each stage ends with a decision gate based on evidence. Stopping at an early gate is a success of the process, not a failure, if it saves money on something that would not work.

```ref
ISO | ISO/IEC 5338 AI system life cycle processes | A reference for staged delivery of AI systems, from data and model to deployment and retirement.
```


## Your decision

Victoria asks for a fixed date and a fixed accuracy before approving the budget.

```decision
? What do you say?
- Promise ninety five percent accuracy by a fixed date | You commit to an outcome nobody can know yet, and you risk pressure to hide failure.
- Refuse to give any date or target | You look evasive, and funding is lost.
+ Propose a staged budget with a feasibility gate in six weeks, a target range agreed with the business, an evaluation set to measure it and a decision on the next stage at each gate | She gets control and dates, and the uncertainty is handled honestly.
= Fund in stages with evidence gates. Promise the process and the decision dates, not the accuracy.
```

## What a PM does differently

```accordion
Define good enough | Agree thresholds with the business before building, such as accuracy, safety and cost per query.
Build an evaluation set early | A fixed set of realistic cases to measure each change.
Plan for people | Humans review outputs, handle exceptions and own the result.
Plan for change | Monitoring, retraining or tuning and governance after launch.
Communicate uncertainty | Use ranges and confidence, not false precision.
```
