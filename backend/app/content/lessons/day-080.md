## Two days before go live

It is Wednesday at 4:10 PM. Go live is Friday at 6:00 PM, and a campaign is booked for Saturday. A security advisory has just been published for a widely used open source library inside the Lighthouse portal. It is rated critical, and exploitation has been reported.

This is a simulation. Each decision has consequences. The best answer weighs risk, time and the people involved.

```chat
# Security channel, Wednesday, 4:10 PM
Elena Rossi | CISO | A critical vulnerability was published for a library we use. Reports say attackers are already using it.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | A patched version exists. Upgrading touches the checkout flow, and we have not tested it.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Go live is Friday. The campaign is Saturday. Tell me what we do.
Operations lead | Operations | The portal is not yet public. The old system is still running.
```

## What you know and do not know

```rag
Is it exploitable in our setup | A | Not yet confirmed, depends on how we use the library
Is it internet facing | A | The new portal is not live yet
Patch available | G | A fixed version exists
Patch tested | R | Not tested with checkout
Exploitation in the wild | R | Reported by the advisory
```

```key
Go live does not change what is vulnerable. It changes who can reach it. Establish exposure first, then choose between patch, mitigate and delay.
```

## Your options

```flow
Patch and retest | Upgrade the library, run regression tests, launch if clean
Mitigate | Apply a configuration or network control that blocks the attack path
Launch with acceptance | Go live and patch after the weekend
Delay | Move the launch until fixed and verified
```

```ref
CISA | Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog | Check whether the CVE is known to be exploited. It changes how fast you must act.
FIRST | CVSS | Severity helps triage but context decides the decision.
```


## Your decision

```decision
? What do you recommend at 4:30 PM?
- Launch on Friday as planned and patch next week | You release a known, actively exploited critical flaw to the internet, and the acceptance decision was never made by the owner.
- Cancel the launch now | You may not need to, and you have not explored the options.
- Patch tonight without testing, because it is critical | An untested change to checkout could break the business on its biggest day.
+ Confirm exposure with Elena and Rahul tonight, start the upgrade in a branch with regression tests on checkout, prepare a mitigation as a fallback, set a go or no go checkpoint for Thursday evening, and tell Daniel the three possible outcomes and their likelihood | You use the time you have, protect the launch and keep the decision with the right people.
= Understand exposure, work the patch and a mitigation in parallel, and set a checkpoint. Do not launch with an unassessed critical flaw.
```


## If the patch cannot be ready

```accordion
Mitigation | A web application firewall rule, feature switch off or network restriction that blocks the path, approved by the CISO with an expiry date.
Reduced launch | Release without the affected feature or to a limited audience.
Delay | If neither is safe, delay and explain the reason clearly.
Acceptance | Only the risk owner may accept, in writing, with a review date.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 change enablement, emergency change | If the fix must ship before go live, use the emergency route and record approvals.
NIST | SP 800-40 patch management planning | If no patch exists, plan compensating controls and a time limited risk acceptance.
```


## Lessons

```cards
box | Know your components | A software bill of materials would have shown the library at once. | Hours lost finding where it is used.
clock | Prepare an emergency path | Agree in advance how critical patches skip normal queues. | Fighting the process during the event.
chart | Risk by exposure | The same flaw is less urgent on an internal system than on the internet. | Treating all critical flaws the same.
```
