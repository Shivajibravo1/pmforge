## "Where is it written down?"

The assistant has passed its evaluation and its security tests. Before the steering group approves the pilot, the compliance lead asks two simple questions. Where are the AI risks recorded, with owners and controls? And where is a plain description of what this model does, what it was tested on and what it should not be used for?

```chat
# Message, Monday, 9:20 AM
Legal counsel | Legal | I need an AI risk register, and a description of the system that a non technical person can read.
Elena Rossi | CISO | The register should map to the risks we have discussed: quality, privacy, security, fairness and misuse.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | I can write the technical facts for a model card. Help me make it understandable.
```

An AI risk register is a project risk register extended for AI specific risks, with clear owners, controls and tests. A model card is a short document describing a model or an AI system: its purpose, data, evaluation, limits and intended and unintended uses. They are two of the core pieces of evidence for accountable AI.

```key
If it is not written down, you cannot show that you thought about it. A risk register and a model card turn good intentions into inspectable evidence.
```

## The AI risk register

```cards
chart | Quality risks | Wrong, outdated or inconsistent answers. | Averages hiding serious failures.
lock | Privacy and security risks | Leakage, injection, unauthorised access. | Risks left to general IT.
users | Fairness and harm | Unequal performance for groups, harmful content. | Not measuring by group or language.
scale | Legal and compliance | Regulation, contracts, intellectual property. | Surprises at audit.
coins | Cost and dependency | Usage growth, provider changes, lock in. | Single provider with no exit.
heart | People and trust | Staff concerns, overreliance, loss of skills. | Ignoring the human side.
```

## A register row

| Field | Example content |
| --- | --- |
| Risk | The assistant gives wrong refund advice |
| Cause | Outdated document or retrieval of the wrong passage |
| Impact | Customer harm, financial claim, trust |
| Likelihood and impact rating | Rated with the agreed scale |
| Controls | Approved sources, citations, handover for refunds, evaluation cases |
| Owner | Named service owner |
| Test and evidence | Evaluation results and sampling logs |
| Review date | Next review |

## A model card

```accordion
Purpose and intended use | What it is for, who uses it and in which context.
Out of scope uses | What it must not be used for.
Model and components | Model provider and version, prompt version, retrieval sources, tools.
Data | Sources, date range, known gaps, personal data handling.
Evaluation | Test sets, metrics, results by category, known failure modes.
Safety and security | Tests performed, controls and limits.
Limitations | Where it performs poorly, languages, topics and groups.
Oversight and contacts | Owner, how to report problems, review dates.
```

## Process

```flow
Draft | Owner and engineer write the first version
Review | Legal, security, data and business review
Test | Check claims against evidence
Publish | Store with version and date, share with those who need it
Update | On every significant change, and on a schedule
```

## Your decision

The team wants to publish a model card that lists strengths only, because customers will read it.

```decision
? What do you recommend?
- Publish strengths only, to protect the brand | The card becomes marketing, and the first failure damages trust and legal position.
- Keep the card internal only | You lose the benefit of honesty and transparency for those affected.
+ Write it accurately, including limitations and known failure modes in plain language, and decide with legal what is shared externally and what remains internal | It is trustworthy, useful and appropriate for each audience.
= A model card with no limitations is an advertisement. Be honest about what it cannot do.
```

## Rating risks

```accordion
Scale | Agree a simple scale such as low, medium and high, with definitions that include harm to people, not only money.
Inherent and residual | Rate before controls and after controls.
Appetite | Compare with the organisation's risk appetite, and escalate what exceeds it.
Acceptance | Only the named risk owner or governance board may accept a residual risk, with a review date.
```

## Quality of the pack

```cards
check | Evidence linked | Each control points to a test or record. | Controls claimed with nothing to show.
users | Plain language | A non specialist can read it. | Jargon that hides risk.
refresh | Alive | Updated after incidents and changes. | A document written once.
```
