## The vendor missed its SLA

Week twenty five. The assistant went live to a small group of staff. Over the weekend the vendor's service was unavailable for several hours. The contract promised 99.9 percent availability. This month it has delivered 98.5 percent so far.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 8:10 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Staff could not use the assistant on Saturday. The vendor says it was planned maintenance. Is that true?
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | We took forty calls. I need a clear answer to give.
Vendor account manager | Vendor | We are sorry for the inconvenience. We are investigating and will send a report.
```

This is a simulation. You have a call with the vendor's account manager and delivery director in an hour. They will be polite. The goal is not to win an argument. It is to establish the facts, protect the service, apply the contract properly and keep the relationship usable for the next two years.

```key
An escalation call has three jobs: agree the facts, agree the remedy and agree what changes. Stay calm, be specific and write down every commitment.
```

## Prepare before you call

```cards
file | The contract terms | The SLA measure, the exclusions and the service credit rules. | Arguing from memory.
chart | The evidence | Your own monitoring, incident times and customer impact. | Relying on the vendor's figures only.
users | The business impact | Forty calls, staff time and risk to the launch. | Describing it as an inconvenience.
flag | Your ask | What you want: root cause, remedy, credit and prevention. | A call with no outcome.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 service level management practice | Compare the measured service with the agreed target before you call. Facts first.
```


## Checking the numbers

Illustrative: a 30 day month has 43,200 minutes. At 99.9 percent the allowed downtime is about 43 minutes. At 98.5 percent, downtime is 1.5 percent of 43,200, which is 648 minutes, or about 10.8 hours.

```stats
43 | Minutes allowed per month
648 | Minutes of downtime so far
10.8 | Hours of downtime
15x | Roughly the allowed downtime
```

648 divided by 43.2 is 15, so downtime is about fifteen times the allowance. Check whether "planned maintenance" is excluded in the contract before you accuse the vendor of a breach.

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, procurement: control procurements and claims | Know the contract remedies before you escalate. Check for service credits and cure periods.
```


## Your decision

At the start of the call the vendor says the outage was planned maintenance and is excluded.

```decision
? How do you respond?
- Accuse them of lying | You lose the relationship and may be wrong about the contract.
- Accept the explanation and move on | If maintenance windows were not notified as the contract requires, you lose a right and your staff the service.
+ Ask for the maintenance notice, the contract clause that excludes it and the notification given, and check these against the SLA wording before agreeing it was excluded | You treat it as a fact check, not an accusation, and protect your position.
= Verify before you accuse or concede. Facts first, positions second.
```

## A structure for the call

```flow
Open | Thank them, state the purpose and the impact
Facts | Timeline, cause and measurement
Contract | What it says and how it applies
Remedy | Credit, fix, additional monitoring
Prevent | Actions, owners and dates
Close | Summarise and send written notes the same day
```

## Escalation ladder

```timeline
Day 0 | Incident reported to the account manager | info
Day 2 | No root cause, escalate to delivery director | warn
Day 5 | No credible plan, escalate to executive sponsor on each side | warn
Day 10 | Formal notice under the contract, with legal input | bad
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 incident management escalation | A ladder from service manager to account director to executive keeps escalation fair and traceable.
```

```incident
# The vendor met uptime and missed quality (illustrative)
What happened | A vendor reported full availability for an AI service while answers got worse for a week after a silent model update.
Impact | Customers received wrong answers and the SLA report showed no breach.
Root cause | The SLA measured uptime only and had no answer quality target or model change notice.
Resolution | The PM escalated with test set evidence, secured a rollback and added quality service levels and change notice at renewal.
PM move | Add quality service levels and change notice to AI contracts.
```

```answer
? A vendor has missed its SLA. How do you handle the escalation?
- Threaten to terminate | Escalation without facts damages the relationship.
- Wait for the next review | Delay costs time and leverage.
+ I check the numbers first, with evidence on the breach, impact and contract terms. I plan the call: objective, facts, the remedy I want and my fallback. I use the escalation ladder from account manager to executive, stay factual, agree actions with dates and follow up in writing. For AI services I look beyond uptime to answer quality and model changes.
= Facts first.
= State the remedy.
= Follow up in writing.
```
