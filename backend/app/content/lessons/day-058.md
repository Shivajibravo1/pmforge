## What do we do when this happens

By now Nimbus has had a late vendor, a conflict between senior people, a resignation and a red status. Each time, you improvised. Victoria asks for something that will help the next project manager.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 8:15 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | I want a playbook. When something goes wrong, people should know who to call, what to say and what to do in the first hour. Not another policy document.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Make it short enough that someone reads it at two in the morning.
Elena Rossi | CISO | And make sure it covers security incidents and AI incidents.
```

An escalation playbook is a short, practical guide for the moments when things go wrong. It names the situations, the people, the first actions and the triggers. It is not a policy for lawyers. It is a tool for people under stress.

```key
A good playbook is short, specific and tested. In a crisis people follow what they have rehearsed, so write the first hour in simple steps.
```

## What belongs in it

```cards
alert | Situations | The common problems: a late vendor, a key resignation, a severity one incident, a security breach, an AI failure. | Generic advice that fits nothing.
users | Roles and contacts | Who decides, who acts, who is told, with phone numbers. | Names that are years out of date.
clock | First hour steps | A checklist of what to do and what to say. | A long document nobody opens.
flag | Triggers | The facts that move you from one level to the next. | Judgement calls without guidance.
```

```ref
NIST | SP 800-61 computer security incident handling guide | An escalation playbook borrows from incident response: roles, triggers, contacts and steps.
CIS | Controls v8, incident response management | Know the control and who in your organisation owns it.
```


## One page per situation

```flow
Recognise | What does this look like, and how severe is it
Contain | The first actions to limit damage
Inform | Who is told, in what order, with what message
Decide | Who decides the next step and by when
Review | What we learn afterwards
```

## An example entry

```accordion
Situation | The AI assistant gives a customer a wrong and harmful answer.
First 15 minutes | Capture the conversation, switch the assistant to human handover mode, inform the service owner and the CISO.
First hour | Assess scope, check for similar answers, brief the sponsor and the communications lead.
Decision | The sponsor and the CISO decide whether to pause the assistant.
After | Root cause, fix, evaluation on the test set, report to governance.
```

## Your decision

The communications lead asks to keep AI incidents out of the playbook "until we understand more".

```decision
? What do you say?
- Agree, to avoid delay | The first real AI incident hits with no plan and everyone guessing.
- Insist on a full document before launch | A perfect plan arrives after the incident.
+ Include a first version now with the first hour steps, the people and the kill switch, mark it as version one, and schedule a review after the first month of live use | The team has something to follow, and it improves with evidence.
= A rough, rehearsed playbook beats a perfect one that does not exist. Start with the first hour and improve.
```

## Test it

```timeline
Week 1 | Playbook written, short | ok
Week 2 | Tabletop exercise, a pretend incident | info
Week 3 | Gaps found: no weekend contact, unclear owner | warn
Week 4 | Version two published | ok
```

## AI and the playbook

```cards
brain | AI incident types | Wrong answer, harmful content, data exposure, prompt attack, outage of the provider. | One category called "AI problem".
check | The kill switch | A tested way to turn the assistant off or to hand over to people. | A switch nobody knows about.
file | AI helps draft the entries | A fast first draft of each situation. | Plausible steps that do not fit your organisation.
```

```ai

Use AI to | Draft playbook steps and scenario scripts.
Keep human | Authorising actions and rehearsing them. Someone with the right authority must own each step, and the team must practise it.
Risk | A playbook that was never tested.
Control | For each scenario define who can switch it off, how a customer reaches a person, what is logged and who notifies others. Rehearse it.
Interview line | My escalation playbook covers AI scenarios: who can switch it off, how a customer reaches a person, what is logged and who notifies. We rehearse it.
```

```incident
# No playbook entry for a wrong AI answer (illustrative)
What happened | An AI assistant gave a customer wrong policy advice. The team argued over who decides to switch it off.
Impact | The assistant stayed live for hours and more customers were affected.
Root cause | The escalation playbook had no entry for AI failures, no owner for the kill switch and no communication step.
Resolution | The PM added entries for wrong answers, data exposure and provider outage, with triggers, owners, rollback and customer messages, then ran a drill.
PM move | Add AI failure scenarios to the playbook and test them.
```

```answer
? What does an escalation playbook contain and how do you know it works?
- A policy document | A policy is not usable in an incident.
- A contact list | Contacts do not say what to do.
+ One page per situation with trigger, severity, owner, first actions, who to tell and when, and the decision to stop or roll back. I include AI scenarios such as wrong answers, data exposure and provider outage. I test it with a drill and update it after each use.
= One page per situation.
= Owners and triggers.
= Drill it.
```
