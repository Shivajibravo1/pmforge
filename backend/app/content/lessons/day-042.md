## "Fixed date, fixed scope"

It is week eighteen. A new executive, Victoria Hale, the chief operating officer, has taken over responsibility for Lighthouse's business outcomes. She has read the plan and she has a simple view.

```chat
# Executive review, Thursday, 5:15 PM
Victoria Hale | COO | The board has been told March. The scope in the charter is what the business needs. You are agile, so you should be faster. Deliver both.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | We are about eight weeks behind the scope as planned.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I told Victoria it would be fine.
```

This is a simulation of one of the hardest conversations in delivery. A senior person is asking for something that cannot be done, as stated. Your job is to avoid both easy exits: agreeing and failing, or refusing and being replaced. The skill is to turn a demand into a decision.

```key
Date, scope, cost and quality are linked. If one is fixed, another must give, or risk must rise. Say it plainly, with numbers, and offer a way forward.
```

## The triangle with a fourth side

```cards

layers | Scope | The list of what is delivered. | Scope fixed and treated as everything.
money | Cost | Budget available, which can often flex, with limits. | Used as a hidden lever.
check | Quality | The level of defects and risk accepted. | Silently cut to hold the other three.
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, project constraints: scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, risk | The triangle has more than three sides. Say which constraint is flexible and which is fixed.
```


## Reading the demand

Victoria did not say "ignore quality". She expects it. That is the lever that quietly moves when no one chooses. Your first move is to make the choice visible.

```flow
Acknowledge | Say what she needs, in her words
Show the facts | Where the project really stands
Show the options | Each with a cost and risk
Recommend | One, with reasons
Ask | The decision, by a date
```

## Options for Lighthouse

| Option | What gives | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Hold date, cut scope | Defer features to a second release | Some users wait for features |
| B. Hold scope, move date | Eight weeks later | Board is told a new date |
| C. Hold both, add people and cut testing | Cost and quality give | Highest risk of a late failure |
| D. Phase the launch | Core on date, remaining scope in waves | Dependent on clear release plan |

```ref
Agile | MoSCoW prioritisation from DSDM | Use Must, Should, Could, Won't to negotiate scope inside a fixed date. A practical answer to fixed date and fixed scope.
```


## Your decision

Victoria says: "I do not want options. I want March and everything."

```decision
? How do you respond?
- Say "we will do our best" | A polite no that becomes a failure in March, with your name on it.
- Say it is impossible and walk away | You lose her support and she looks for someone who says yes.
+ Say you understand March matters, show the numbers on the gap, and recommend option D with the core release in March and a dated plan for the rest, and explain what you would need from her to make it work | She keeps the date she announced, and you keep the integrity of the plan.
= When an executive wants everything, give her the version of yes that is true. Show what she can have on the date, and when the rest follows.
```

## What to prepare

```accordion
The numbers | Remaining points, forecast range, and the date range they imply. Illustrative: the remaining 120 points at an average of 20 per sprint is 6 sprints, which is 12 weeks.
The cost of each option | Money, risk and what the business loses.
The cost of doing nothing | The date at which the board hears about a failure if nothing changes.
What you need | Decisions, people, vendor escalation or a clearer scope priority.
```

```incident
# Fixed date and scope accepted for an AI feature (illustrative)
What happened | An executive demanded a fixed date and full scope for an AI assistant. The PM accepted to avoid conflict.
Impact | The team cut testing and monitoring to hit the date, and the assistant gave wrong answers after launch.
Root cause | The trade off between date, scope, cost and quality was not made visible, and quality became the hidden variable.
Resolution | The PM returned with options: reduce scope, move the date, add cost or accept risk, each with consequences. The sponsor chose a phased release.
PM move | Answer a fixed demand with options and consequences, and protect quality controls.
```

```answer
? An executive demands a fixed date and fixed scope. How do you respond?
- Accept and hope | Quality or the team absorbs the risk and the failure arrives later.
- Refuse and explain agile | A lecture does not give the executive a decision.
+ I seek to understand why the date and scope matter, then show the trade off: if date and scope are fixed, cost, quality or risk must flex. I offer options such as reducing scope, phasing, adding resource or accepting stated risk, each with consequences, and recommend one. I ask for a decision and record it. For AI features I show what each option does to accuracy and controls.
= Ask why the date matters.
= Offer options with consequences.
= Protect quality and controls.
```
