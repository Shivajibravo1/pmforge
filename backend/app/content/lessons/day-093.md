## Twenty ideas, one budget

After the workshop, Lighthouse has a list of twenty AI ideas. Every director believes theirs is the most important. Victoria can fund three. She asks you for a way to choose that nobody can call unfair.

```chat
# Message, Wednesday, 9:10 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | I do not want the loudest director to win. Give me a method, and show me the ranking.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | And I want to see which ones can show value quickly.
Elena Rossi | CISO | And which ones are safe to try.
```

Use-case discovery is the structured search for places where AI can help. Feasibility scoring is the structured comparison of the candidates. Together they replace opinion with evidence, and they make it easier to say no without making enemies.

```key
Score every idea on value, feasibility and risk with the same criteria, then pick a mix: some quick wins, one meaningful bet and none that you cannot govern.
```

## Where to look

```cards
users | Frequent, repetitive work | Tasks done thousands of times with similar structure. | Rare tasks with no volume.
file | Text heavy processes | Reading, summarising and answering. | Documents of poor quality.
chart | Decisions with data | Predictions that support a decision. | Predictions nobody will act on.
clock | Bottlenecks | Where waiting costs money or customers. | Automating a step that is not the constraint.
```

## Scoring criteria

```accordion
Value | Time saved, revenue, risk reduced, customer experience. State the measure and a rough size.
Feasibility | Data available and good enough, technology proven, skills, integration effort.
Risk | Impact of errors, privacy, regulation, security, reputation.
Readiness | Sponsor, owner, process clarity and willingness to change.
Time to value | How fast you can show a measured result.
```

```ref
PMI | Standard for Portfolio Management | Scoring and prioritising ideas against strategy is portfolio practice. Use value, feasibility and risk.
```

Use your own scale, for example one to five, and agree the weights with the sponsor before scoring.

## Process

```flow
Collect | Gather ideas from workshops, tickets and interviews
Clarify | Write each as a problem, user, measure and data source
Score | Use the same criteria and a cross functional panel
Select | Pick a portfolio, not only the top scores
Test | Run a feasibility test on the chosen ones
```

## Your decision

The top scoring idea has high value but high risk. A director with strong influence wants the lowest scoring idea funded.

```decision
? How do you handle it?
- Fund the director's idea to keep the peace | The method loses credibility, and money goes to the weakest case.
- Fund only the top scoring idea | You ignore risk and may fail in public.
+ Share the scoring, ask the director to improve the case on value and feasibility, fund a small feasibility test for the high risk idea with extra oversight and controls, and keep a quick win in the portfolio | The method holds, the director has a route and risk is controlled.
= Keep the method fair and visible. Offer a route for ideas to improve, and balance the portfolio.
```

## Feasibility checks

```checklist
Is the problem clearly defined with a measure
Does the data exist, and can we use it legally
Is the quality of the data known
Do we have the skills and the platform
Who owns the process and the outcome
What happens when the system is wrong
What would we need to stop
```

```ref
NIST | AI RMF Map function | Check data, skills, risk and context before approving an idea.
```

