## "I have accepted another offer"

It is 4:40 PM on a Thursday, three weeks before the Nimbus pilot. Ahmed, the engineer who knows the order system better than anyone, asks for a private word.

```chat
# One to one, Thursday, 4:40 PM
Ahmed | Senior engineer | I have been offered a role in another company. I will give notice tomorrow. My last day is in four weeks.
Ahmed | Senior engineer | I am sorry about the timing. I am not leaving because of you.
```

This is a simulation. A key person leaving in the middle of a release is one of the most common events in delivery, and one of the most damaging when handled badly. Your first reaction will determine whether Ahmed helps you for four weeks or stops caring. You also have a risk to manage, a team to reassure and a sponsor to inform.

```key
Respond to the person first, the risk second. Then protect the work through knowledge transfer, redistributed responsibility and a plan the team believes.
```

## In the first hour

```cards
heart | Thank him | Acknowledge the decision with respect and ask how he would like to handle his last weeks. | Anger or guilt that makes him disengage.
file | Understand the situation | What does he hold that nobody else knows, and what is half finished. | Discovering the gaps after he has left.
users | Keep it contained | Agree who is told, and when, before it spreads by rumour. | The team learns from the hallway.
```

```ref
NIST | SP 800-53 personnel security family | Departures trigger access removal and handover tasks. Cite the family when security is questioned.
CIS | Controls v8, account management and access control management | Remove the leaver's access promptly and on the day it ends.
```


## Four weeks of handover

```flow
Week 1 | List knowledge, access, open work and decisions only he holds
Week 2 | Pair with a second engineer on the critical items
Week 3 | The second engineer leads, Ahmed reviews
Week 4 | Documentation reviewed, access removed, farewell
```

```arch
# Key person risk
Knowledge | Documented, Shared, Held in one head
Access | Credentials, Systems, Contacts
Plan | Backup, Handover, Hiring
~Release risk | Re-plan scope, Date, Quality
```

## What to capture

```accordion
Interfaces and configuration | Document how the order system interface works and the settings that are not obvious.
Decisions and reasons | Why things were done a certain way, and the traps already found.
Contacts | Who to call at the vendor and in operations.
Open work | What is half finished, and what is the safest way to complete it.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 knowledge management practice | Record what only that engineer knows. Prefer runbooks, diagrams and recorded walkthroughs.
```


## Your decision

Daniel suggests offering Ahmed a large retention bonus to stay until the pilot is over.

```decision
? What do you say?
- Support it, since the pilot is at risk | It may keep him for money alone, signal that leaving gets rewarded and does not fix the knowledge risk.
- Refuse any retention measure and focus on handover | Principled, but you may be ignoring a real option that also protects the project.
+ Ask Ahmed if a different arrangement would interest him, such as consulting after he leaves or a short extension, and discuss it with HR, while starting the handover plan regardless | You explore a real option without relying on it, and the plan works even if he leaves.
= Do not depend on a single remedy. Plan for departure, and explore options that respect the person and the policy.
```

## AI and key person risk

```cards
file | AI helps capture knowledge | Interview notes, transcripts and diagrams turned into documents. | It can record things wrongly or confidently.
lock | Confidential content | Do not paste credentials or customer data into a tool. | Leaks in the name of documentation.
brain | AI is not a replacement | An assistant cannot hold judgement or relationships. | False comfort that the knowledge is safe.
```

```ai

Example (illustrative) | A key engineer resigns. AI drafts documentation from interviews, and the successor reviews it with the engineer and corrects two steps.
Use AI to | Transcribe, structure and draft documentation from interviews.
Keep human | Validating the content with the person while they are still there.
Risk | Plausible documentation that is wrong or missing the tricky parts.
Control | Have the successor review the draft with the departing engineer before they leave.
Interview line | I use AI to draft documentation from interviews, and the successor reviews it with the departing engineer while they are still there.
```


```incident
# Prompt knowledge left with the engineer (illustrative)
What happened | The engineer who tuned the prompts and evaluation set for an AI assistant resigned. Documentation was thin.
Impact | The replacement could not explain why some prompts were written as they were, and a change caused a regression.
Root cause | Rationale lived in one person's head and the evaluation set had no owner.
Resolution | During handover the PM captured prompt rationale, evaluation data, access and decisions, paired a successor and added a backup owner.
PM move | Capture AI knowledge in handover and name a backup owner for critical assets.
```

```answer
? A key engineer resigns mid release. What do you do?
- Panic and counter offer | A counter offer does not remove the risk.
- Ignore it until the last week | Knowledge walks out the door.
+ I thank the person, agree a handover plan and capture what only they know: decisions, access, runbooks and, for AI, prompt rationale and evaluation data. I pair a successor, name a backup, reassess the plan and risks, and tell the sponsor early with options. I support the team morale.
= Handover plan first.
= Capture tacit knowledge.
= Tell the sponsor early.
```
