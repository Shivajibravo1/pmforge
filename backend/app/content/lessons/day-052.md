## Nobody reports to you

Nimbus needs one thing from seven people who do not work for you, by Friday. Rahul reports to the head of engineering. Priya reports to operations. Elena reports to the CISO's own line. Marcus reports to the business. You report to the programme office. None of them has to do what you ask.

```chat
# Project channel, Monday, 8:45 AM
Elena Rossi | CISO | My team is committed to two other launches this month. I will see what I can do.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | I can release two people for training if my manager agrees.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | I have a boss who gives me different priorities every week.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | I will do what I can, but I report to the business, not to you.
```

This is the normal situation for IT project managers. You are accountable for results, and the people who produce them belong to other leaders. Formal power is almost never enough. What you have in its place is influence, and influence can be built deliberately.

```key
Without authority, you lead through clarity, credibility and relationships. Be clear about the goal, be reliable, make it easy for people to help you and make their help visible.
```

## Sources of influence

```cards
target | Clear purpose | People help when they understand why it matters and what their part is. | Asks that sound like orders or like favours.
check | Credibility | Do what you say, tell the truth early and know your facts. | One missed promise costs ten kept ones.
users | Relationships | Know what each person cares about, before you need something. | Only calling when there is a problem.
flag | Sponsor backing | Use the sponsor for decisions that need authority, and not as a stick. | Escalating everything, which devalues it.
```

## Making it easy to say yes

```flow
Be specific | What, by when, and how much effort
Explain why | The outcome and who benefits
Offer something | Recognition, information or a swap
Agree in writing | A short message with the commitment
Follow up | Check in before the date, not after
```

## What not to do

```accordion
Chasing with emails | Frequent nudges build resentment. Use a shared board and short, scheduled check-ins.
Copying their boss | Escalating every delay damages the relationship. Escalate when you have tried to resolve it directly first.
Taking over the work | You become the bottleneck and the team stops owning it.
Vague asks | "Can you help with testing?" gets a vague answer. "Can you review these twelve cases by Thursday?" gets a yes or a no.
```

## Your decision

Rahul says his boss has changed his priorities again. The assistant work will slip by a week.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Complain to Rahul's boss immediately | You bypass Rahul, and the relationship becomes a conflict about authority.
- Accept the slip without comment | A week becomes two, and no one decides if the trade is right.
+ Ask Rahul what he needs, then request a short conversation with his boss and Daniel to agree the priority, bringing the impact on the launch and options | The right people decide the priority with the facts, and Rahul sees you as an ally.
= Work with the person first, then use the sponsor to settle priorities between line managers.
```

## AI and influence

```cards
file | AI drafts the ask | A clear message with a date and a reason. | Sounds generic and cold if you do not edit it.
search | AI prepares for the conversation | Predicts objections. | It does not know the person.
users | AI and trust | People can tell when a message was not written by you. | Eroded trust when personal messages feel automated.
```

```ai
Shift | AI makes it easy to write persuasive messages, so trust becomes the differentiator.
Example (illustrative) | A PM needs a security lead's time. She prepares with AI, writes the request herself, explains the impact and offers to meet.
Use AI to | Sharpen a request and prepare for a conversation.
Keep human | Sending it in your own voice and holding the relationship.
Risk | Handing the relationship to a tool, which people can tell.
Control | Use AI for preparation, write the final message yourself and follow up in person.
Interview line | I use AI to prepare and I send the message in my own voice. Influence rests on trust.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Influences by evidence and demos, and by saying what is not yet known. | Overpromises to win support.
users | Sponsor | Needs a short, honest picture and a clear ask. | Gets a long technical update.
shield | AI security consultant | Influences through risk framed as business impact. | Blocks with jargon.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Carries customer voice into decisions. | Heard only in escalations.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Shows results from automated tests. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Results stay in a tool.
```

```incident
# AI summary used to lobby, accuracy unchecked (illustrative)
What happened | A PM used an AI summary of stakeholder feedback to argue for a change. The summary overstated support from one group.
Impact | A senior stakeholder checked and challenged it, and trust in the PM dropped.
Root cause | The summary was not checked against the source before use in a persuasion setting.
Resolution | The PM apologised, shared the source, restated the case with checked facts and set a habit of verifying before sharing.
PM move | Verify AI summaries before using them to influence anyone.
```

## Check your understanding

```quiz
? A team member's manager keeps changing their priorities. What is the best first step?
- Escalate to the CEO
+ Speak to the person, then arrange a conversation with their manager and the sponsor with the facts and options
- Ignore it and hope it passes
= Settle priorities with the people who own them, using facts and options.
```

```comm
# Get a favour from someone who does not report to you
Audience | A senior engineer in another department
Situation | You need two days of their time and have no authority over them.
Task | Write the request so that it explains the benefit and respects their workload.
Good looks like | Names the outcome and why it matters, states the time needed and the latest date, offers something in return, and makes saying no easy. Respectful and direct.
```

```answer
? How do you lead a project when nobody reports to you?
- Rely on the project title | Authority from a title is weak.
- Escalate every disagreement | It spends goodwill and speed.
+ I build influence from clarity, trust and reciprocity. I map stakeholders by interest and power, learn what each needs, make it easy to say yes with a clear ask and options, deliver on small commitments and keep people informed. I escalate only with facts and after trying to resolve.
= Say stakeholder map.
= Say make yes easy.
= Reserve escalation.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
Authority is rarely enough, influence is built
Clarity, credibility and relationships
Make asks specific, with a date and a reason
Escalate priorities to those who can decide, after talking to the person
Do not outsource trust to a tool
```
