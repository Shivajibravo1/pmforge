## The week after go live

Lighthouse is live. The project team is celebrating, and the next morning the first wave of real users arrives. By ten o'clock the service desk has forty calls about a login problem that never appeared in testing.

```chat
# Service desk channel, Tuesday, 10:05 AM
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | Forty calls already. Users cannot log in with their old passwords.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | The migration reset a flag for users who had not logged in for ninety days.
Operations lead | Operations | We did not know this could happen. It was not in the runbook.
```

Transition is the period where a service moves from project to operations. Hypercare is the short, intensive support period after go live, where the project team remains close to the service and fixes problems quickly. Without hypercare, early problems fall between teams, and trust in the new service is damaged in the first week.

```key
Hypercare protects the first impression. It combines extra support, fast fixes, close monitoring and a planned exit once the service is stable.
```

## From project to operations

```flow
Prepare | Acceptance criteria, training, runbooks
Go live | Cutover and first users
Hypercare | Heightened support and monitoring, fast fixes
Stabilise | Fewer incidents, stable measures
Exit | Formal handover, project team released
```

## What hypercare includes

```cards
users | Extra support | A larger team on hand, staffed for peak hours. | Skeleton cover on day one.
eye | Close monitoring | Dashboards and alerts for health and business measures. | Checking only when users complain.
rocket | Fast fixes | A streamlined path for urgent changes. | Normal queues for critical defects.
chat | Daily briefings | Short stand ups with support, operations and developers. | Information stuck in separate teams.
book | Knowledge capture | Record new issues and answers as they appear. | The same question answered ten times.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 incident management and knowledge management practices | Hypercare is a project term. In ITIL terms it is intensified support with fast access to knowledge.
```


## Length and exit criteria

```timeline
Day 0 | Go live, full team on the bridge | warn
Week 1 | Daily review of issues and fixes | warn
Week 2 | Stable pattern, issues falling | ok
Week 4 | Exit criteria checked | ok
Week 5 | Formal handover and lessons learned | ok
```

Agree the length and exit criteria in advance. A typical hypercare is a few weeks, depending on size and risk.

## Exit criteria

```checklist
No open critical or high incidents
Incident volume falling and within forecast
Service levels met for an agreed period
Support team confident, trained and using runbooks
Known issues documented with owners
Monitoring and alerts tuned
Business owner signs off
```

```ref
SRE | Service level objectives as exit criteria | Exit hypercare when SLOs are met for an agreed period. State the period.
```


## Your decision

At the end of the planned hypercare, the exit criteria are almost met. The sponsor wants the project team released to the next programme.

```decision
? What do you recommend?
- Release the team as planned, to meet the next programme's start | Open issues fall to operations with no context, and the service suffers.
- Extend hypercare indefinitely | The team cannot move on, and operations never takes ownership.
+ Extend by one to two weeks with a short list of named exit items, reduce the team to those needed, set a firm date and agree with operations on who takes each remaining item | The risk is managed, the team is released in stages and ownership is clear.
= Extend by facts with a firm end. Hand over the open items to named owners.
```

## Lessons from day one

```cards
search | Test real data | Real users hold states test data misses. | Surprises on day one.
book | Update the runbook | Every new issue should lead to a new entry. | The same incident repeating.
heart | Thank the support team | They carry the first impression. | A forgotten team.
```
