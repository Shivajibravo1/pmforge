## 2:17 AM: the bridge is open

Your phone rings. The Lighthouse portal is down for all customers. Monitoring shows the order database is not responding. The bridge call has twelve people on it, and no one is leading it.

This is a simulation. You are the incident manager. Your job is not to fix the system. Your job is to organise the people who can.

```chat
# P1 bridge, Saturday, 2:17 AM
Operations lead | Operations | The database is unresponsive. I restarted it once. It came back for two minutes and went down again.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | A scheduled job started at two. I think it locked the order table.
Network engineer | Infrastructure | I see no network problem.
Support analyst | Service desk | Customers are calling. What do I tell them?
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I have just been woken up. What is happening?
```

```key
The incident manager coordinates and decides. Others investigate and act. Keep a single voice, a single timeline and a regular rhythm of updates.
```

## Roles on the bridge

```cards
user | Incident manager | Runs the call, sets priorities, makes decisions, keeps time. | Trying to fix it personally.
tool | Technical lead | Directs the investigation and proposes actions. | Several people changing things at once.
pencil | Scribe | Records the timeline, decisions and actions. | Memory based reports afterwards.
chat | Communications lead | Updates customers, staff and executives. | Experts interrupted for updates.
```

```ref
SRE | Incident command roles | Incident commander, communications lead and operations lead. Name them and say who talks to whom.
ITIL | ITIL 4 incident management, major incident | A major incident has a separate fast route with an owner and regular updates.
```


## First ten minutes

```flow
Declare | Confirm severity and start the clock
Assign | Name the incident manager, technical lead, scribe and comms
Stabilise | Reduce impact first, with any safe step
Hypothesise | List possible causes and test one at a time
Communicate | First update to support, sponsor and customers
Cadence | Agree update times, such as every thirty minutes
```

```ref
NIST | SP 800-61 computer security incident handling guide | If the incident is security related, follow the preparation, detection, containment, eradication and recovery flow.
```


## Your decision

Rahul wants to kill the scheduled job and restart the database. Operations worries that killing the job mid run could corrupt data.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Let Rahul kill the job at once, since the system is down | The risk of corruption is real, and nobody has checked.
- Wait for certainty before any action | Customers stay down while the team debates.
+ Ask operations how the job behaves on interruption and whether a recent backup exists, confirm the answers within five minutes, then approve the action with a named person, record the decision and prepare a restore option in parallel | You act quickly with eyes open, and keep a fallback.
= Take the fast action with a short, explicit risk check and a fallback ready.
```

## What to say and to whom

```cards
headset | Support | A short script: the service is affected, we are working, next update at a stated time. | Guessing the cause or the time.
mail | Customers | Honest, brief, no blame, no promise of a fix time you cannot meet. | Silence or technical jargon.
user | Sponsor and executives | Impact, what is being done, decisions needed and next update time. | Long technical threads.
```

## After the incident

```accordion

Actions | Specific fixes with owners and dates, such as moving the job and adding a lock alert.
```

## AI incident variant

```cards
brain | Different signals | No outage, but wrong or harmful answers reported by users. | Waiting for technical alerts.
flag | First move | Switch the feature to human handling or the previous version, then investigate. | Debating while harm continues.
mail | Communication | Customers who received wrong answers may need direct contact. | Silence about errors that affected decisions.
```

```ai
Shift | An AI incident can be a data exposure, so legal and privacy questions begin in the first hour.
Example (illustrative) | During a P1 an assistant is found to have exposed personal data. The bridge asks legal and privacy in the first hour and decides on notification.
Use AI to | Draft the incident timeline, prepare stakeholder updates and list questions to ask in the first hour.
Keep human | Decisions on notification and public statements.
Risk | Missing a legal notification window because nobody asked what data was exposed.
Control | Include a privacy and legal check in the first hour: what data was exposed, to whom and whether notification is required. Involve the data protection officer early.
Interview line | In an AI incident I add a privacy and legal check in the first hour and involve the data protection officer early.
```

```ref
NIST | AI RMF Manage function | AI incidents need AI specific triage, such as turning a feature off and checking prompts and data.
```

```incident
# Bridge call for a wrong AI answer (illustrative)
What happened | At night an AI assistant began giving unsafe guidance. The bridge opened with no clear commander and no one knew who could switch the feature off.
Impact | The feature stayed live for an hour longer than needed.
Root cause | The incident process had no AI scenario, owner for the kill switch or communication template.
Resolution | The PM named roles, wrote an AI entry in the playbook with the switch owner, set update intervals and held a review without blame.
PM move | Name the commander, scribe and kill switch owner before an AI incident.
```

```answer
? You join a P1 bridge call at 2 AM. What do you do in the first ten minutes?
- Start debugging | It is not the PM role.
- Wait for instructions | Someone must coordinate.
+ I confirm roles: commander, communications, scribe and technical leads. I get the facts on impact and start time, agree the first actions and update interval, and start the timeline. I tell stakeholders early with facts and next update time. After recovery I run a blameless review. For AI I check for a kill switch decision and exposure.

= Timeline and review.
```
