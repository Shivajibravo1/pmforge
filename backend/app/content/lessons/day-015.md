## "Can we also add..."

Week four of Nimbus. The charter is signed. Then the requests start arriving.

```chat
# Project channel, Tuesday, 11:20 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Can customers also download their invoices from the portal? Should be easy.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | Can staff see what the customer sees, so we can help them over the phone?
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | And the assistant should answer in three languages, right?
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Each of these sounds small. Together they are a different project.
```

Scope is not what is written in the charter. It is what each person believes is included. Without a way to break the work into pieces everyone can see, each "easy" request is accepted on trust and the project grows by a quarter before anyone notices.

```key
Scope is the work required to deliver what was promised, and only that work. The work breakdown structure, or WBS, makes it visible.
```

## The work breakdown structure

A WBS breaks the project into smaller and smaller deliverables until each piece can be estimated, assigned and checked. The rule that matters is the 100 percent rule: the pieces at every level add up to all of the work, with nothing missing and nothing extra.

```arch
# Nimbus WBS, first two levels
1 Portal | Sign in, Order tracking, Notifications
2 Assistant | Order questions, Human handover, Evaluation
3 Data | Order history load, Reconciliation
4 Platform | Environments, Monitoring, Deployment pipeline
~5 Security and governance | Penetration test, Access control, Approvals
```

Notice that the WBS lists deliverables, such as "Order history load", not activities, such as "meet with Rahul". Activities come later, when you plan the schedule.

```cards
layers | Work package | The smallest unit you plan, estimate and assign to one owner. | Too big and you cannot estimate it, too small and you drown in admin.
eye | Scope baseline | The approved scope, WBS and WBS dictionary together. | Changes are argued from memory.
alert | Scope creep | Small unapproved additions that each seem harmless. | The project grows 25 percent and nobody agreed.
```

```ref
PMI | Practice Standard for Work Breakdown Structures, the 100 percent rule | The WBS must include all the work and only the work in scope. A strong interview line on scope control.
```


## What goes wrong without it

```timeline
Week 4 | Invoice download added "because it is easy" | warn
Week 6 | Three languages added to the assistant, test effort not updated | warn
Week 8 | Staff view added for service desk, no budget change | warn
Week 11 | The team is behind and nobody can say why | bad
```

Each decision was reasonable. Together they added work that was never estimated or approved.

## Your decision

Daniel asks for invoice download. It is a good idea. It is also not in the charter.

```decision
? How do you respond to the invoice request?
- Say yes, since the sponsor asked and it sounds small | Scope grows with no estimate. The next request is harder to refuse because you set the precedent.
- Say no, because it is out of scope | Correct but unhelpful. The sponsor feels blocked, and a real need goes unrecorded.
+ Say it is a good idea, log it as a change request, size it with Rahul, and show Daniel the options: add it with more time or money, swap it for something, or schedule it for a later release | The need is captured, the cost is visible, and Daniel makes the trade-off with facts.
= Never say yes or no on the spot. Capture the request, size it and put the trade-off in front of the person who owns the budget.
```

## A WBS dictionary entry

For each work package, a short dictionary entry removes arguments later.

| Field | Example for "Order history load" |
| --- | --- |
| Description | Load the last 24 months of orders from the legacy system into the portal database |
| Acceptance | Record counts reconcile to the legacy system with every difference explained |
| Owner | Rahul Mehta |
| Depends on | Test environment ready |
| Out of scope | Orders older than 24 months |

```ref
PMI | WBS dictionary | Each work package gets a description, owner, acceptance criteria and estimate. Show you can write one.
```

```incident
# Scope grew through a series of small asks (illustrative)
What happened | A customer facing engineer agreed small additions to an AI assistant on site: another data source, another language, a new report.
Impact | The schedule and budget slipped, and the original features were late.
Root cause | There was no WBS dictionary or change control for the AI work, so additions never reached the plan.
Resolution | The team added the AI work packages to the WBS, routed requests through change control and told the customer what each addition cost.
PM move | Make all work visible in the WBS and put one route for change requests in front of everyone, including field engineers.
```

## Check your understanding

```quiz
? A WBS at level two contains deliverables that add up to about 80 percent of the project. What rule is broken?
- The critical path rule
+ The 100 percent rule: the WBS must include all of the work
- The float rule
= A WBS must cover all the work. Missing 20 percent becomes unplanned work later.
```

```comm
# Say no to scope without saying no
Audience | A business manager who asks "can we also add a loyalty feature?" two weeks before launch
Situation | The WBS is baselined and the team is at capacity.
Task | Write a reply of three sentences that keeps the relationship and routes the request into change control.
Good looks like | Thanks them for the idea, explains what adding it would displace or delay, and offers the next step such as an impact assessment by a date. Firm, warm and specific.
```

```answer
? How do you control scope on a project?
- I say no to changes | Blocking all change hurts the business and the relationship.
- I accept changes to keep people happy | Silent growth breaks budget and schedule.
+ I define scope in a WBS with a dictionary so everyone sees what is in and out. Every request goes through one change route where we assess impact on time, cost and risk, and the sponsor decides. For AI work I make data, evaluation and security tasks visible work packages so they are not hidden.
= Mention the WBS and change control by name.
= Say who decides: the sponsor or a change board.
= Add AI work packages as a modern touch.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
Scope is promised work, nothing more
A WBS lists deliverables, not activities
The 100 percent rule: nothing missing and nothing extra
Never say yes or no on the spot: log, size and show the trade-off
AI features need their own work packages
```


```mindmap
# Scope and WBS
Define | WBS, WBS dictionary, out of scope
Control | Change request, impact assessment, decision
Visible work | Data, evaluation, security, integration
Rule | 100 percent of the work, nothing outside scope
```

```flashcards
WBS | A hierarchy that breaks the total scope into smaller work packages.
The 100 percent rule | The WBS includes all the work in scope and nothing outside it.
Scope creep | Uncontrolled growth in scope without matching change in time or cost.
Change route | One process that assesses and decides every request.
```
