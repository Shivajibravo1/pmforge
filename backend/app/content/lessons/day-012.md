## The sponsor who does not want to be here

Monday morning. You are about to open the Lighthouse kickoff. In the corridor, Daniel Okafor gives you a look that says he would rather be anywhere else.

```chat
# Corridor, before the kickoff, 9:50 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Last year's CRM project cost us a fortune and went live late. Why should I believe this one is different?
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I have given you fifteen minutes in front of my directors. Do not waste them.
```

The kickoff is not a ceremony. It is the moment the room decides whether you can be trusted. A sceptical sponsor is not a problem. He is the person who will fund you or stop you, and he is asking the honest question everyone else is too polite to ask.

```key
A kickoff earns trust by answering three questions fast: why this, what is different this time, and what do you need from the people in the room.
```

## What a sceptical sponsor listens for

```cards
target | The outcome in his words | The business result, such as fewer calls to the contact centre, not the technology. | You start with architecture and he stops listening.
alert | What is different from last time | Specific changes, such as a named security gate, a demo every fortnight and a stop rule. | "We have learned our lessons" with nothing concrete.
users | What you need from him | Specific decisions and dates, not goodwill. | Vague asks that he can ignore and later blame you for.
flag | An honest risk | The one or two things that could really hurt, stated early. | Pretending there are none, which a sceptic reads as naivety.
```

## A fifteen minute shape that works

```flow
Why this matters | Outcome and cost of doing nothing, 2 minutes
What is different | Three concrete controls, 3 minutes
The plan | Phases, gates, dates, 4 minutes
Top risks | Two honest risks and owners, 3 minutes
What I need | Three decisions with dates, 3 minutes
```



## Your decision

Midway through your plan Daniel interrupts in front of his directors: "I have heard all of this before. What if you simply miss the date?"

```decision
? How do you answer?
- Promise that the date will not be missed | A promise nobody can keep. When the first slip happens, you have no credibility left.
- Say it depends on many things outside your control | True, but it sounds evasive, and the sponsor concludes you have no plan.
+ Say what would cause a slip, which two dates you watch weekly, and what you would bring him and when, with a trigger such as: if the test environment is not ready by week 4, you bring him options that week | He hears a process for early warning instead of a promise, and the room sees control.
= Do not promise outcomes you cannot control. Promise visibility and speed of escalation, with specific triggers.
```

## Handling the likely objections

```accordion
"Why not just buy a product instead?" | Say you evaluated it and give the single strongest reason, such as integration with the order system. Offer the comparison page. Do not argue, show evidence.
"This is too expensive." | Show the cost of doing nothing, such as contact centre volume, and what is fixed versus variable. Offer a phased start.
"Security will slow us down." | Show that gates are planned into the schedule, so they are not a surprise at the end.
"Who is accountable?" | Name one accountable person per decision, as in the RACI from Day 11.
```

```incident
# Demo promised more than it had proven (illustrative)
What happened | In a kickoff the team showed a polished AI demo and the sponsor assumed it was ready for production.
Impact | The sponsor set a date based on the demo, and later lost confidence when accuracy issues appeared.
Root cause | The demo was not labelled as a prototype, and nobody stated what was untested.
Resolution | The project manager reset expectations with a one page view of proven, unproven and next steps, and agreed a measured pilot.
PM move | Label demos, state what is unproven and agree evidence gates with the sponsor.
```

```answer
? How do you run a kickoff with a sceptical sponsor?
- Present the full plan and defend it | A sceptic hears defensiveness. It does not address their concerns.
- Avoid the topic and talk about the team | The sponsor still has the doubts and now trusts you less.
+ I ask for their concerns first and listen. Then I use a short structure: the outcome in their terms, the biggest risks and how we handle them, what they will see and when, and the decision I need from them. I am honest about what is unproven, including AI limits, and offer an early checkpoint so they can judge with evidence.
= Open by asking, not telling.
= Say what you will show them and when.
= Be honest about uncertainty. It builds credibility with sceptics.
```

```mindmap
# Sceptical sponsor kickoff
Listen | Concerns, history, success in their words
Show | Outcome, risks, milestones, checkpoints
Be honest | Unproven items, AI limits, dependencies
Ask | Decisions needed, support, escalation route
```
