## "Show me the night"

The Lighthouse programme is about to move the order portal to the cloud. Cutover will happen over a weekend. Victoria wants a release and cutover plan that every person involved can follow, hour by hour, with a clear point where the team decides to go back.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 10:30 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | I want to see the cutover as a timeline. Who does what, at what time, and when we decide to stop.
Operations lead | Operations | I want named owners for every step, with a phone number.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | And I want to know exactly when to tell customers.
```

A cutover plan is a minute by minute script for the transition from old to new. It is a different thing from a release plan. A release plan describes what and why. A cutover plan describes the order of actions, the people, the checks and the rollback, for the period when the system is most fragile.

```key
A cutover plan is a rehearsed script, not a document to read for the first time during the event. Every step has an owner, a duration, a check and a rollback point.
```

## Parts of the plan

```cards
calendar | Timeline | Steps in order with start times, durations and dependencies. | Optimistic durations never tested.
users | Roles and contacts | Owners, deputies, escalation contacts and bridge details. | Missing contact for a critical step.
check | Verification | A check after each major step, with expected result. | Moving on without proof.
undo | Rollback | Decision points, triggers and steps. | The point of no return passed without noticing.
chat | Communication | Who is told what, when, and by whom. | Support and customers learning from each other.
```

## A cutover timeline

```timeline
Fri 18:00 | Freeze changes, confirm backups and the team on the bridge | info
Fri 20:00 | Final data sync starts, application in maintenance mode | warn
Fri 22:00 | Data checks complete, go or no go decision 1 | warn
Sat 00:00 | Traffic switched to the new environment | warn
Sat 02:00 | Smoke tests, business checks, decision 2: commit or roll back | warn
Sat 06:00 | Point of no return, old environment retained read only | info
Sun 18:00 | Hypercare begins, old environment still available | ok
```

Times are examples. Build yours from rehearsals.

## Two rehearsals

```flow
Dry run | Walk the plan with the team, no changes
Rehearsal 1 | Do it in a copy of production, record real durations
Fix | Remove surprises, update the plan
Rehearsal 2 | Run again, measure and stop at the same decision points
Final review | Confirm owners, contacts and rollback
```

## Your decision

In rehearsal, the final data sync took ninety minutes, not the planned sixty. The window is tight.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Keep the plan and hope it is faster on the night | You carry a known risk into the event, and the window overruns.
- Cancel the cutover | You miss the date with no attempt to improve.
+ Extend the window by thirty minutes, move the decision point accordingly, inform the business and sponsor of the revised times, and look at improving the sync speed before the next rehearsal | You work with measured facts and keep the plan honest.
= Believe the rehearsal. Update the plan with measured times and tell the people who depend on it.
```

## Go and no go checks

```checklist
Backups verified and restorable
Data counts and checksums match
Core business flows pass smoke tests
Security controls active and logs flowing
Monitoring and alerts live
Support briefed and scripts ready
Rollback tested within the window
Sponsor and business owner available for decisions
```

## Risks

```rag
Timing | A | Sync duration needs a longer window
People | A | Two key engineers are the only ones who know the step
Rollback | G | Rehearsed successfully
Communication | A | Customer message awaiting approval
Dependencies | A | Vendor support window confirmed for first half only
```
