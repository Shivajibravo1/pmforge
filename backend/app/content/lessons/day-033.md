## "How much will you deliver this sprint?"

It is Sprint Planning for sprint one. The room is tense. Daniel has come to listen.

```chat
# Sprint Planning, Monday, 9:30 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | How many of these items will be done in two weeks? I need a number.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Honestly we do not know yet. We have never worked together as this team.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | I have put twenty items at the top. That is what the business needs.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | Please do not promise something that makes people work all weekend.
```

Planning asks two questions: why is this sprint valuable, and what can the team realistically do. The second one is the hard part. A first sprint is mostly learning how much a team can do, and the honest answer to Daniel is a range, with a plan to narrow it.

```key
In Sprint Planning the Developers forecast what they can deliver. The Product Owner proposes value. Nobody else tells the team how much to take on.
```

## What Sprint Planning produces

```flow
Why | A Sprint Goal that explains the value
What | Items selected from the top of the backlog
How | A plan, usually tasks, to turn them into an increment
```

```ref
Scrum | Scrum Guide, Sprint Planning topics: why, what, how | Three questions a planning session answers. Easy to state, often forgotten.
```


## Story points: relative size

Story points are a way of sizing relative to each other, not a measure of hours. A story that is twice as big as another gets about twice the points. Many teams use a Fibonacci like scale: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13.

```cards
layers | Relative, not absolute | Compare a new story to a known one, such as "about the same as the login story". | Converting points to hours and treating them as promises.
users | The team estimates | Developers size the work, often by planning poker. | A manager assigning points to look good.
clock | Velocity | The points completed per sprint, averaged over several sprints. | Comparing velocity between teams, which are not on the same scale.
```

```ref
PMI | Agile Practice Guide, relative estimation and story points | Points are team specific. Never compare velocity between teams. A common trap question.
```


## From velocity to forecast

Illustrative: over the last four sprints the team completed 21, 24, 18 and 21 points.

```stats
21 | Average velocity per sprint
18 | Lowest of the last four
24 | Highest of the last four
84 | Points left in the backlog
```

Average of 21, 24, 18, 21 is 84 divided by 4 = 21. The backlog holds 84 points, so about 84 divided by 21 = 4 sprints, with a range of 84 divided by 24 = 3.5 to 84 divided by 18 = 4.7 sprints. A forecast is a range: about four sprints, likely between three and a half and five.

## Your decision

It is sprint one. There is no velocity history. Daniel wants a commitment.

```decision
? What do you tell him?
- Give a number based on Marcus's wish list | The team is set up to fail, and the first review disappoints everyone.
- Refuse to forecast at all until there is data | Unhelpful. The sponsor needs something to plan around.
+ Say the team will pick a conservative first sprint, deliver a usable increment and learn their velocity, and that after three sprints you will give a forecast as a range with a date | Daniel gets a clear plan to a reliable number, and the team is protected from an impossible target.
= Early on, be honest about what you do not know and say when you will know it. Replace guesses with data.
```

## AI and estimation

```cards
search | AI suggests sizes | It compares a new story to similar ones you provide. | It does not know your team or your codebase.
brain | AI features add uncertainty | Evaluation and prompt tuning can take several rounds. | Treating AI stories as predictable as a form.
```

```ai
Shift | AI can suggest story points, but the commitment belongs to the people doing the work.
Example (illustrative) | An AI suggests five points for a story. The team estimates first, then compares, and discovers the AI missed a vendor dependency.
Use AI to | Prompt discussion by listing unknowns and comparing similar past items.
Keep human | The estimate and the commitment. The people who will do the work decide what they can deliver, and they own the promise.
Risk | Team members anchoring on a machine number and not thinking.
Control | Estimate first as a team, then compare with AI as a challenge, not as an answer.
Interview line | I do not let AI set the points. It prompts discussion and the team decides.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Estimates uncertain work in ranges and spikes. | A single number for a research task.
users | Product Owner | Needs forecasts as ranges, not promises. | Treats points as dates.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Flags on site work that changes the forecast. | Not part of planning.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Estimates tooling work for evaluation. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Hidden effort.
code | Traditional developers | Estimate integration work with known patterns. | Optimism about dependencies.
```

```incident
# A tool's story points became the commitment (illustrative)
What happened | A team accepted AI suggested story points and committed to the sprint without discussion.
Impact | An integration with a vendor took twice as long as the suggestion, and the sprint goal was missed.
Root cause | The suggestion did not include the vendor dependency and the team did not discuss risk.
Resolution | The team estimated first, compared with the suggestion, found the gap and agreed to use AI only as a prompt for discussion.
PM move | Keep estimation with the team and use AI to prompt questions.
```

## Check your understanding

```quiz
? Velocity in the last three sprints was 20, 25 and 15. About how many points should the team plan for next sprint?
- 30
+ About 20
- 15
= The average of 20, 25 and 15 is 20. Plan near the average, and treat it as a range, not a promise.
```

```answer
? How do you plan a sprint and forecast what the team will deliver?
- Set the target from the deadline | Working backward from a date ignores real capacity.
- Use the highest velocity ever recorded | Best case thinking leads to missed commitments.
+ The Product Owner brings an ordered backlog and a sprint goal. The Developers estimate in relative terms, check capacity and forecast from recent velocity as a range. I keep dependencies and risks visible and keep the commitment with the team. For AI work I plan evaluation and human review inside the sprint.
= Say the team forecasts.
= Use ranges from velocity.
= Include evaluation work for AI.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
Planning covers why, what and how
Story points are relative size, not hours
Velocity is an average, and teams differ
A forecast is a range with a date to review
Do not overcommit in the first sprint
```


```mindmap
# Sprint planning
Inputs | Ordered backlog, sprint goal, capacity
Estimation | Story points, relative size, discussion
Forecast | Velocity range, risks, dependencies
AI work | Evaluation, review, buffer
```

```flashcards
Story points | A relative measure of effort, complexity and uncertainty.
Velocity | The amount of work a team completes per sprint, used for forecasting as a range.
Sprint goal | The single objective that gives the sprint focus.
Who forecasts | The Developers, not the PM or the sponsor.
```
