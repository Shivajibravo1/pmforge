## "We need 20 percent back"

Thursday, 4:40 PM. Daniel calls you in. The company's quarterly numbers were weak. Every project has been told to find savings. Lighthouse is asked to cut its remaining budget by 20 percent. Illustrative figures: the remaining budget is 500,000, the target is 400,000.

```chat
# Sponsor's office, Thursday, 4:40 PM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I do not have a choice. I need the plan to work with 400,000. Same date, same scope. Make it work.
Elena Rossi | CISO | Security is not on the list of things you can trim. I need that to be clear.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | A fifth is about eight weeks of developer time. That has to come from somewhere.
```

This is a simulation of the real thing. A budget cut with unchanged scope and date is not a plan, it is a wish. Your job is not to say no and not to say yes. It is to show what a smaller budget really buys.

```key
When budget falls, one of scope, time or quality must move, or risk must rise. Show the sponsor the trade-off. Do not hide it.
```

## The four levers

```cards
layers | Reduce scope | Defer lower value features to a later release. | Cutting the wrong features and losing the business case.
clock | Extend time | A longer schedule lowers the cost of some resources, though overheads continue. | Fixed costs keep running while the date slips.
check | Lower quality | Skip tests, reviews or documentation to save effort. | The cost returns later, larger, as defects and incidents.
alert | Accept more risk | Remove contingency or checks. | An overrun you cannot absorb.
```

## Options Daniel can choose between

Prepare two or three realistic options, each with what it costs the business.

| Option | What changes | Saving | What it costs the business |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Defer features | Move three language support and the staff view to release 2 | About 15% | Service desk uses old tools for longer |
| B. Phase the launch | Launch to staff first, customers four weeks later | About 10% | Benefit starts later |
| C. Cut testing and security | Drop most of the penetration test | About 20% | Highest risk of a serious late failure |

Figures are illustrative. The point is the shape: each option shows a cost, and one of them is a trap.

## Your decision

```decision
? Daniel says "same date, same scope, 400,000". How do you respond?
- Agree and try to absorb the cut | The team works overtime, quality slides, and in month four you report the overrun you could have predicted.
- Refuse and say the project cannot continue | You look unhelpful, and the cut happens anyway, without your input.
+ Bring two or three options that each reach 400,000, state what each costs the business, recommend one, and say plainly that same scope, same date and same quality is not possible | Daniel keeps control of the decision, sees the price of each path, and trusts you more for the honesty.
= Do not absorb impossible cuts and do not refuse. Offer a short list of real options, recommend one, and make the trade-off the sponsor's decision.
```
