## Planning day

Victoria has approved a pilot of cross team planning. Next Tuesday, Lighthouse's five teams and the vendor will plan the next quarter in one room. Daniel wants a board and a dependency map ready by Monday.

```chat
# Message, Thursday, 4:30 PM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I have never seen a planning day work. If this turns into a speech, I am leaving.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | Teams need to see each other's objectives. I will bring the backlogs.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Bring the dependency list. If we do not find them in the room, we find them in week six.
```

A planning board shows, for each team and each sprint in the quarter, the objectives and the work planned. Red strings or tags connect the dependencies between teams. Done well, the board makes the plan visible and the problems obvious in one glance.

```key
A PI planning board has rows for teams, columns for sprints, and visible dependency links. Its job is to expose conflicts early, while they are still cheap to fix.
```

## What the board looks like

```arch
# Quarter planning board for Lighthouse (sprints one to six)
Portal team | Sign in, Order list, Notifications
Assistant team | Order questions, Handover to a person, Evaluation
Data team | Order feed, Reconciliation, Data quality checks
Platform team | Environments, Pipeline, Monitoring
~Milestones and gates | Security gate 1, Pilot with staff, Go live review
```

Each box is a team objective placed in the sprint where it will be delivered. Add one marker per dependency, for example "needs the order feed from the data team by sprint two".

## Running the session

```flow
Context | Leaders share outcomes and priorities, 30 minutes
Draft plans | Teams plan their sprints, 90 minutes
Map dependencies | Teams place and agree them, 60 minutes
Risks | ROAM each risk in the room, 45 minutes
Confidence vote | Fist of five on the plan, 15 minutes
```

ROAM is a way to handle risks in the room: Resolved, Owned, Accepted or Mitigated. Each risk gets a decision before people leave.

## Reading the board

```rag
Dependencies | A | Three late dependencies, owners named
Capacity | A | Platform team over booked in sprint four
Objectives | G | Each team has clear, measurable objectives
Confidence | G | Average confidence three and a half out of five, one team at two
```

A confidence vote below three is a signal to rework the plan. Do not ignore a team that says two.

## Your decision

During the session, the assistant team shows a confidence of two. Everyone else voted four.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Move on, since the average is acceptable | The assistant team carries the risk silently, and the plan fails where the biggest uncertainty lies.
- Ask them to change their vote | The vote becomes meaningless, and people stop being honest.
+ Ask the assistant team what would raise their confidence, record the answer as a risk or a dependency, and adjust the plan or assign an owner before closing | The doubts become actions, and the plan improves.
= A low vote is information. Treat it as the most valuable output of the session.
```

## The output

```checklist
Each team has objectives with a business value
Dependencies are recorded with owner and date
Risks are ROAMed
The confidence vote is recorded
Next steps and owners are written down
```
