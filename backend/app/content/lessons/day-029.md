## Buffer day: catch up and look back

There is no new Lighthouse crisis today. The project is in better shape after the steering committee, and you have a day to breathe, finish what is unfinished and look at what you have built.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 8:30 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | A quiet morning. That is unusual. What are you doing with it?
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | I would go back through the earlier lessons. There are things I would do differently now.
```

Buffer days exist because real projects have them too. A good PM uses slack to reduce risk, not to relax: to close open actions, to tidy documents and to prepare for the next phase.

```key
Use buffer time for the work that never has an owner: clearing open items, checking consistency and recording what you learned.
```

## What a month of work should leave behind

```cards
file | A consistent portfolio | Charter, RACI, plan, RAID log and status report that tell one story. | Documents that contradict each other.
brain | Your own judgement | You can say why you chose a lifecycle, a response or an option. | Memorised answers that fall apart under follow up questions.
users | Stories for interviews | Two or three situations you can describe in detail. | Generic answers with no evidence.
```

## Reflect on one decision

```decision
? You have one free hour. Which use of it helps you most for the level assessment tomorrow?
- Re read every lesson from the start | Slow and passive. You feel busy but retain little.
- Skip review and rest, since you have done the lessons | Rest matters, but gaps stay unnoticed until the assessment.
+ Pick the three topics you found hardest, reread only those and explain each aloud as if teaching a new colleague | Teaching exposes gaps quickly and gives you fresh, specific stories.
= Targeted review and explaining aloud beat rereading. Spend time where your own judgement is weakest.
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, lessons learned and knowledge management | Record what happened and what you will do differently. Keep it blame free.
ITIL | ITIL 4 continual improvement model | Use the model as a reflection template: vision, current state, target, plan, action, check, keep momentum.
```


## AI as a study partner

```cards
search | Quiz yourself | Ask an AI to question you on a topic and mark your answers. | It can accept a plausible but wrong answer.
file | Summarise your notes | Turn journals into a short study sheet. | It may drop the nuance you needed.
lock | Your employer's data | Keep real project names and numbers out of public tools. | A leak while studying.
```

```ai
Use AI to test you, then verify anything it marks wrong against the lesson or a primary source. Be wary of confident answers that sound right.
```
