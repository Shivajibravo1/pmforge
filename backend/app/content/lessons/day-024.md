## The status report nobody reads

Daniel forwards you an email. His director wrote one line: "I have no idea how Nimbus is doing. Everything is green but people look worried."

```chat
# Message, Friday, 8:50 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | My director thinks the project is fine because the report is green. Meanwhile the vendor interface is two weeks late. Why is it green?
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | I only get told about problems when they reach my team.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | The report is nine pages. I read the first one.
```

Communication fails in two directions. People either receive too little, so they are surprised, or too much, so they stop reading. A communication plan decides who needs what, how often and in what form, so each person hears what they need at the moment they need it.

```key
Report to the decision. Tell each audience what they must know, what they must decide and what you need from them, in the shortest form that works.
```

## RAG, done honestly

RAG means red, amber, green. It only works if the colours have rules everyone understands.

```rag
Green | G | On track. No decision needed from the sponsor.
Amber | A | At risk. The PM has a plan, may need a decision soon.
Red | R | Off track. Needs a sponsor decision now.
Watermelon | A | Green on the outside, red inside, which is the pattern to avoid
```

The last tile is the trap. Reporting green while the vendor is two weeks late is called watermelon reporting, and it destroys trust the moment someone sees through it.

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 guiding principle: collaborate and promote visibility | Honest RAG supports this principle. Tell the interviewer you report red early.
```

```arch
# Who hears what, and how often
Executives | Monthly summary, Decisions needed
Project board | Weekly RAG, Risks, Changes
Delivery team | Daily stand-up, Task board
Operations and users | Release notes, Training, Service updates
~Rules | Owner of each message, Single source of truth, Escalation triggers
```

## Your decision

The vendor interface is two weeks late. The date can still be held if the team works extra hours. Daniel's director is watching.

```decision
? What colour do you report for schedule?
- Green, because the date can still be held | Hidden effort and hidden risk. If the team falls short, the report was misleading.
- Red, to protect yourself | It overstates the situation and treats a manageable slip as a crisis.
+ Amber, with one line on the cause, the plan to recover, the date by which you will decide if recovery works, and what you need from the sponsor | It is honest about the risk, shows control and gives the sponsor a decision point.
= Colour reflects the risk to the commitment, not the effort being spent. Always add the cause, the plan and the date for the next decision.
```

## A one page status that works

```flow
Overall colour | One word and one sentence
Progress | What is done and what is next
Decisions needed | Who, what, by when
Top risks | Three, with owners
Next milestones | Dates and confidence
```

## AI in communication

```cards
file | AI drafts the update | It turns bullet points into a clear paragraph in minutes. | It can soften bad news into something reassuring and wrong.
search | AI summarises meetings | Useful for minutes and actions. | It may attribute a statement to the wrong person.
lock | Sensitive content | Do not paste confidential or personal material into an unapproved tool. | Leaking information through a status summary.
```

```ai
Shift | AI can write a status report in seconds, so the risk is a confident report that is wrong.
Example (illustrative) | An AI drafted status shows green for every workstream. The PM checks the evidence, finds one blocked dependency and changes it to amber.
Use AI to | Draft the report from your inputs, adapt the tone for each audience and propose a summary.
Keep human | The colour, the dates and the message. You sign it, not the assistant.
Risk | A green status copied from optimistic inputs, or sensitive detail in a draft sent to the wrong group.
Control | Check each colour and date against evidence, and read the report as the recipient would.
Interview line | I let AI draft the status, then I check every colour and date against evidence before I send it.
```

```ref
CIS | Controls v8, data protection control | Do not paste client data into AI to write a status report. Use approved tools only.
OWASP | Top 10 for LLM Applications, sensitive information disclosure | A status draft can leak confidential detail if the tool is not approved.
```

```cards

handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Reports what users say on site. | Feedback left out because it is anecdotal.
```

```incident
# A green report hid a quality problem (illustrative)
What happened | A weekly report showed green for an AI assistant because the schedule was on track. Test results showed accuracy below the agreed threshold.
Impact | The sponsor learned of the gap at the steering meeting and lost confidence in the reports.
Root cause | Status measured schedule only, and nobody had defined a quality measure in the RAG rules.
Resolution | The PM redefined RAG to include accuracy against threshold and risk status, and reported amber with a recovery plan.
PM move | Define RAG criteria with the sponsor, include a quality measure and report bad news early.
```

```comm
# Report amber honestly
Audience | The project board, which reacts badly to surprises
Situation | The schedule is amber because a vendor dependency is two weeks late, and you expect to recover one week.
Task | Write the status headline and the next two lines so the board understands the real position.
Good looks like | States amber with the reason, says what you have done and what is still uncertain, and gives the date you will update the rating. Does not hide behind green or jump to red.
```

```answer
? How do you report project status honestly to a sponsor?
- Keep everything green until it cannot be hidden | Surprises destroy trust faster than bad news.
- Send a long report with every detail | Sponsors need a decision focused summary.
+ I agree RAG criteria with the sponsor in advance, with measurable thresholds for schedule, cost, scope, risk and quality. I report on one page: overall status, what changed, top risks, decisions needed and next steps. I report amber early with a recovery plan. For AI work I include accuracy against threshold and escalations to humans.
= Say criteria are agreed in advance.
= Mention decisions needed.
= Say you report bad news early.
```

## What to remember

