## Where does the money actually go?

Finance has asked for the Nimbus budget by Friday. Daniel's instruction was one line: "Give me a number I can defend."

```chat
# Finance review, Monday, 2:00 PM
Finance partner | Finance | Your estimate is a single figure. What does it include, and what happens if you need more?
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Cloud costs will grow once customers use the assistant. We cannot know by how much.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I do not want to come back and ask for more in month six.
```

A budget that is one number cannot be defended, because nobody can tell which part is solid and which part is a guess. A defensible budget has structure: costs by type, a contingency for the risks you know about, and a reserve held by someone else for the risks you do not.

```key
The cost baseline is the approved budget by work package and time. You manage against it, and changes go through approval.
```

## Building the number

```cards
users | Labour | Internal and contractor time, from your estimates and rates. | Counting only the build and forgetting testing, management and handover.
server | Technology | Licences, cloud, tools, environments. | Forgetting recurring costs after launch.
file | Vendors | Fixed price and time and materials charges, plus change requests. | Treating a fixed price as if it cannot move.
alert | Contingency | Money for identified risks. Owned by the project manager. | Hidden inside task estimates.
```

```stats
60% | Labour (illustrative share)
20% | Technology and licences
12% | Vendors and services
8% | Contingency
```

The split above is illustrative. Your project will differ, and the shape matters more than the exact numbers.

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, cost baseline and cost estimating | The baseline is time phased budget excluding management reserve. Say it correctly.
```


## Contingency versus reserve

```flow
Task estimates | Honest, with no padding
Contingency | For known risks, held by the project manager
Cost baseline | Task estimates plus contingency
Management reserve | For unknown risks, held by the sponsor
Budget | Baseline plus management reserve
```

This separation matters. If every task has hidden padding, nobody can see what is really needed. If the contingency is visible and tied to named risks, you can explain what it is for and release it when the risk passes.

```ref
PMI | Contingency reserve and management reserve | Contingency covers known risks and sits inside the baseline. Management reserve covers unknown unknowns and sits outside it. A frequent interview question.
```


## Your decision

Finance asks you to remove the contingency to make the number "look cleaner".

```decision
? What do you do?
- Remove it and hope nothing goes wrong | The first risk that happens becomes an overrun that needs a new approval, and your credibility pays.
- Hide it inside task estimates so it does not show | Padding makes tasks look expensive, and Finance finds it later and trusts none of your figures.
+ Keep it visible, tie each part to a named risk, and explain which parts will be released if the risks pass | Finance sees a number that is structured and defensible, and the sponsor knows how much protection exists.
= Be transparent about uncertainty. A visible, justified contingency is a sign of control, not weakness.
```

## Running costs: the part people forget

```timeline
Year 0 | Build cost approved, run costs not included | warn
Month 3 | The assistant goes live, usage grows | info
Month 6 | Monthly cloud and AI service charges exceed forecast | bad
Month 9 | Sponsor asked for more money for something already built | bad
```

```cards
money | Usage based AI cost | Many AI services charge by usage, so cost grows with success. | A popular feature quietly becomes expensive.
users | Support and review | People check answers and handle exceptions. | The saving disappears into review time.
```

```ai
Shift | AI cost is not a one off. Usage fees, review time and monitoring recur every month and grow with adoption.
Example (illustrative) | A pilot costs little, then adoption triples and usage fees rise. The PM adds a run cost line, caps per user usage and alerts the sponsor at agreed thresholds.
Use AI to | Model cost scenarios at different volumes and draft the finance summary.
Keep human | The assumptions and the sign off with finance.
Risk | A baseline that stops at go live, so running cost appears later as a surprise.
Control | Include run costs for at least the first year, set usage caps and alerts, and show a range.
Interview line | AI cost is not a one off. I include usage, review time and monitoring as running costs, and I show a range because volume is uncertain.
```

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 service financial management practice | Running cost after go-live belongs in the cost model. Mention total cost of ownership.
Azure | Well-Architected Framework, cost optimization pillar | Use it to challenge cloud and AI run costs before approval.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Model usage, evaluation runs and tuning create ongoing cost. | Costs forecast from pilot volume only.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | On site deployment and support time. | Travel and time missing from the baseline.
shield | AI security consultant | Testing, reviews and monitoring recur each release. | One off budget for ongoing work.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Logging, storage and tool calls add run cost. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Log and trace storage unbudgeted.
money | Finance partner | Needs a baseline with contingency and reserve stated separately. | A number with hidden padding.
```

```incident
# The baseline left out running costs (illustrative)
What happened | A project baseline included build cost but not the first year of running costs for a cloud hosted AI feature.
Impact | The operations team asked for extra budget soon after launch and the benefit case looked weaker.
Root cause | The baseline stopped at go live and did not include usage fees, support and monitoring.
Resolution | The team added a run cost view with usage ranges, set alerts and agreed an operating budget with finance.
PM move | Include run costs and usage assumptions in the baseline, not only build costs.
```

## Check your understanding

```quiz
? Who typically holds management reserve, and what is it for?
- The project manager, for known risks
+ The sponsor or senior management, for unknown risks
- The vendor, for scope changes
= Contingency, for known risks, is held by the project manager. Management reserve, for unknown risks, sits above the project.
```

```answer
? How do you build a project budget and cost baseline?
- Add up estimates and send the total | Without contingency, assumptions and run costs, the total is fragile.
- Add a big margin to be safe | Hidden padding destroys trust with finance.
+ I build bottom up from the WBS, add contingency for known risks, and show management reserve separately for unknown risks. I state assumptions, include run costs for at least the first year, and agree the baseline with finance and the sponsor. After approval I track actuals against it and report variances early.
= Name contingency versus reserve.
= Say you include run costs.
= Say how you report variance.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
A budget has structure: labour, technology, vendors, contingency
Contingency is for known risks and is visible
Management reserve is for unknown risks and sits above the project
Include running costs after launch
Usage based AI costs grow with success
```

