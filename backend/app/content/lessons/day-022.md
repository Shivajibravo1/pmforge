## The risk nobody wrote down

Week seven of Nimbus. In the status meeting, Rahul casually mentions that the one engineer who understands the order system interface is moving to another team next month.

```chat
# Status meeting, Wednesday, 11:40 AM
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Oh, and Ahmed moves to the data platform team in four weeks. He knows the interface better than anyone.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Is that a problem?
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | It is not in the risk log. I checked last night.
```

A risk is an uncertain event that could affect the project. The moment Rahul said it, it became one. The failure was not that the risk existed. The failure was that nobody had a place, an owner and a habit for catching it.

```key
Risk management is a weekly habit, not a document. Find risks early, rate them, give each one an owner, decide a response and review them often.
```

## The RAID log

RAID stands for risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies. One log, four lists, one weekly review.

```cards
alert | Risk | Might happen. Has a probability and an impact. | Never logged, so never owned.
check | Assumption | Something you treat as true. If false, it becomes a risk. | Forgotten until it fails.
zap | Issue | Already happened and needs action now. | Treated as a risk and left waiting.
link | Dependency | Something you need from someone outside the project. | Assumed to arrive on time.
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, risk register | A risk has a cause, an event and an effect. Write it in that form and an interviewer will notice.
ISO | ISO 31000 risk management principles and process | The international reference for risk vocabulary. Handy for global clients.
```

```arch
# How a risk moves through the log
Identify | Workshops, Reviews, Lessons from past projects
Assess | Likelihood, Impact, Score
Respond | Avoid, Reduce, Transfer, Accept, Escalate
Monitor | Owner, Trigger, Review date
~RAID log | Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies in one place
```

## A simple way to rate

Score probability and impact from one to five, then multiply. Illustrative scale: 1 to 6 low, 8 to 12 medium, 15 and above high.

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Score | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Key engineer leaves before interface is built | 4 | 4 | 16 | Rahul Mehta |
| Vendor interface delayed | 3 | 5 | 15 | Vendor manager |
| Assistant gives wrong order status | 3 | 4 | 12 | Marcus Webb |
| Test data not available | 2 | 3 | 6 | Priya Nair |

## Five responses

```flow
Avoid | Change the plan so the risk cannot happen
Mitigate | Reduce the probability or the impact
Transfer | Move it to someone better placed, such as insurance or a vendor
Accept | Do nothing now and set aside contingency
Escalate | Raise it to someone with the authority to act
```

```ref
PMI | Risk responses for threats: avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept, escalate | Know all five and give an IT example of each.
NIST | SP 800-30 guide for conducting risk assessments | Use it to explain likelihood and impact in a security risk.
```


## Your decision

The engineer risk scores 16. Rahul says "we will manage".

```decision
? What do you do?
- Accept it, because the team always manages | Ahmed leaves, a two week gap appears on the critical path and nobody had a plan.
- Ask HR to stop the move | You are outside your authority and may damage a working relationship.
+ Mitigate: ask Ahmed to document the interface and pair with a second engineer for four weeks, add two days to the plan, and set a review date | The risk is smaller, has an owner and a date, and the cost is clear.
= Give every high risk an owner, a response and a date. "We will manage" is not a response.
```

## Risks that AI adds

```cards
brain | Wrong or invented answers | The assistant may give a confident wrong status. | A customer acts on it, and the company is responsible.
lock | Data leakage | Prompts and answers may expose personal data. | A privacy incident and a regulator question.
users | Over reliance | Staff trust the output without checking. | Errors pass through unchecked.
```

```ai
Shift | AI adds risks that classic registers miss: wrong answers, data leakage, over reliance and a model changing under you.
Example (illustrative) | A RAID log lists generic risks. The PM adds wrong answers, data leakage and over reliance with owners and controls such as human review and logging.
Use AI to | Brainstorm risks, suggest causes and effects and tighten the wording of entries.
Keep human | Scoring likelihood and impact, and naming an owner who accepts the risk.
Risk | A register full of generic entries with no owner and no control.
Control | Add AI specific risks from day one, each with an owner and a control such as human review, logging and monitoring.
Interview line | I add AI specific risks to the RAID log from day one: wrong answers, data leakage and over reliance, each with an owner and a control.
```

```ref
NIST | AI RMF and ISO/IEC 23894 AI risk management guidance | State that AI risk is managed inside the normal risk process, with AI specific categories.
OWASP | Top 10 for LLM Applications | Add prompt injection, data leakage and unsafe output to your RAID log when AI is in scope.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Owns risks on model quality, data and drift. | Risk logged without a measurable trigger.
shield | AI security consultant | Owns risks on prompt injection, leakage and misuse, mapped to the OWASP LLM Top 10. | Listed as security risk with no AI detail.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Sees real user behaviour and new risks first. | Field observations never reach the register.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Owns controls such as guardrails, permissions and logging. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Controls described but not owned.
users | Business owner | Accepts residual risk for wrong answers. | Nobody accepts the risk, so everybody assumes it is fine.
```

```incident
# Wrong answers logged as a low risk (illustrative)
What happened | A RAID log listed the assistant may give wrong answers as low risk with no owner or control.
Impact | After launch an incorrect policy answer reached a customer and the sponsor asked why the risk was not managed.
Root cause | The risk had no measure, no owner and no response, so it was never reviewed.
Resolution | The PM rescored it using a test set result, assigned a business owner, added human review for sensitive topics and set a weekly review.
PM move | Give each AI risk an owner, a measure, a control and a review date.
```

```quiz
- A risk
+ An issue, or a dependency that has already failed
```

```comm
# Write a risk so people can act on it
Audience | A risk owner who will see this line in the weekly review
Situation | Someone wrote "vendor might be late" in the log with no detail.
Task | Rewrite it as one clear risk statement with cause, event and effect, plus a first response.
Good looks like | Uses a cause, event, effect shape such as "Because the vendor has not confirmed staffing, delivery may slip, which would delay testing". Names an owner and a next action with a date.
```

```answer
? How do you manage risk on a project?
- I keep a risk list and review it before steering | A list that is reviewed only before steering misses fast changing risks.
- I avoid risk by saying no to changes | Avoiding all risk blocks value and is not possible.
+ I identify risks early with the team, write them clearly as cause, event and effect, score likelihood and impact, assign an owner and choose a response: avoid, reduce, transfer, accept or escalate. ## What to remember
```

```checklist
RAID: risks, assumptions, issues, dependencies
Rate probability times impact and sort the list
Responses: avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept, escalate
Every high risk has an owner, a response and a date
Review the log weekly, not at the end
```
