## Sprint one starts Monday

The Nimbus plan is approved and the hybrid model is set: fixed gates, with the portal and assistant built in two week sprints. Rahul's team has never worked this way with Elena's gates on top. On Friday afternoon you gather them for a first conversation.

```chat
# Team room, Friday, 4:00 PM
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Who decides what we build first? Daniel? Marcus? You?
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | I thought I was the product owner. Then Daniel said he was.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I fund it. I should not be writing tickets. But I want to see progress every two weeks.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | And who tells us when something changes in what we are getting?
```

Four people, four assumptions about who decides. Scrum answers this with a small number of clearly defined roles. Before your team can run a sprint, they must know who holds each one.

```key
Scrum is a lightweight framework in which a small team delivers a usable increment every sprint, and inspects and adapts after each one. It defines three accountabilities, five events and three artifacts.
```

## The three accountabilities

```cards
flag | Product Owner | Accountable for maximising the value of the product and for managing the Product Backlog. One person, not a committee. | A committee as owner, so priorities flip every week.
users | Developers | The people who do the work of creating the increment, whatever their job titles. | Developers who wait to be told what to do.
workflow | Scrum Master | Accountable for the Scrum framework and for the team's effectiveness. Coaches, removes obstacles and does not manage the people. | A Scrum Master used as a secretary or a project manager in disguise.
```

For Nimbus, Marcus becomes Product Owner. He owns the backlog and the order of its items. Daniel is a key stakeholder who gives direction through Marcus. You take on the Scrum Master accountability in the early sprints while your programme level duties continue.

```ref
Scrum | Scrum Guide, accountabilities: Scrum Master, Product Owner, Developers | Name the three accountabilities and what each owns. Interview line: the PO owns value and the backlog, Developers own the plan and quality, the Scrum Master owns the process.
```


## Five events, one rhythm

```flow
Sprint | One to four weeks, the container for everything
Sprint Planning | What can we deliver and how
Daily Scrum | Fifteen minutes to inspect progress and adapt the plan
Sprint Review | Show the increment and get feedback
Sprint Retrospective | Improve how we work
```

```arch
# Scrum in layers
People | Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers
Events | Sprint, Planning, Daily, Review, Retrospective
Artifacts | Product backlog, Sprint backlog, Increment
~Commitments | Product goal, Sprint goal, Definition of done
```

## Three artifacts, three commitments

| Artifact | Its commitment | In plain words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product Backlog | Product Goal | Everything that might be built, ordered by value |
| Sprint Backlog | Sprint Goal | What the team selected for this sprint and the plan to deliver it |
| Increment | Definition of Done | A usable, finished slice that meets the shared standard |

```note
Terms follow the 2020 Scrum Guide. Check the current guide at scrumguides.org if you need exact wording for an exam or contract.
```

```ref
Scrum | Scrum Guide, artifacts and commitments: Product Backlog and Product Goal, Sprint Backlog and Sprint Goal, Increment and Definition of Done | Pair each artifact with its commitment. It is a clean way to show you know Scrum beyond the words.
```


## Your decision

Daniel says: "I want to approve each sprint's content myself, and I want the team to report to you."

```decision
? How do you respond?
- Agree, since he is paying and you are the PM | Marcus loses authority, the team waits for approval, and the framework collapses into a weekly meeting with the sponsor.
- Refuse and tell Daniel he has no role in Scrum | Wrong and unhelpful. Stakeholders matter, and he feels pushed out.
+ Explain that Marcus owns priorities and the team owns how they build, then give Daniel a clear route: attend the Sprint Review, give input to Marcus and see progress every two weeks | Daniel's need for visibility is met, and the roles stay intact.
= Scrum does not exclude stakeholders. It gives them a defined place to influence the work, and it protects the team's focus.
```

## Scrum inside a project manager's life

```cards
alert | Role confusion | A PM who assigns tasks to developers. | Self managing teams stop owning their work.
clock | Fixed date mindset | Treating the sprint as a mini deadline for a fixed list. | Quality is cut to keep the list.
eye | Ceremony without learning | Holding events with no real inspection. | Scrum in name, not in practice.
```

```ai
Shift | Assistants now draft backlog items and summarise Scrum events. The roles and accountabilities do not change.
Example (illustrative) | A Product Owner uses AI to draft backlog items, then orders them by value herself. The AI saved time and the ordering decision stayed with the owner.
Use AI to | Draft backlog items, summarise events and suggest refinement questions.
Keep human | The Product Owner decides order. The Developers decide how much they can forecast. The Scrum Master coaches.
Risk | Handing decisions to a tool, so the team stops owning its plan.
Control | Use AI output as input to the discussion, and record who decided.
Interview line | AI saves time on drafting and summarising. In Scrum the decisions stay with the accountable roles.
```

```cards
users | Product Owner | Orders the backlog and owns the product goal, including AI features. | Treated as a requirements taker.
cpu | AI engineer | Sits in the Developers accountability and shares ownership of the forecast. | Seen as a separate research team.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Brings real customer feedback to the Product Owner and review. | Feedback bypasses the backlog.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Builds evaluation and guardrail items that belong in the sprint. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Work kept outside the Sprint Backlog.
shield | AI security consultant | Advises the team on security items and the Definition of Done. | Reviews only at the end.
```

```incident
# Scrum roles blurred on an AI product (illustrative)
What happened | A project manager set the sprint priorities for an AI assistant team while the Product Owner was in meetings. Developers took tasks from three people.
Impact | The sprint goal changed twice and the team delivered less than it forecast.
Root cause | The three accountabilities were not respected and the PM acted as a fourth decision maker.
Resolution | The PM agreed with the Product Owner on ordering, left forecasting to the Developers, and supported the Scrum Master on impediments and stakeholder reporting.
PM move | Respect the accountabilities. Work through the Product Owner for priorities and report outside the team.
```

```answer
? What is the role of a project manager when a team uses Scrum?
- Assign tasks to developers | Scrum teams self manage, and tasks are not assigned from outside.
- There is no role for a PM | Governance, budget, risk, dependencies and stakeholders still need an owner.
+ I work around the Scrum team. The Product Owner orders the backlog, the Developers forecast and the Scrum Master coaches.
```
