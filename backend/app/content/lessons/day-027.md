## The steering committee is waiting

It is week eleven. Lighthouse is red. The vendor interface is three weeks late, the penetration test cannot start, and the date is at risk. Tomorrow the steering committee meets. Daniel Okafor has asked you to present.

```chat
# Message, Wednesday, 6:30 PM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I am going to be asked why I was not told earlier. I need to hear that from you first. What is the real position?
Elena Rossi | CISO | If you plan to ask for more time, bring evidence. I will not approve a rushed test.
Finance partner | Finance | If you plan to ask for more money, show me what it buys.
```

This is a simulation. You are in the room with senior people who are not pleased. The skill is not finding words that make the red go away. It is presenting a clear position, with options and a recommendation, while keeping their trust.

```key
A steering committee does not need comfort. It needs a clear picture, a recommendation and a decision it can make in the time available.
```

## A structure that holds under pressure

```flow
State the position | Red, in one sentence with the cause
Show the impact | Date, cost and risk, in numbers
Give options | Two or three, each with a trade-off
Recommend | One, and say why
Ask | The decision, who owns it and by when
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, governance and escalation | A steering committee wants decisions, not a status read. Bring options and a recommendation.
```


## What not to do

```cards
alert | Spin | Calling red amber or hiding the cause. | Committees notice and trust collapses.
users | Blame | Pointing at the vendor or the team in public. | You look defensive and the real cause gets lost.
clock | Drowning in detail | Twenty slides of status. | The decision never gets made.
```

## Your decision

A director asks: "Why did we not hear about this three weeks ago?" The honest answer includes a mistake of yours.

```decision
? How do you answer?
- Explain that the vendor only told you last week | It may be partly true, but it sounds like blame and ignores your own part.
- Say it was reported amber in earlier updates and deflect | You are right on the document and wrong on the impact, because nobody acted.
+ Say plainly what you knew and when, say what you should have escalated sooner, and say what changes from today, such as an earlier trigger for escalation | Ownership builds trust. The committee moves from blame to the decision.
= Own the part that is yours, briefly, and move to the fix. Defensiveness costs more than the mistake.
```

## Options for Lighthouse

| Option | What changes | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Move go-live | Two weeks later, hold the budget | Benefit delayed, lowest risk |
| B. Phase the launch | Staff first, customers later | Partial benefit on time, moderate risk |
| C. Hold the date and add resource | More developers, parallel testing | Higher cost, still depends on the vendor |

Illustrative. Note that one of the options always reduces risk by changing time, one by changing scope and one by adding money. If your options are all the same lever, the committee will think you have not looked.

```incident
# Red project, optimistic steering pack (illustrative)
What happened | A red AI project presented a steering pack that stressed progress and gave the recovery date without evidence of accuracy.
Impact | The committee approved more funding and then found accuracy still below target, which damaged trust.
Root cause | The pack did not state the evidence, options or the decision needed.
Resolution | The PM returned with a clear structure: facts, root causes, options with consequences, a recommendation and the decision needed.
PM move | Bring facts, causes, options and a recommendation, and ask for a specific decision.
```

```answer
? A project is red and the steering committee wants answers. How do you present?
- Defend the plan and show progress | Hides the problem and weakens credibility.
- Blame the vendor or the team | A blame story offers no way forward.
+ I give the facts first: what is red and why, with evidence. Then root causes, three options with consequences for scope, time, cost and risk, my recommendation and the decision I need today. I name what I need from each member. For AI I show accuracy against threshold and what each option does to it.
= Facts, causes, options, recommendation, decision.
= Stay calm and specific.
= Say what you need from the committee.
```
