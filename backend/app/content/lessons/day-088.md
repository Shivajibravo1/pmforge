## "Give me the runbook and the targets"

Lighthouse operations has agreed to take over the service, on one condition: a complete runbook for the recurring tasks and a sheet that shows what they are expected to deliver. Victoria wants both before she signs the acceptance.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 9:45 AM
Operations lead | Operations | I will accept the service if I get a runbook for the ten things we do every week and targets I can be held to.
Victoria Hale | COO | And I want the targets to match what we promised customers.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | We will pair on the runbook. I want the SLO sheet to reflect real measurements.
```

This final artifact for the level combines the two skills of Days 85 and 86. A good runbook turns knowledge into repeatable action. A good SLO sheet turns promises into measurable targets. Together, they let operations accept a service with confidence, and let the business know if it is being delivered.

```key
Runbooks make operations independent of individuals. SLO sheets make quality a shared, measurable commitment. Both should be written from real data and tested by real users.
```

## The runbook for recurring tasks

```cards
repeat | Routine tasks | Daily checks, backups, patching, certificate renewal, user changes. | Tasks performed from memory.
alert | Incident procedures | First response for the most likely failures. | A generic procedure that fits nothing.
undo | Recovery | Restore from backup, fail over, roll back. | Procedures never tested.
phone | Escalation | Who to call and when. | Contacts out of date.
```

## A runbook entry template

```accordion
Title and purpose | What this procedure does and when to use it.
Trigger | The alert or condition that starts it.
Prerequisites | Access, tools and approvals needed.
Steps | Numbered actions, with commands or screens, expected results and times.
Verification | How to confirm success.
Rollback | How to undo, and when.
Owner and review date | Who maintains it and when it was last tested.
```

## Building the SLO sheet

```flow
Journeys | Select the journeys that matter most
Indicators | Choose measurements that show user experience
Targets | Set from data, not hope
Budgets | Define error budget and policy
Alerts | Alert on budget burn
Review | Monthly, with business and operations
```

## Linking SLOs to runbooks

| SLO | Alert | Runbook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Checkout within two seconds, 99 percent | Fast budget burn | Checkout slowdown procedure |
| Login success 99.5 percent | Failure rate above threshold | Identity provider check and fallback |
| Assistant correct answers 90 percent | Weekly sample below target | Switch to previous prompt, human review |
| Availability 99.9 percent | Health check failing | Failover procedure |

Targets are examples. Use measured values and agreed priorities.

## Your decision

The operations lead asks for 24 by 7 support and a thirty minute response for every alert. The budget covers business hours plus on call for P1 only.

```decision
? What do you propose?
- Promise 24 by 7 for everything, and sort out the cost later | The promise cannot be kept, and trust is damaged at the first failure.
- Offer only business hours, to stay within budget | P1 incidents at night would go unattended.
+ Propose tiered service levels: on call for P1 and P2 on critical journeys with defined response times, business hours for the rest, and present the cost of extending cover as an option to the sponsor | Cover matches business impact, and cost is a visible choice.
= Tier service levels by impact, and present extra cover as a priced option.
```

## Test before you sign

```checklist
Each runbook entry has been executed by someone who did not write it
Alerts exist for each SLO and each links to a runbook
Contacts verified by a test call
Backup restore tested
Targets compared with real data of the last month
Business owner and operations both agree to the sheet
Review dates set
```
