## "Nobody is using it"

Three months after launch, the Nimbus service desk uses the AI assistant's suggested answers on only a fifth of tickets. Agents say they do not trust it. Some have found ways to avoid it. The tool works. The adoption does not.

```chat
# Team meeting, Wednesday, 3:00 PM
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | My team thinks the assistant will take their jobs. Some are quietly ignoring it.
Agent | Service desk | It gave me a wrong answer once. I stopped trusting it.
Agent | Service desk | Nobody explained how it works or what happens to my role.
Victoria Hale | COO | We paid for the tool. Why are people not using it?
```

Technology rarely fails because it does not work. It fails because people do not change how they work. Change management is the discipline of helping people move from the old way to the new way. ADKAR is a widely used model from Prosci that describes five things an individual needs for change to succeed: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability and Reinforcement. AI adds fear about jobs, trust in outputs and questions about accountability, so change work matters even more.

```key
Adoption is a project deliverable. Plan it, measure it and fund it, with the same discipline as the technology.
```

## ADKAR

```cards
eye | Awareness | Why the change is needed and what is happening. | People hear about it from rumours.
heart | Desire | The personal motivation to support and take part. | Fear that nobody addresses.
book | Knowledge | How to change, the skills and information. | One short training session.
tool | Ability | Being able to do it in practice, with support and time. | Training with no practice or time.
star | Reinforcement | Recognition and measures that sustain the change. | Old habits that return.
```

```ref
Prosci | ADKAR model: awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement | Know the five elements and what barrier each solves.
ITIL | ITIL 4 organizational change management practice | Adoption is managed as change, not announced as a launch.
```


## Diagnose the barrier

```flow
Ask | Interview and survey: where do people stand
Locate | Which ADKAR element is weakest, for each group
Act | Targeted interventions for that element
Measure | Adoption, satisfaction and quality
Adjust | Repeat with new information
```

## Interventions by element

```accordion
Awareness gap | Clear message from leadership on purpose, scope and what will not change, repeated in several channels.
Desire gap | Address job fears honestly, involve staff in design, show how the tool removes boring work, and agree what happens to time released.
Knowledge gap | Practical training with real cases, guidelines on when to trust and when to check.
Ability gap | Time to practise, a champion network, quick help, and an easy way to report problems.
Reinforcement gap | Share wins, include use in goals where appropriate, fix issues quickly and keep improving.
```

## Your decision

Victoria proposes to make use of the assistant mandatory and to track usage per agent.

```decision
? What do you recommend?
- Mandate use and publish a usage league table | Fear and resentment grow, and people game the numbers.
- Do nothing and wait for people to adapt | Adoption stays low, and the benefit is not realised.
+ Co design the working method with a group of agents, address job concerns openly, train on real cases, create a feedback loop that visibly fixes problems, track team level adoption and quality, and ask for use with human judgement where it helps | People feel ownership, and the data is used for improvement, not blame.
= Involve the people who do the work, and measure the system, not individuals.
```

## Trust in AI outputs

```accordion
Calibrate | Teach where the tool is strong and where it is weak, with examples.
Show sources | Citations let staff verify quickly.
Keep control | Staff can edit and override, and overrides help improve the system.
Admit errors | Share what went wrong and what was fixed.
Fair outcomes | Be clear on accountability: the person who uses an output remains responsible for checking it.
```

**Risks**

```cards
alert | Overreliance | People accept answers without checking. | Errors passing through.
heart | Skill loss and morale | Fear of being replaced, or boredom. | Good people leaving.
scale | Fairness | Monitoring individuals' use of AI can be unfair. | Metrics used as punishment.
```

```ref
NIST | AI RMF characteristics of trustworthy AI | Trust grows when users can see limits, provenance and a route to a human.
```

```incident
# "Nobody is using it" (illustrative)
What happened | An AI assistant launched with strong sponsor support. Weeks later usage was low.
Impact | Benefits did not appear and the sponsor questioned the project.
Root cause | A diagnosis showed users lacked trust in the answers and managers had not changed targets or workflows.
Resolution | The PM applied ADKAR by element, fixed the weakest barrier first, added coaching, trust building with visible limits, manager expectations and adoption signals.
PM move | Diagnose which barrier is weakest before running more training.
```

```answer
? Nobody is using the AI tool we delivered. What do you do?
- Send more training | Training does not fix every barrier.
- Mandate use | It breeds workarounds and hides problems.
+ I diagnose with ADKAR: awareness, desire, knowledge, ability and reinforcement.
```
