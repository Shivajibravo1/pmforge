## "The Product Owner changed the sprint"

Sprint six of Nimbus. Midway through, Marcus, the Product Owner, asks the team to drop two stories and take on a new one from Daniel. Rahul reacts in the team channel.

```chat
# Team channel, Wednesday, 2:45 PM
Marcus Webb | Product owner | Daniel needs an invoice download for a board demo on Friday. Please swap it in.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | We are halfway through the sprint. If we swap in a new item, the goal is gone.
Marcus Webb | Product owner | The board is more important than a sprint goal.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Then the team stops trusting the sprint. This is the third time.
```

This is a simulation of one of the most common conflicts in agile delivery. Both people are partly right. The Product Owner owns value and order. The team owns how much it takes on and how it works. When the boundary blurs, trust falls quickly.

```key
During a sprint, the Sprint Goal does not change, scope may be clarified and renegotiated between the Product Owner and the Developers, and the Developers decide how much they can take. Protect the goal, and keep the conversation honest.
```

## Options for the swap

| Option | What happens | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refuse the change | Sprint goal protected, Daniel's request waits | Business need unmet |
| Swap items, no discussion | Team takes new work, drops old work | Sprint goal likely lost, trust damaged |
| Negotiate: swap equal size, protect the goal | Developers choose what to drop, if the goal survives | Some delay, clear trade-off |
| Cancel the sprint | Allowed only if the goal becomes obsolete | Rare and costly |

## Your decision

You are asked to mediate within the hour.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Side with Marcus, because the business comes first | Rahul's team feels overruled, and the conversation moves out of sight.
- Side with Rahul and tell Marcus the sprint is fixed | The business need is ignored, and Marcus loses face with Daniel.
+ Bring both to a short call, ask Marcus what is truly needed by Friday, ask Rahul what could be done for a demo without dropping the goal, and agree a trade-off: a thin invoice demo built on mock data, with the real work next sprint | Both needs are served, the goal survives, and the team sees its capacity respected.
= Facilitate a trade-off. Ask what is the real need underneath the request, and look for the smallest change that serves it.
```

## Prevent it happening again


```incident
# A mid sprint swap broke the evaluation plan (illustrative)
What happened | A Product Owner swapped a planned evaluation story for a new feature request mid sprint. The team agreed to avoid conflict.
Impact | The feature shipped untested against the evaluation set, and accuracy dropped in production.
Root cause | There was no working agreement on mid sprint changes and the trade off was not made visible.
Resolution | The Scrum Master facilitated a conversation, the team agreed a rule that a swap needs a removal of equal size and a note on risk, and the evaluation story returned.
PM move | Agree a working rule for mid sprint changes that shows cost and risk.
```

```answer
? A Product Owner changes the sprint scope and the developers push back. What do you do?
- Side with the Product Owner because they own value | Ignores capacity and risk, and the team stops trusting the process.
- Side with the developers because they do the work | The Product Owner needs authority over priorities.
+ I listen to both and make the trade off visible: what the change adds, what it displaces, the risk and the effect on the sprint goal. I help them agree a rule, such as swapping equal sized work and noting risks, and I record it in the working agreement. If it cannot be resolved I escalate the decision to the right authority. The change must be explicit, never silent.
= Describe the trade off conversation.
= Say you build a working agreement.
= Do not side with a person.
```
