## The team that never meets

A year into Lighthouse, the team has grown. Rahul is in the head office. Two developers work from home in different cities. The vendor team is overseas. Marcus travels between sites. In a retrospective, a new developer says something that stops the room.

```chat
# Retrospective, Friday, 4:30 PM
New developer | Portal team | I joined three weeks ago. I have not spoken to anyone except in tickets.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | We used to sort this over lunch.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | We cannot go back to lunch. We need a different way.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | My people feel they hear about changes last.
```

Distributed teams are normal now. They can be as effective as co-located ones, but only if the habits that happened by accident in an office are created on purpose. A good manager of a distributed team designs communication, rather than hoping for it.

```key
In a distributed team, trust, clarity and connection do not appear by accident. Design them: clear working agreements, written decisions, regular contact and fair inclusion.
```

## What the office gave for free

```cards
users | Informal contact | Hallway conversations and quick questions. | Isolation and slow answers.
eye | Awareness | Seeing who is busy or stuck. | Hidden blockers.
heart | Belonging | A sense of being part of one team. | People who feel like contractors.
file | Shared context | Overheard decisions and background. | Decisions known only to those present.
```

## How to replace it

```flow
Write it down | Decisions and context live in shared documents
Overlap | Fix daily hours when everyone is reachable
Meet with purpose | Short, structured calls, and camera policy agreed
Connect | Time for people, not only for tasks
Include | Rotate meeting times so one group is not always inconvenienced
```

## Choose the channel to match the message

| Message | Best channel |
| --- | --- |
| Quick question | Chat |
| Decision with consequences | A written note, then a short call if needed |
| Sensitive feedback | One to one call |
| Complex design | Shared document and a workshop |
| Celebration | A public channel |

## Your decision

A remote developer has gone quiet for three days. Their updates stopped.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Send a stern message asking why they are not responding | The silence may have a cause, and tone sets the relationship.
- Wait, since people are adults | A struggling person stays stuck, and a blocker stays hidden.
+ Send a friendly direct message asking how they are and if they are blocked, and offer a short call, then check in again the next day | You find out the reason, show care and unblock work if needed.
= Check in with curiosity, not suspicion. Silence in a remote team is information, not a charge.
```

## Signals that a team is struggling

```timeline
Week 1 | Fewer questions asked in the channel | warn
Week 2 | Cameras off, updates short | warn
Week 3 | Two people stop contributing in meetings | bad
Week 4 | A good developer resigns, saying nobody noticed | bad
```

## Working agreements to write down

```accordion
Core hours | The hours each day when everyone is reachable.
Response times | How fast to reply on chat, email and urgent calls.
Meeting rules | Agenda, notes, rotation of times and camera norms.
Decision record | Where decisions are written and who owns the record.
```

## AI and distributed teams

```cards
file | AI summarises threads | Helps people catch up after time away. | It may miss tone and unresolved tensions.
search | AI answers questions | A knowledge base for common questions. | An old document produces a wrong answer.
lock | Monitoring and privacy | Tools that track activity can destroy trust and break rules. | Surveillance disguised as productivity.
```

```ai
Use AI to reduce the cost of catching up, and do not use it to monitor individuals. Trust is the foundation of remote teams, and surveillance erodes it.
```
