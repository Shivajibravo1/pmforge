## The team that sleeps while you work

The vendor's development team is in a different country, six hours ahead. The data team is in the same office as you. The security reviewers work in a third location. Nobody on the project has met all the others.

```chat
# Project channel, Monday, 7:15 AM
Offshore lead | Vendor team | We shipped the fix overnight. It is in staging.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Good, but we asked for a different fix. The requirement was unclear.
Offshore lead | Vendor team | We asked a question in the ticket on Thursday. No one replied.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | So we lost a week?
```

Onsite and offshore models spread work across locations to lower cost, extend working hours and access skills. They also add handovers, time zone gaps and cultural differences. The model works when each one is planned for, and fails when the distance is treated as an invisible detail.

```key
Distance multiplies the cost of unclear requirements. Remote delivery needs clearer communication, shorter feedback loops and explicit handovers.
```

## Common models

| Model | Description | Strength | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Onsite | Everyone in the client location | Fast communication | Highest cost |
| Offshore | Delivery by a team in another country | Lower cost, scale | Handover delays, context loss |
| Nearshore | A team in a nearby time zone | Overlapping hours | Costs closer to onsite |
| Hybrid onsite and offshore | A small onsite core with an offshore team | Balance of cost and contact | Two groups with different information |

## Where it breaks

```cards
clock | Time zones | Only a few overlapping hours for conversation. | Questions wait a day for an answer.
file | Context loss | The offshore team does not hear the hallway conversations. | Work that follows the ticket and misses the intent.
users | Two tier culture | The onsite team is treated as the real team. | Offshore engineers disengage.
eye | Invisible quality | Problems found late, by the client. | Rework after every handover.
```

## Making it work

```flow
Overlap hours | Protect two to four hours each day for live conversation
Clear tickets | Context, acceptance criteria and examples in every item
Daily handover | A short written note at the end of each shift
Shared demos | The whole team sees working software
Single team | One backlog, one goal, one set of norms
```

## Your decision

The offshore team asks questions slowly and sometimes builds the wrong thing. Rahul wants to cut them out.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Remove the offshore team from design discussions | You reduce the problem and increase it, since they build without context.
- Accept the delays as the cost of the model | Delays and rework continue to waste time and money.
+ Move the offshore lead into planning calls at their best overlap time, require acceptance criteria on every ticket, introduce a daily written handover and review the first month's rework rate | You attack the cause, which is lost context, and measure whether it improves.
= Treat distance as a design problem. Add context, shorten feedback and measure the result.
```

## AI in distributed delivery

```cards
file | AI translates and summarises | Overnight notes made readable in minutes. | Subtle meaning lost in translation.
search | AI answers questions | Gives quick answers from documentation. | A confident wrong answer from outdated documents.
lock | Data across borders | Where data may be processed and viewed has legal limits. | An offshore team views data it should not see.
```

```ai
When AI helps a remote team, set rules for what data can be pasted and where it can be processed. Data residency rules do not disappear because a tool is convenient.
```
