## The change that was "just a small tweak"

Week nine of Lighthouse. A developer pushes a small change to the test environment on a Thursday afternoon, to speed up the order lookup. The next morning the service desk cannot log in to the staff tool.

```chat
# Incident channel, Friday, 8:35 AM
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | Staff login has been down since early this morning. What changed?
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Yesterday I changed a connection setting to speed up lookups. It was tiny.
Elena Rossi | CISO | Was it reviewed? Was security told?
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I do not care who did it. I want to know how we stop this happening again.
```

Most outages are caused by change, not by failure. A change that is small, well meant and unreviewed can break something far from where it was made. Change control is the habit that puts the right eyes on a change before it goes live.

```key
Change control does not exist to slow people down. It exists so that each change is understood, approved by the right people and reversible, before it touches something others rely on.
```

## Two kinds of change

```cards
layers | Project scope change | A change to what the project delivers, such as adding invoice download. | Scope grows without budget or date changing.
server | Technical or operational change | A change to a live or shared system, such as a setting, a release or a patch. | A tweak breaks another team's service.
```

## Change types in IT service management

| Type | Example | Approval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | Routine, low risk, pre approved, such as resetting a certificate | Pre approved, follows a procedure |
| Normal | Needs assessment, such as a release to staging | Reviewed, often by a change authority |
| Emergency | Urgent fix for an incident | Fast path with emergency approval and review after |

## What a change request needs

```flow
Describe | What changes, where and why
Assess | Risk, impact and who is affected
Plan | Steps, timing and test evidence
Back out | How to undo it, tested if possible
Approve | The change authority or CAB decides
Review | Did it work, what did we learn
```

A change advisory board, or CAB, is a group that reviews higher risk changes. Modern teams often shorten this, using automated checks and peer review for low risk work, and reserving a CAB for higher risk changes.

## Your decision

Next month a developer wants a fast fix before a demo. The change process takes two days. The demo is tomorrow.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Let the developer make the change quietly to save time | You repeat last Friday's pattern, with a bigger audience watching.
- Refuse and tell the sponsor the demo must be cancelled | Rigid, and it teaches people to hide changes.
+ Treat it as an expedited change: record it, get a quick review from the system owner and security, confirm the back out step, and do a short review after | The demo goes ahead, the risk is understood and the change is traceable.
= Good change control has a fast path. The aim is visibility and reversibility, not delay.
```

## What a good week looks like

```timeline
Monday | Change requested with a back out plan | info
Tuesday | Reviewed by system owner and security | ok
Wednesday | Applied in test, results recorded | ok
Thursday | Approved for release at a quiet time | ok
Friday | Post change review, no issues | ok
```

## AI and change

```cards
brain | Prompts and models are changes too | Updating a prompt or switching a model version changes behaviour. | Answers shift overnight with no record of why.
check | Test before release | Re run the evaluation set after any change. | A change that quietly lowers accuracy.
file | AI helps draft change requests | It structures the description and risks. | It will not know what else depends on the system.
```

```ai
Treat a model version, a prompt or a data source change like any other change: assess it, test it on the evaluation set, plan a back out and record it.
```
