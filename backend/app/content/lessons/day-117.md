## "You have fifteen minutes"

The board has set a date. You will present your capstone programme plan to five people: the sponsor, the COO, the CISO, finance and legal. You have fifteen minutes to present and fifteen for questions. Victoria has one piece of advice: they will remember three things, so decide what they are.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 8:40 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | Do not walk us through the document. Tell us what you want, why, what could go wrong and what you need from us.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | And do not hide the weak points. Show me you know them.
Elena Rossi | CISO | And be ready for the hard questions on security and AI risk.
```

A good capstone presentation is a decision conversation, not a tour. You are asking a group of busy people for a decision and giving them the evidence to take it. Your plan document carries the detail. Your presentation carries the argument.

```key
Open with the ask. Give the story in three parts. Show the evidence for each. Close with the decision, the next steps and the risks you are managing.
```

## The three part story

```flow
The problem | Why this matters, measured by a baseline
The plan | What you will do, in what order, with what controls
The proof | Evidence that it will work and how you will know
The ask | The decision, resources and conditions
```

## Fifteen minute structure

| Minutes | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The ask and the headline outcome |
| 2 | The problem and the baseline |
| 4 | The plan: portfolio, roadmap, delivery approach |
| 3 | Risk, security, compliance and governance |
| 2 | Budget, benefits and the stopping rule |
| 2 | People, adoption and next steps |
| 1 | Close: the decision and the first ninety days |

## Slides that work

```cards
target | One message per slide | The title states the point. | Titles that name topics, not points.
chart | Evidence in pictures | Simple charts and diagrams from your artifacts. | Dense tables on screen.
list | Few words | Speak the detail, show the structure. | Reading from the slide.
flag | Show the weak spot | Name the main risk and your response. | Hiding it and being found out.
```

## Handling questions

```accordion
Listen first | Let them finish. Repeat the question if needed.
Answer then evidence | Give your position in one sentence, then the reason.
If you do not know | Say so, and say how and when you will find out.
Hostile or loaded | Stay calm, look for the legitimate concern, address it.
Park | Offer to follow up on detail that is not for the room.
```

## Likely hard questions

```cards
coins | Finance | What if the benefits arrive later than planned, and what do we lose if we stop. | A vague answer on stopping rules.
shield | CISO | How do you know the assistant cannot be manipulated into leaking data. | Claiming total safety.
scale | Legal | Who is responsible if the assistant gives wrong advice. | Relying on a disclaimer.
user | Sponsor | Why not do this faster. | Without a trade-off argument.
```

## Your decision

The night before, you realise one number in your slides, the expected benefit, rests on an assumption you cannot fully support.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Present it with confidence, since nobody will check | If someone does, your credibility is gone for everything else.
- Remove the benefit slide entirely | You lose the case for the money.
+ Show the benefit as a range, state the assumption openly, show how the pilot will test it and make funding of the next stage depend on the measured result | Honest, defensible and consistent with how you have asked others to work.
= Present uncertainty honestly and tie funding to evidence.
```
