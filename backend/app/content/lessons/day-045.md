## Five teams, one launch

Lighthouse has grown. After the steering committee, the portal, the assistant, the data load, the platform and a vendor team all work in parallel. Each runs its own sprints. At the monthly review, Victoria asks a simple question.

```chat
# Monthly review, Thursday, 10:00 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | Five teams, each doing well in their own report. Why is the launch still uncertain?
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Teams finish their work, then discover that another team's piece does not fit.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | We plan in two week sprints. No one plans across teams.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | I need someone to see the whole picture, every quarter.
```

One agile team can steer by feel. Five teams with shared deadlines cannot. Large organisations use frameworks that add a common rhythm above the sprint. The most widely used is the Scaled Agile Framework, usually called SAFe. You do not need to be certified to work with it, but you do need to know its vocabulary and the logic behind it.

```note
SAFe is a commercial framework that changes between versions. Check the current guidance at scaledagileframework.com before you quote details in an interview or contract.
```

```key
Scaled agile adds a shared planning rhythm so that many teams can commit to common objectives, expose dependencies early and deliver together.
```

## The vocabulary

```cards
workflow | Agile Release Train (ART) | A long lived team of agile teams that plans, builds and delivers together, usually 50 to 125 people. | A train with no clear owner or too many teams.
clock | Program Increment (PI) | A fixed planning period, often eight to twelve weeks, made of several sprints. | An increment that is planned once and never adjusted.
users | PI Planning | A shared event where all teams plan the increment, expose dependencies and agree objectives. | A presentation instead of a negotiation.
flag | Release Train Engineer | The servant leader who keeps the train running and facilitates events. | A PM who assigns tasks to teams.
```

Figures such as team size and PI length are typical values in published guidance, not fixed rules.

## What PI Planning achieves

```flow
Business context | Leaders explain priorities and vision
Teams plan | Each team drafts its plan and objectives
Dependencies | Teams find what they need from others
Risks | Teams raise risks, which are discussed openly
Commitment | Teams agree objectives with a confidence vote
```

The result is a shared plan with visible dependencies and a measure of confidence. It replaces a hidden negotiation across five spreadsheets.

## Your decision

Rahul is sceptical. "Two days of planning with fifty people is a waste."

```decision
? How do you respond?
- Force PI planning, since the programme office wants it | The team attends, disengages and treats it as theatre.
- Drop the idea and continue as before | The same surprises recur, and the launch stays uncertain.
+ Pilot a lighter version first: a half day cross team planning session with a dependency board and a risk discussion, then compare how many surprises appear in the next increment | You test the value with evidence, and the team sees the benefit for itself.
= Introduce practices by showing the problem they solve. Pilot, measure and adapt, rather than impose.
```

## Common pitfalls

```timeline
Quarter 1 | Planning event held, objectives agreed | ok
Quarter 2 | Objectives treated as fixed contracts | warn
Quarter 3 | Teams hide risks to avoid blame | bad
Quarter 4 | Event becomes a ritual with no learning | bad
```

## AI at scale

```cards
brain | AI teams need a place | A data and AI team often serves several trains. | A shared team that is everyone's priority and no one's.
search | AI finds dependencies | It reads backlogs and highlights links. | It misses unwritten dependencies, such as a person's availability.
```

```ai
Use AI to propose a first dependency list from backlogs, then validate it in the room. Planning is a human negotiation, and people's commitments cannot be inferred.
```
