## The team waiting on a team

Week twenty. The assistant team has been blocked for six days. Their stories need a data feed from the data team, which is busy with a different priority.

```chat
# Stand-up of stand-ups, Tuesday, 11:00 AM
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | The assistant team is blocked. We need the order feed.
Data team lead | Data team | It is on our backlog for the sprint after next. Nobody told us it was urgent.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Why did we find this out on day six?
```

Dependencies are the most common cause of delay in multi team delivery. They are also predictable. The skill is to find them before sprint planning, and to manage them as deliberately as you manage risks.

```key
A dependency is something one team needs from another, a person, a decision or a thing, before it can finish. Unmanaged dependencies turn into late surprises.
```

## Types of dependency

```cards
users | Team to team | One team needs another's work, such as an API or a data feed. | Different priorities in different backlogs.
flag | Decision | Waiting for an approval or a design choice. | Everyone assumes someone else will decide.
server | Technical | A shared environment, component or release train. | A blocked environment stops several teams.
file | External | A vendor, regulator or supplier. | Outside your control and often late.
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, dependency types: mandatory, discretionary, internal, external | Classify each dependency to decide who can move it.
PMI | Precedence relationships: finish to start, start to start, finish to finish, start to finish | Know the four with an example.
```


## The dependency map

```arch
# Dependencies between Lighthouse teams
Experience | Portal team, Assistant team
Services | Order tracking, Sign-in service
Data | Data team, Order history load
Platform | Environment team, Release pipeline
~External | Vendor interface, Security review
```

Draw the arrows between boxes on a whiteboard, and you will see the critical dependencies. In this diagram the assistant team and the portal team both depend on the data team, which makes it a bottleneck.

## Managing them

```flow
Identify | At planning, ask each team what it needs
Record | One shared board, each with owner and date
Agree | The providing team confirms in writing
Track | Review weekly at the cross team sync
Escalate | If at risk, raise it early with options
```

```ref
SAFe | ROAM risk categories: resolved, owned, accepted, mitigated | A neat way to treat dependency risk in planning events.
```


## Your decision

The data team says the order feed cannot be delivered for six weeks. The assistant team cannot proceed without it.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Tell the assistant team to wait | Six weeks pass, the date slips, and nobody chose it.
- Pressure the data team to drop their own priorities | You create a quarrel over priorities that you cannot settle.
+ Show Daniel and the data team lead the impact on the launch, ask Daniel to settle the priority, and offer the assistant team an interim mock feed so they can proceed on the interface | The priority is decided by someone with authority, and work continues on a risk reduced basis.
= Dependency conflicts are priority conflicts. Take them to the person who can settle priorities, with the cost of each choice.
```

## Reducing dependencies

```cards
layers | Align teams to value | Organise so that a team can deliver a feature end to end. | A team per technical layer, which creates many handovers.
link | Agree interfaces early | A contract for an API lets two teams work in parallel. | The interface changes, and both teams rework.
check | Create a buffer | Plan slack where dependencies are risky. | A plan with zero float.
```

## AI and dependencies

```cards
brain | AI depends on data teams | An assistant depends on clean, timely data. | The most important dependency is the least visible.
lock | Vendor dependency | The model provider is an external dependency with its own changes. | An upgrade that alters your answers.
```

Add the AI model provider and the data pipeline to the dependency map from day one, with owners and a fallback if the provider changes terms or behaviour.
