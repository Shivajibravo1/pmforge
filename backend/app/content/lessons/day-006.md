## Case study: the Healthcare.gov launch (2013)

In October 2013 the United States government launched Healthcare.gov, the online marketplace for health insurance under the Affordable Care Act. The launch is widely used in project management teaching because the failure was visible, public and well documented in government reviews and press reporting.

```note
Details below are summarised from widely reported accounts. Treat figures as approximate and verify them against the primary reports before quoting them.
```

## What was reported

```cards
alert | Struggled from day one | Many users could not create accounts or finish enrolment, and traffic far exceeded what the system handled.
users | Split ownership | Many contractors, with a government agency acting as its own integrator. Reviews pointed to unclear ownership and weak coordination.
clock | Late change, late testing | Requirements changed late and the testing window was compressed. End-to-end testing came very late.
flag | Immovable date | A fixed political launch date was treated as immovable while scope and readiness were not.
rocket | Recovery | A small group of senior engineers set daily priorities and tracked defects visibly. The site improved substantially within weeks.
```

```flow
Fixed date | Launch date locked
Late changes | Scope moved, tests squeezed
No integrator clarity | Owners disagreed
Launch | Public failure
Recovery | Daily priorities, visible defects
```

## Questions to analyse

```accordion
What were the root causes, not the symptoms? | Symptoms are crashes and queues. Root causes sit in governance, ownership, scope control and test timing.
Which PM practices were weak or missing? | Look at integration management, scope control, testing, risk escalation and the go or no-go decision.
Who should have been able to say not ready? | Ask who had the authority and the evidence, and why the decision structure let a launch proceed anyway.
What did the recovery team do that you can reuse? | Small empowered group, daily priorities, visible defect tracking, clear decisions.
```

```quiz
? Which is a root cause rather than a symptom?
- Users could not sign up on day one
+ A fixed launch date with no authority to call a no-go
- The site was slow
= Symptoms are what users see. Root causes are the decisions and structures that made failure likely.
```
