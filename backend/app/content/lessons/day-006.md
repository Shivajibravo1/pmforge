## Case study: the Healthcare.gov launch (2013)

In October 2013 the United States government launched Healthcare.gov, the online marketplace for health insurance under the Affordable Care Act. The launch is widely used in project management teaching because the failure was visible, public and well documented in government reviews and press reporting.

Details below are summarised from widely reported accounts. Treat figures as approximate and verify them against the primary reports before quoting them.

## What was reported

- The site struggled from the first day. Many users could not create accounts or complete enrolment, and traffic far exceeded what the system handled.
- Delivery was split across many contractors, with a government agency acting as its own integrator. Reviews pointed to unclear ownership and weak coordination between parts of the system.
- Requirements changed late, and the testing window was compressed. End-to-end testing of the integrated system happened very late.
- A fixed political launch date was treated as immovable while scope and readiness were not.
- After the launch, a recovery effort brought in a small group of senior engineers, set daily priorities and tracked defects visibly. The site was substantially improved within weeks.

## Questions to analyse

1. What were the root causes, as opposed to the symptoms users saw?
2. Which core PM practices were weak or missing: integration management, scope control, testing, risk escalation, go or no-go decision?
3. Who should have been able to say "not ready", and why did that not happen?
4. What did the recovery team do differently that you can reuse?
