## Your Level 2 assessment

Thirty days ago the Lighthouse teams were arguing over roles, charts and contracts. Now Victoria wants to know if you can lead delivery across several teams and suppliers, and keep people with you while you do. She has asked for a written assessment.

```chat
# Message, Tuesday, 9:00 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | I am adding two more programmes to your remit. Show me how you would set up the delivery model, the suppliers and the teams, and what you would do when it goes wrong.
```

This assessment is written. It draws on Level 2 and asks you to show judgement. Use your capstone, make choices and defend them.

```key
A strong assessment shows trade-offs and priorities. Say what you would choose, what you would give up and what would change your mind.
```

## What the assessment looks at

```cards
workflow | Delivery models | Scrum, Kanban, hybrid and scaled approaches, chosen for a reason. | Naming frameworks with no link to the project.
chart | Measures | Flow and outcome measures used as questions, not as targets. | Metrics used to judge individuals.
file | Vendors | Contract type, SLAs, governance and exit. | Trusting the contract to manage itself.
users | Leadership | Influence without authority, conflict, distributed teams and escalation. | Technical focus only.
brain | AI awareness | How AI features are planned, governed and escalated. | Treating AI as an afterthought.
```

## A structure for your answer

```flow
Context | Your capstone and its teams
Delivery model | Choice and why
Measures | What you watch and why
Suppliers | Contract and governance
People | A conflict, a departure and escalation
AI | How an AI feature fits
Lessons | What you would change
```

## Self check

```rag
Choices | G | Each choice has a reason and a trade-off
Specifics | A | Names, numbers and dates, not generalities
People | G | At least one real conversation described
AI | A | Controls named, thresholds to agree
```

## Your decision

You have 800 words, and the topics are many.

```decision
? How do you use them?
- Cover every topic in one sentence | A list, with no evidence of judgement.
- Write only about the topic you know best | Deep but incomplete.
+ Give each area two or three sentences tied to your capstone, then use the largest share on the two trade-offs that matter most and your reasoning | Breadth and judgement show together.
= Show prioritisation. A senior person decides where depth matters.
```

## What strong answers sound like

A weak answer says "I would use agile with sprints and good communication". A strong answer says "I would run two week sprints inside quarterly gates, because the contract fixes the envelope but the assistant's accuracy is unknown, and I would track lead time and accuracy on a fixed test set. I would not use velocity as a target, because it invites inflated estimates."

## AI in the assessment

```cards
brain | Name the AI feature | State what the assistant does, what data it sees and who owns the outcome. | Describing AI as a general benefit with no owner.
check | Name the control | Test set, accuracy threshold, human handover and a kill switch. | Controls listed but never rehearsed.
alert | Name the incident path | Who is told in the first hour and who can switch it off. | Assuming the vendor will handle it.
```

```ai
A strong answer treats the AI feature like any other delivery risk: an owner, a measure, a threshold, a control and an escalation path. Say which of these you would set first and why, and say what evidence would make you pause the launch.
```

Spend two or three sentences here, tied to your capstone. Do not repeat generic statements about AI risk. Reviewers look for a specific feature, a specific threshold to be agreed and a specific person accountable for the result.
