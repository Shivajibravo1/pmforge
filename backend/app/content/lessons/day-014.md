## The pack goes to the sponsor on Friday

Two weeks of Lighthouse work is scattered across documents: the charter from Day 5, the stakeholder register and RACI from Day 11, the business case from Day 9 and the risks from Day 8. Daniel wants one pack he can read in ten minutes.

```chat
# Message, Wednesday, 5:20 PM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Friday. One document. If I have to open five files to understand where we are, I will assume you do not know either.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | The charter says scope is the portal, the RACI says Rahul owns data load, and the business case assumes a call reduction nobody owns. Do these agree?
```

Marcus has asked the right question. The risk of a consolidated pack is not that it is long. It is that the parts contradict each other. A reader who finds one contradiction stops trusting the rest.

```key
A version two document is not the first version with more words. It is the first version with every part checked against every other part.
```

## The consistency check

Before sending anything, trace each fact through the whole pack. This takes thirty minutes and prevents most embarrassing questions.

```flow
Scope statement | Matches the deliverables list
Deliverables | Each has an owner in the RACI
Milestones | Dates agree with the schedule
Benefits | Each has an owner and a baseline
Risks | Each has an owner and a review date
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, integrated change control and baselines | Scope, schedule and cost baselines must agree. A consistency check is a mini integration review.
```


## Your decision

You find that the business case promises a benefit by month 6, while the charter's go-live is month 8.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Leave it, since each document is correct on its own | The sponsor spots it in ten minutes and doubts the whole pack.
- Quietly change the business case date without telling anyone | You change a number the sponsor and finance approved, which breaks trust more than the original gap.
+ Fix the inconsistency in the pack, mark the change in a short change log, and tell Daniel and finance why the benefit date moved | The pack is consistent, the change is traceable, and the sponsor sees you manage numbers openly.
= When documents disagree, resolve it visibly. A change log is cheap, and it shows you control the baseline.
```

## Before you send

```checklist
Every date appears once in the same form everywhere
Every number traces back to a source and an owner
Every deliverable in scope has a row in the RACI
Every top risk has an owner and a review date
A change log lists what moved since version one and why
A stranger could read page one and say what you are asking for
```

Read the pack aloud once. Contradictions you cannot see on screen are often obvious when spoken.
