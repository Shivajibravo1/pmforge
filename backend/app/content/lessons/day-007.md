## Your capstone

Throughout this program you will build one real or realistic IT initiative from charter to delivery plan. Every artifact you submit becomes part of your portfolio, so the initiative you choose matters.

## Choose well

A good capstone is:

- **Realistic.** Something your organisation, or a similar one, could actually do.
- **Bounded.** Deliverable in six to twelve months by a small team.
- **Rich enough.** It touches stakeholders, vendors, security and, by Level 4, an AI component.
- **Shareable.** Described so you can publish it without exposing confidential information.

## Examples

| Initiative | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| Customer service portal with an AI assistant | Application, data, security and AI in one story |
| Migration of a finance system to the cloud | Strong data, vendor and cutover content |
| Employee identity and access modernisation | Security-heavy with many stakeholders |
| Document processing automation | Clear business case and measurable accuracy |

## Protect confidentiality

Do not use real client names, internal systems or numbers. Change names and figures. A portfolio that leaks employer information can harm you more than it helps.

## Your task

Describe your capstone in 200 to 400 words: the business problem, the proposed solution, who the sponsor and key stakeholders are, the expected benefit, and the top three risks. State which of the five project types from Day 2 it is, and which lifecycle from Day 3 you expect to use. Submit it for mentor review.
