## Your project, built all the way through

By now you have followed Nimbus for a week: triage, project types, lifecycle, environments, a charter, and a real failure. Now the story becomes yours. You will pick one real or realistic IT initiative and take it from charter to delivery plan, one artifact at a time.

```chat
# A message you will get in an interview, sooner than you think
Hiring manager | Panel interview | Tell me about a project you planned end to end. What was the problem, who were the stakeholders, and where did you use AI?
```

Most courses teach ideas and send you home with nothing to show. In ten minutes this question exposes who has a real project to describe and who does not.

```key
Everything you submit becomes part of your portfolio. By the end you can show a hiring manager a complete, believable project, not a list of certificates.
```

## What makes a good capstone

```cards
target | Realistic | Something your organisation, or a similar one, could really do. | A fantasy project that falls apart on the first question.
clock | Bounded | Deliverable in six to twelve months by a small team. | A programme so large nothing is ever finished.
layers | Rich enough | Touches stakeholders, vendors, security and, by Level 4, an AI component. | Too thin to show real judgement.
eye | Shareable | Described so you can publish it without exposing anything confidential. | Leaking employer details.
```

## Ideas that work

| Initiative | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| Customer service portal with an AI assistant | Application, data, security and AI in one story |
| Migration of a finance system to the cloud | Strong data, vendor and cutover content |
| Employee identity and access modernisation | Security-heavy with many stakeholders |
| Document processing automation | Clear business case and measurable accuracy |

```decision
? You are choosing your capstone. Which choice gives you the strongest interview story?
- A huge enterprise transformation that touches everything | It sounds impressive, but you cannot finish it, and every question exposes missing detail.
- A tiny task such as updating one document | Finished, but it shows no judgement about stakeholders, risk or trade-offs.
+ A bounded project with a sponsor, a vendor, a security gate and an AI component, small enough to complete | You can describe the problem, the people, the trade-offs and the evidence, and every interview question has an answer.
= Pick something finishable with enough texture to show judgement. Depth beats size.
```

## Where does AI show up in your capstone?

A strong capstone shows you can use AI sensibly, not just mention it. There are three realistic places it appears.

```cards
brain | AI inside the product | An assistant, a classifier or a document reader in the solution. | No clear measure of whether it works.
chart | AI in delivery | Drafting reports, summarising meetings, checking requirements. | Output is trusted without checking.
shield | AI governance | Rules for what data AI can see and who approves its use. | Rules exist on paper but nobody follows them.
```

```ai
Shift | Employers now expect project managers to use AI well, and they will ask exactly where you used it and where you did not.
Example (illustrative) | A learner pastes real project notes into a public chatbot to speed up a capstone. They then rewrite the scenario with invented names and note that AI helped with structure only.
Use AI to | Brainstorm capstone ideas, challenge your scope and draft early versions of documents.
Keep human | The choice of project, the judgement calls and your own account of what you did and why.
Risk | Using real employer data, or presenting AI generated work as entirely your own.
Control | Use invented or anonymised material, record where AI helped and be ready to explain any line in your submission.
Interview line | I used AI to draft and to challenge, and I checked every fact myself. I did not put real company data into any tool.
```

```ref
NIST | AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 | If your capstone includes AI, state which framework you would align to. Pick one and explain why.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Would define the AI parts of your capstone, such as retrieval or evaluation. | Capstone adds AI with no data or measure.
shield | AI security consultant | Would review data and access in your design. | Real employer data used in public tools.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Would deploy and tune in the customer environment. | Plan ignores on site constraints.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Would own tools, limits and logging if you use an agent. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Agent with unlimited access in a plan.
code | Traditional developers | Would build the integration. | Dependencies missing from the plan.
```

```incident
# Confidential data pasted into a public AI tool (illustrative)
What happened | A learner drafted a capstone using real project documents from their employer and pasted them into a public AI tool for help.
Impact | The documents contained confidential details, and the learner had to report the exposure internally.
Root cause | Real data was used instead of an anonymised scenario, and the tool terms were not checked.
Resolution | The learner switched to an invented scenario with no real names or figures, and followed the employer's policy on AI tool use.
PM move | Use anonymised or invented material for practice, and check policy before using any AI tool with work data.
```

## Protect your employer

```warn
Do not use real client names, internal system names or real numbers. Change names and figures. A portfolio that leaks employer information can hurt you far more than it helps.
```

```ref
CIS | Controls v8, data protection control | Before you paste anything into an AI tool, classify the data. Mention the CIS data protection control when asked how you protect client information.
OWASP | Top 10 for LLM Applications, sensitive information disclosure | Explains why confidential client detail must never go into a public AI tool. Check your employer policy and local data protection law.
```


## Before you submit

```checklist
Business problem in plain words
Proposed solution
Sponsor and key stakeholders
Expected benefit
Top three risks
Primary project type from Day 2
Lifecycle from Day 3
No confidential names or numbers
```

```comm
# Pitch your capstone in 60 seconds
Audience | A hiring manager who has seen many generic project stories
Situation | You are in an interview and asked about the project you are building during this program.
Task | Write a 60 second spoken pitch: the problem, your role, one hard decision and the result you expect.
Good looks like | Starts with the business problem, makes your own decision visible, names one trade off honestly, and ends with an outcome you can measure. Uses plain words and no buzzwords.
```

```answer
? Walk me through a project you led, end to end.
- I list every task we completed | Lacks outcome, decisions and results.
- I describe how well the team worked | Culture matters, but without a result and your role it does not persuade.
+ I set the context in one line: the problem, size and my role. Then I explain the outcome and how I measured it, the two biggest risks and what I did about them, one decision I had to make with trade offs, and what I would change next time. If AI was involved, I explain the data, owner and the control that kept it safe.
= Use your own real project. Never borrow a story.
= Use a structure: context, outcome, risks, decision, lesson.
= Do not share confidential figures or names. Describe at the level you are allowed.
```

```mindmap
# Capstone story
Context | Problem, scope, your role
Outcome | Result, measure, stakeholders
Control | Risks, decisions, trade offs
Learning | What you would change, AI lens
```

```flashcards
A good capstone | Real enough to matter, scoped to finish, and safe to share.
Protect your employer | Do not use confidential data, names or figures. Anonymise or invent.
Structure of a project story | Context, outcome, risks, decision, lesson.
AI in a capstone | State the data, owner, measure and control for the AI part.
```
