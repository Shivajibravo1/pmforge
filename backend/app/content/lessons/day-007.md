## One project, built all the way through

Most courses teach you ideas and send you home. This one does not. You will pick one real or realistic IT initiative and take it from charter to delivery plan, one artifact at a time.

```key
Everything you submit becomes part of your portfolio. By the end you can show a hiring manager a complete, believable project, not a list of certificates.
```

## What makes a good capstone

```cards
target | Realistic | Something your organisation, or a similar one, could really do.
clock | Bounded | Deliverable in six to twelve months by a small team.
layers | Rich enough | Touches stakeholders, vendors, security and, by Level 4, an AI component.
eye | Shareable | Described so you can publish it without exposing anything confidential.
```

## Ideas that work

| Initiative | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| Customer service portal with an AI assistant | Application, data, security and AI in one story |
| Migration of a finance system to the cloud | Strong data, vendor and cutover content |
| Employee identity and access modernisation | Security-heavy with many stakeholders |
| Document processing automation | Clear business case and measurable accuracy |

## Protect your employer

```warn
Do not use real client names, internal system names or real numbers. Change names and figures. A portfolio that leaks employer information can hurt you far more than it helps.
```

## Before you submit

```checklist
Business problem in plain words
Proposed solution
Sponsor and key stakeholders
Expected benefit
Top three risks
Primary project type from Day 2
Lifecycle from Day 3
No confidential names or numbers
```

## Your task

Describe your capstone in 200 to 400 words: the business problem, the proposed solution, who the sponsor and key stakeholders are, the expected benefit and the top three risks. State which of the five project types from Day 2 it is and which lifecycle from Day 3 you expect to use. Submit it for mentor review.
