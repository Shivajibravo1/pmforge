## Your project, built all the way through

By now you have followed Lighthouse for a week: triage, project types, lifecycle, environments, a charter, and a real failure. Now the story becomes yours. You will pick one real or realistic IT initiative and take it from charter to delivery plan, one artifact at a time.

```chat
# A message you will get in an interview, sooner than you think
Hiring manager | Panel interview | Tell me about a project you planned end to end. What was the problem, who were the stakeholders, and where did you use AI?
```

Most courses teach ideas and send you home with nothing to show. In ten minutes this question exposes who has a real project to describe and who does not.

```key
Everything you submit becomes part of your portfolio. By the end you can show a hiring manager a complete, believable project, not a list of certificates.
```

## What makes a good capstone

```cards
target | Realistic | Something your organisation, or a similar one, could really do. | A fantasy project that falls apart on the first question.
clock | Bounded | Deliverable in six to twelve months by a small team. | A programme so large nothing is ever finished.
layers | Rich enough | Touches stakeholders, vendors, security and, by Level 4, an AI component. | Too thin to show real judgement.
eye | Shareable | Described so you can publish it without exposing anything confidential. | Leaking employer details.
```

## Ideas that work

| Initiative | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| Customer service portal with an AI assistant | Application, data, security and AI in one story |
| Migration of a finance system to the cloud | Strong data, vendor and cutover content |
| Employee identity and access modernisation | Security-heavy with many stakeholders |
| Document processing automation | Clear business case and measurable accuracy |

```decision
? You are choosing your capstone. Which choice gives you the strongest interview story?
- A huge enterprise transformation that touches everything | It sounds impressive, but you cannot finish it, and every question exposes missing detail.
- A tiny task such as updating one document | Finished, but it shows no judgement about stakeholders, risk or trade-offs.
+ A bounded project with a sponsor, a vendor, a security gate and an AI component, small enough to complete | You can describe the problem, the people, the trade-offs and the evidence, and every interview question has an answer.
= Pick something finishable with enough texture to show judgement. Depth beats size.
```

## Where does AI show up in your capstone?

A strong capstone shows you can use AI sensibly, not just mention it. There are three realistic places it appears.

```cards
brain | AI inside the product | An assistant, a classifier or a document reader in the solution. | No clear measure of whether it works.
chart | AI in delivery | Drafting reports, summarising meetings, checking requirements. | Output is trusted without checking.
shield | AI governance | Rules for what data AI can see and who approves its use. | Rules exist on paper but nobody follows them.
```

```ai
A hiring manager will ask where you used AI and where you did not. Decide your answer now: where it helped, where you checked it, and where you refused to use it.
```

## Protect your employer

```warn
Do not use real client names, internal system names or real numbers. Change names and figures. A portfolio that leaks employer information can hurt you far more than it helps.
```

```ref
CIS | Controls v8, data protection control | Before you paste anything into an AI tool, classify the data. Mention the CIS data protection control when asked how you protect client information.
OWASP | Top 10 for LLM Applications, sensitive information disclosure | Explains why confidential client detail must never go into a public AI tool. Check your employer policy and local data protection law.
```
