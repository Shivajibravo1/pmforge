## "How long will it take?"

Daniel catches you after the steering meeting. "Rahul's team says the order tracking work is six weeks. Marcus says ten. Which is it? I need one number for the board."

```chat
# Hallway, Wednesday, 1:15 PM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | One number. I do not need a lecture.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Best case six weeks, if the order system API behaves. Realistically ten. If the vendor delays the interface, twenty.
Marcus Webb | Business analyst | I would say ten. Add some for testing.
```

Both are right. They gave different points on the same range. A single number given without its range is the most dangerous thing a project manager can report, because it turns a guess into a promise.

```key
An estimate is a range with a level of confidence, not a single number. A good PM reports the range, the assumptions and what would narrow it.
```

## Three ways to estimate

```cards
clock | Analogous | Use a similar past project as the basis, then adjust. Fast, rough, useful early. | The past project was not actually similar.
chart | Three-point | Estimate best, most likely and worst, then weight them. Shows uncertainty. | Optimism hides in the best case, so people anchor on it.
layers | Story points | Relative sizing of backlog items by the team, tracked by velocity over time. Used in agile work. | Treating points as hours and comparing teams.
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, estimating techniques: analogous, parametric, bottom-up, three-point | Know when to use each. Interview line: analogous early, bottom-up when the WBS is clear, three-point when uncertainty is high.
PMI | Agile Practice Guide, relative sizing, story points, planning poker | Story points size effort relatively, not in hours. Say it and the team velocity link.
```


## The three-point calculation

Rahul gave you three numbers: optimistic 6 weeks, most likely 10, pessimistic 20. The PERT weighted average is:

```key
Expected = (optimistic + 4 x most likely + pessimistic) divided by 6
```

```stats
6 | Optimistic (weeks)
10 | Most likely (weeks)
20 | Pessimistic (weeks)
11 | Expected (weeks)
```

Calculation: (6 + 4 x 10 + 20) divided by 6 = 66 divided by 6 = 11 weeks. A rough measure of spread is (pessimistic minus optimistic) divided by 6 = 14 divided by 6, about 2.3 weeks. So a sensible statement is "about 11 weeks, plausibly 9 to 13, with a tail risk of 20 if the vendor interface slips". The numbers are illustrative.

## Your decision

Daniel insists: "One number."

```decision
? What do you give him?
- Six weeks, since that is what the developer said at best | You have turned the best case into a commitment. Missing it will be your fault.
- Twenty weeks, so you cannot be wrong | Safe for you, but it wrecks the business case and teaches the sponsor to ignore your numbers.
+ Eleven weeks as the planning number, with a stated range of 9 to 13 and one named risk, the vendor interface, that could push it to 20 | He gets one number for the board, with honest uncertainty and a trigger to watch.
= Give the sponsor the single number he needs, but attach the range and the risk that would break it. That is both usable and honest.
```

## Where estimates go wrong

```timeline
Day 1 | Estimate given as a single optimistic number | warn
Week 3 | Hidden work found, testing and data cleansing not included | warn
Week 6 | Team works overtime to hold the date | bad
Week 9 | Quality drops, defects rise | bad
Week 12 | Date missed, trust lost | bad
```

## Estimating AI work

```cards
brain | AI work is uncertain | Nobody knows how accurate the assistant will be until it is tested on real data. | An estimate that assumes it works on the first try.
database | Data readiness | Cleaning data often takes longer than building the feature. | Data work is left out of the plan.
check | Evaluation cycles | Expect several rounds of test, fix and retest. | One round of testing in the plan.
```

```ai
Interview line: "For AI work I estimate in rounds. I budget for several evaluation cycles and I give a range, because accuracy is only known after testing on real data."
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, uncertainty and estimating under uncertainty | AI work has more unknowns. Use ranges and re-estimate after a spike.
```
