## "Who found it first?"

A researcher emails Nimbus's security inbox: a page on the customer portal reveals other customers' order numbers when a URL is changed. They have reported it responsibly and asked for an acknowledgement. The message sat unread for six days.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 8:35 AM
Elena Rossi | CISO | A researcher reported a flaw six days ago. Nobody replied. Who watches that inbox?
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | We thought it was a spam address.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | Customers have not noticed, as far as I know.
```

Vulnerability management is the continuous process of finding, ranking, fixing and verifying weaknesses. Penetration testing is a time limited exercise where authorised testers try to exploit weaknesses to show real impact. A project manager coordinates both, so that findings turn into fixes and not into a report in a drawer.

```key
Finding vulnerabilities is easy. Fixing the right ones, in time, with proof, is the work.
```

## The cycle

```flow
Discover | Scans, tests, reports from researchers and vendors
Assess | Severity, exposure, exploitability, business impact
Prioritise | Fix order and deadlines by risk
Remediate | Patch, configure, redesign or accept with approval
Verify | Retest to prove it is fixed
Report | Track trends and time to fix
```

```ref
CIS | Controls v8, continuous vulnerability management | Scan, prioritise, remediate, repeat. Name the cycle.
NIST | SP 800-40 enterprise patch management planning | A reference for patch planning, maintenance windows and risk acceptance.
```


## Ranking findings

```cards
chart | Severity score | A standard scoring system such as CVSS gives a base rating. | Treating the score as the whole story.
globe | Exposure | Internet facing systems are more urgent. | Fixing internal low risk items first.
zap | Exploitation in the wild | Known active exploitation raises urgency. | Missing public advisories.
database | Data and business impact | What the system holds and does. | Ignoring the crown jewels.
```

Check current guidance from sources such as CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list for exploitation status.

```ref
FIRST | Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS) | A severity score, not a risk score. Add exploit status and asset value to rank findings.
CISA | Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog | A vulnerability listed there is being exploited. Treat it as urgent.
```

## Typical time targets

```accordion
Critical | Fix or mitigate within days, with an emergency path.
High | Within a few weeks.
Medium | Within a planned cycle, such as a quarter.
Low | Backlog, or accept with documented reasons.
```

Targets differ between organisations. Agree yours in policy and apply it consistently.

## Penetration test coordination

```timeline
Weeks before | Define scope, rules of engagement, legal approval and dates | info
Week before | Confirm contacts, environments and emergency stop process | ok
Test week | Testers work, daily check in, critical findings reported immediately | warn
Week after | Report, findings review, ownership and dates | ok
Month later | Retest of fixed items and closure report | ok
```

## Your decision

The penetration test report has twenty findings, three of them critical. Launch is in two weeks, and the team says all three cannot be fixed in time.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Hide the report from the sponsor to avoid panic | You carry a hidden risk into launch, and the decision belongs to the sponsor and the CISO.
- Delay the launch until everything is fixed | Perhaps right for one critical issue, but wrong as a reflex for all twenty.
+ Present the three critical findings with options for each: fix, mitigate with a compensating control, limit exposure or delay, agree decisions with Elena and Daniel, record any acceptance with an owner and expiry date, and retest before launch | The decision is informed, documented and time bound.
= Bring risk to the people who own it, with options. Record acceptances with owner and expiry.
```

## Responsible disclosure

```accordion
Publish a policy | Tell researchers how to report, what is in scope and what they can expect.
Monitor the channel | Assign an owner, and acknowledge within a short, stated time.
Triage | Reproduce, rank and assign to a team.
Communicate | Keep the researcher informed and agree timing for any public disclosure.
```

```ref
ISO | ISO/IEC 29147 vulnerability disclosure | Reference for how to receive and handle reports from outside researchers.
```

## AI and vulnerability management

```cards
brain | AI systems have new weaknesses | Prompt injection, data leakage and unsafe tool use. | Standard scans that miss them.
search | AI red teaming | Structured adversarial testing of models and prompts. | A one time test of a system that keeps changing.
file | AI triage | Helps summarise and cluster findings. | Wrong prioritisation with confident wording.
```

```ai
Shift | Penetration tests must include AI features and their new attack paths.
Example (illustrative) | A penetration test covers the web app only. The PM expands scope to the assistant and the tester finds data exposure through a tool.
Use AI to | Draft scoping notes and summarise findings.
Keep human | Scoping with the testers and accepting fixes.
Risk | A test that covers servers and ignores the assistant.
Control | Ask for tests of prompt injection, data exfiltration through tools and denial of wallet. Repeat after significant model or prompt changes.
Interview line | I include AI features in pen test scope, including prompt injection, exfiltration through tools and denial of wallet, and I repeat after major changes.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Tracks model, library and data pipeline vulnerabilities. | Only application code scanned.
shield | AI security consultant | Ranks findings and coordinates testing, including AI red teaming. | Test scope excludes the AI.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Automates scans and re tests. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Re tests by request.
code | Traditional developers | Fix findings within agreed time targets. | Fixes queued behind features.
users | Risk owner | Accepts or defers risk in writing. | Silent acceptance.
```

```incident
# Pen test excluded the AI component (illustrative)
What happened | A pen test covered the web application and network. The AI assistant and its tools were out of scope.
Impact | A later review found the assistant could be steered into exposing internal data.
Root cause | Scope was set from old templates and did not include AI features.
Resolution | The PM widened the next test scope to AI behaviour and tool access, added re tests and tracked findings with owners and dates.
PM move | Include AI components in the pen test scope.
```

```comm
# Coordinate a penetration test
Audience | The testing vendor and the system owner
Situation | The test must start Monday without disrupting production.
Task | Write the briefing message with scope, timing, contacts and stop rules.
Good looks like | States what is in and out of scope, the dates and times, who to call and when to stop testing. Removes assumptions.
```

```answer
? How do you coordinate vulnerability management and a pen test?
- Run the test and file the report | Findings need ownership and re tests.
- Fix everything at once | Ranking is needed.
+ I agree scope and rules of engagement with the tester, plan windows, rank findings by severity and exposure, set fix time targets by rank, assign owners, track to closure and re test. I report programme health to leadership and record accepted risks. For AI I include model behaviour and tool access in scope.
= Scope and rules.
= Rank and time targets.
= Re test and close.
```
