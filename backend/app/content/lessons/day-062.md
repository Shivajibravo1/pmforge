## "Just lift it and shift it"

The Nimbus programme owns eleven applications on old servers in a data centre whose lease ends in nine months. The infrastructure lead says move everything as is. The architect says rebuild everything. Victoria wants a number and a date.

```chat
# Planning meeting, Tuesday, 11:00 AM
Infrastructure lead | Operations | Lift and shift all eleven. It is the fastest and cheapest.
Architect | Engineering | If we lift and shift, we pay cloud prices for data centre design. Rebuild the important ones.
Victoria Hale | COO | I do not care about the term. I care about cost, risk and the date the lease ends.
```

One strategy for eleven applications is almost always wrong. The 7 Rs give you a menu. Each application is assessed on its value, condition and constraints, and then assigned the lowest effort option that meets the need.

```key
Migration is a portfolio decision. Assign a strategy per application, based on business value, technical condition, risk and the deadline.
```

## The 7 Rs

```cards
archive | Retire | Switch off applications nobody needs. | Hidden users discovered after switch off.
pause | Retain | Leave it where it is, for now. | Retained systems that never move.
server | Rehost | Lift and shift to virtual machines with few changes. | Cloud costs of an unoptimised design.
wrench | Replatform | Small changes for managed services, such as a managed database. | Scope creep into redesign.
refresh | Repurchase | Replace with a SaaS product. | Data migration and process change.
code | Refactor | Rebuild to be cloud native. | Highest cost, longest duration.
globe | Relocate | Move a whole platform to the cloud with minimal change. | Dependencies left behind.
```

```ref
AWS | The seven migration strategies: retire, retain, rehost, relocate, repurchase, replatform, refactor | Know all seven names and give a use for each. Azure Cloud Adoption Framework uses a shorter list, so say which list you follow.
```


## How to choose

```flow
Inventory | List every application, owner, users and dependencies
Assess | Business value, technical health, risk, data sensitivity
Assign | Pick an R for each application
Sequence | Group into waves by dependency and risk
Validate | Check cost, skills and the deadline
```

```ref
Azure | Cloud Adoption Framework, migrate methodology | Assess each application before choosing a strategy. Do not apply lift and shift to everything.
```


## A sample portfolio

| Application | Value | Condition | Strategy | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Old reporting tool | Low | Poor | Retire | Users have moved to the new dashboard |
| Order portal | High | Medium | Replatform | Managed database reduces operations |
| HR leave system | Medium | Poor | Repurchase | SaaS product fits |
| Pricing engine | High | Poor | Refactor | Core, and changes often |
| Legacy archive | Low | Stable | Retain | Regulations require it, no urgency |
| Internal wiki | Low | Fine | Rehost | Quick and low risk |

## Your decision

The lease ends in nine months. Rehosting everything meets the date, but the pricing engine will be costly and fragile in the cloud.

```decision
? What do you recommend?
- Rehost everything, and fix problems later | You meet the date, but you carry cost and risk into the cloud and later fixes rarely get funded.
- Refactor everything for a clean result | The date is missed and costs rise sharply.
+ Rehost or retire the simple ones to meet the date, replatform the order portal, and begin the pricing engine refactor as a parallel track with a temporary rehost as a fallback | You meet the deadline, reduce risk and improve the applications that matter.
= Mix strategies by value and deadline, and keep a fallback for the hardest item.
```

## Risks to watch

```rag
Dependencies | A | Applications talk to each other, plan moves in waves
Data transfer | A | Volumes and cutover windows need testing
Skills | A | The team may be new to the target platform
Cost | A | Estimate run costs before agreeing the strategy
Date | G | Retire and rehost cover the lease deadline
```

## A caution about the number seven

Some organisations use five or six categories, and some add others. The names matter less than the discipline of deciding per application. Check the terms used in your organisation or provider guidance.

## AI and migration

```cards
search | AI assists discovery | Reads configuration and code to suggest dependencies. | It misses hidden links and sounds sure.
code | AI assists refactoring | Suggests code changes and tests. | Subtle behaviour changes and licence questions.
file | AI drafts the plan | Quick first version of waves and risks. | Plans that look neat and ignore your constraints.
```

```ai
Shift | AI speeds up inventory and code analysis for migrations. The decisions stay with people.
Example (illustrative) | AI scans an application and lists dependencies. An engineer finds a batch job missing, and the PM adds it before the migration wave.
Use AI to | Draft application inventories and suggest migration strategies for each.
Keep human | Verifying every dependency map before a wave is approved.
Risk | A missed dependency discovered during cutover.
Control | Have a person verify each map and test it in a rehearsal.
Interview line | AI speeds up inventory and analysis, and a person verifies every dependency map before a migration wave is approved.
```

```ref
NIST | AI RMF Map function | If AI services are part of the target state, assess data location and model risk during migration planning.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Assesses whether an AI feature can move with the app or must be rebuilt. | Treated as a normal server move.
code | Traditional developers | Own refactoring effort and technical debt estimates. | Estimates assume a lift and shift.
shield | AI security consultant | Reviews data movement and new exposure during migration. | Review after cutover.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Builds the pipeline and tests that prove parity after the move. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Parity checked by hand.
money | Finance partner | Watches cost of running both environments. | Dual running cost forgotten.
```

```incident
# AI used to classify apps, strategy chosen too fast (illustrative)
What happened | A team asked an AI tool to assign a migration strategy to a portfolio of applications. The PM adopted the suggestions with little review.
Impact | Two applications marked "retire" were in use by a regional team and had to be restored.
Root cause | The tool lacked business context and the suggestions were not validated with owners.
Resolution | The PM used AI to draft options only, then reviewed each application with its owner before deciding.
PM move | Use AI to draft options and confirm each decision with the application owner.
```

```answer
? How do you choose a migration strategy for each application?
- Rehost everything for speed | It moves problems and misses the benefit.
- Rebuild everything | Cost and risk are high and rarely justified.
+ I assess each application on business value, technical fit, risk, dependencies and cost, then choose among retire, retain, rehost, replatform, repurchase, refactor or relocate, with the owner. I check the caveat that the list of Rs varies by source. I plan waves and review results.
= Per application, not blanket.
= Owner confirms.
= Plan waves.
```

```mindmap
# Migration strategies
Light touch | Retire, retain, rehost
Medium | Replatform, repurchase
Heavy | Refactor, rebuild
Decision inputs | Value, fit, risk, dependencies, cost
```

```flashcards
Rehost | Move as is, often called lift and shift.
Replatform | Make small changes to gain cloud benefits.
Refactor | Redesign the application for the cloud.
Caution | The count and names of the Rs vary by source, so state your list.
```
