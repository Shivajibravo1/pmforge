## "Release it to everyone on Friday"

The new Lighthouse assistant features are ready. The business wants them live for all customers on Friday afternoon. Rahul wants a gradual release. Operations does not want a release before a weekend.

```chat
# Message, Wednesday, 4:30 PM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Marketing has announced Friday. Everyone gets it Friday.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | If something is wrong, everyone gets the problem at once.
Operations lead | Operations | And my weekend team is two people.
```

Deploying and releasing are different. Deploying puts code on production servers. Releasing makes a feature available to users. Separating them gives you control. Modern release strategies reduce the blast radius of a mistake, so that failure affects few people, and recovery is quick.

```key
Reduce risk by limiting exposure and keeping a fast way back. Release to few first, watch real signals, then widen.
```

## Four strategies

```cards
repeat | Blue green | Two identical environments. Switch traffic from the old (blue) to the new (green). Switch back if there is a problem. | Database changes that cannot be undone, and double the infrastructure cost.
bird | Canary | Release to a small share of users first, watch, then widen. | A sample that does not represent real users.
flag | Feature flags | Code ships switched off. Turn the feature on for chosen users, and off again without a deployment. | Old flags that pile up and interact.
refresh | Rolling | Update servers in small batches. | Mixed versions running together during the roll.
```

## A canary release

```timeline
Hour 0 | One percent of users, internal staff first | info
Hour 4 | Five percent, error and satisfaction measures normal | ok
Day 1 | Twenty five percent, support tickets stable | ok
Day 2 | Fifty percent, one defect found and fixed | warn
Day 3 | Everyone | ok
```

The percentages are examples. Agree your own with the team, based on traffic and risk.

## What to watch

```accordion
Technical signals | Errors, latency, resource use, failed requests.
Business signals | Orders, conversions, abandonment, complaints.
Support signals | Ticket volume and topics, call length.
AI signals | Wrong answers, refusals, escalations to humans, cost per query.
```

## Your decision

The business insists on Friday for everyone. Rahul proposes starting Friday for staff and five percent of customers, widening on Monday.

```decision
? What do you recommend to Daniel?
- Release to everyone on Friday as announced | You keep the date, but you take the biggest risk at the worst time for support.
- Move the whole launch to the following week | You protect operations, but disappoint marketing.
+ Keep Friday for the announcement and a staff and five percent customer release behind a feature flag, widen on Monday with agreed go and stop criteria, and prepare a message for customers | The date holds in a meaningful way, risk is limited and the weekend team is protected.
= Keep the headline date and limit exposure. Agree go and stop criteria before you start.
```

## Choosing between strategies

| Situation | Often suitable |
| --- | --- |
| Need an instant, full switch back | Blue green |
| Unknown real world behaviour | Canary |
| Business wants to control timing independently of deployment | Feature flags |
| Many instances of a stateless service | Rolling |

## Rollback needs design

```cards
database | Data changes | A rollback of code may not reverse changes to data. | Releasing a schema change you cannot undo.
clock | Decision time | Agree who decides, using which signals, and by when. | A debate at two in the morning.
check | Rehearsal | A rollback never tried is a hope. | Discovering steps are missing during the incident.
```

## AI features and release

```cards
bird | Canary the model | Compare the new model or prompt against the old on live traffic. | Judging by a few impressive examples.
flag | Flag the feature | Switch it off instantly without a deployment. | A kill switch that is not tested.
users | Human fallback | Route to staff when confidence is low or the feature is off. | A dead end for customers.
```

```ai
Shift | AI releases need stop criteria defined before the release starts.
Example (illustrative) | A canary release of a new model shows rising complaints. The pre agreed stop criteria are met and the team returns to the previous version within minutes.
Use AI to | Draft the release plan and monitoring checks.
Keep human | The decision to stop or continue.
Risk | A canary that continues while complaints rise.
Control | Define maximum wrong answer rate, complaint rate and cost per query. Keep the previous model or a human route ready and test the switch.
Interview line | Before an AI release I define stop criteria and test the switch back to the previous version or a human route.
```
