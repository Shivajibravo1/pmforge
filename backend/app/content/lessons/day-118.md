## The deck that carries the decision

The board has asked for the presentation deck itself, so that members who cannot attend can read it. Victoria warns you that decks get forwarded without the presenter, so every slide must make sense alone.

```chat
# Message, Tuesday, 10:00 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | If this is forwarded to a director who was not in the room, will it still make sense? That is the test.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Tell the story in the titles. If I read only the titles, I should know what you are asking.
Finance partner | Finance | And put the numbers on the slide with their sources.
```

A capstone deck is a finished document, built to be read as well as presented. Each slide has a message title, a visual and brief supporting text, with the source of the evidence. The whole deck should read as a short story from the problem to the ask. A reader should be able to follow the logic in five minutes.

```key
Write the titles first, as sentences that tell the story. Then build each slide to prove its title.
```


## Your decision

The deck is thirty slides. The board has asked for fifteen minutes.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Present all thirty slides, quickly | The audience loses the thread, and questions are cut.
- Cut to five slides and drop the evidence | The board cannot test your claims.
+ Make a core deck of about twelve slides for the presentation, move evidence and detail to a linked appendix, and number appendix slides so you can go to them when asked | Short for the room, complete for the reader.
= Core deck for the story, appendix for the evidence.
```

## Design principles

```accordion
Consistency | One layout, one palette, one set of icons.
Contrast and size | Large readable text, strong contrast and no tiny tables.
Simplicity | One idea per slide, with room around it.
Accessibility | Alt text for images, readable fonts, colour not the only signal.
Diagrams | Use clear boxes and arrows for architecture and flow.
```

## Quality review

```rag
Titles tell the story | A | Reword three titles
Evidence sourced | A | Add source lines
Consistency | G | Layout set
Length | A | Move detail to appendix
Accessibility | A | Check contrast and alt text
```

## What to avoid

```cards
alert | Jargon | Terms the board does not know. | Explain or avoid.
alert | Overclaiming | Certainty about uncertain benefits. | Use ranges.
alert | Decoration | Icons and images with no meaning. | Visual noise.
```

```ai
Shift | AI can polish a deck, and a polished deck with invented content is worse than a plain one.
Example (illustrative) | AI polishes a deck and adds a statistic with no source. The learner removes it and keeps only figures from the plan.

Control | Trace each figure on a slide to your own plan or a cited source.
```

```incident
# AI generated deck, errors on slides (illustrative)
What happened | A learner had an AI tool generate the capstone deck. A chart used numbers that did not appear in the case and a slide named a framework the plan did not use.
Impact | The reviewer questioned the whole deck.
Root cause | Slides were not checked against the source plan and titles were generic.
Resolution | The learner rebuilt titles as messages, checked every number against the plan, removed unsupported claims and kept AI for layout suggestions.
PM move | Check every slide claim against your source before presenting.
```
