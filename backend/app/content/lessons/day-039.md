## The team that never finishes anything

Lighthouse also has a support team, led by Priya. They do not work in sprints. Requests arrive daily, from small fixes to urgent requests. Their board looks busy and nothing seems to be done.

```chat
# Service desk board review, Tuesday, 9:20 AM
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | We have twenty-four items in progress. Everyone is busy. Nothing closes.
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | Then hire more people.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | I would first limit how many things we start. Starting is easy. Finishing is the hard part.
```

Kanban is a method for managing flow. It does not use sprints. It makes work visible, limits how much is in progress at once and focuses on finishing. It is especially suited to support and operations teams, where work arrives continuously.

```key
Kanban improves flow by limiting work in progress. Finishing one thing is worth more than starting three.
```

## Four ideas of Kanban

```cards
eye | Visualise the work | A board with columns for each state. | Hidden work that nobody sees.
flag | Limit work in progress | A cap on items in each column. | Everyone starts, nobody finishes.
clock | Manage flow | Watch how long items take and where they wait. | Waiting time is invisible and large.
workflow | Improve continuously | Small changes, measured. | Big reorganisations that do not stick.
```

## Metrics

```cards
clock | Lead time | From request to delivery, as the customer sees it. | Measured from start of work and flattering.
workflow | Cycle time | From start of work to done. | Average hides long tails.
chart | Throughput | Items finished per period. | Counting items of wildly different sizes as equal.
layers | Work in progress | Items currently started and not finished. | Grows quietly when no limit exists.
```

Little's Law links them: average cycle time equals average work in progress divided by average throughput. Illustrative: if the team has 24 items in progress and finishes 4 items a week, the average cycle time is 24 divided by 4 = 6 weeks. If you limit work in progress to 8 and throughput stays at 4 a week, the average cycle time falls to 8 divided by 4 = 2 weeks.

```stats
24 | Items in progress now
4 | Finished per week
6 | Average cycle time (weeks)
2 | Cycle time with a limit of 8 (weeks)
```

The law holds for stable systems, and the real effect on throughput varies. Treat the example as an illustration.

## Your decision

Priya's team believes limiting work in progress will make them look lazy.

```decision
? How do you introduce limits?
- Impose a hard limit and monitor compliance | The team follows the number and resents it, and workarounds appear.
- Skip limits, since the team is already busy | The queue grows and the cycle time stays long.
+ Start with a limit slightly below the current level, such as 16, explain Little's Law with their own numbers, and review it every two weeks together | The team sees why and adjusts the limit by evidence.
= Introduce limits with data and agreement. Let the team find the right number.
```

## Scrum or Kanban

| Question | Leans Scrum | Leans Kanban |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is work planned in batches toward a goal? | Yes | No |
| Does work arrive continuously and unpredictably? | No | Yes |
| Does the team benefit from a fixed rhythm? | Yes | Not required |
| Is the team mostly support or operations? | No | Yes |

Many teams combine both, often called Scrumban.
