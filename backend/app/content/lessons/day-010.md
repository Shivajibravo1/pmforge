## Same sentence, three different products

The business case is approved. Marcus Webb, your business analyst, writes one line on the board: "Customers should be able to track their orders." Then he asks three people what it means.

```chat
# Requirements workshop, Monday, 11:00 AM
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | It means the customer sees a status and does not phone us.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | It means a page that reads from the order system. Real time or near real time?
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | It means tracking like a parcel app, with a map.
```

Three people, three products. Six weeks later the team has built something nobody wanted, because each person understood that sentence differently. Requirements documents exist to prevent that, and the skill is knowing which one to use for which job.

## Three documents, three jobs

```flow
BRD | What the business needs and why
FRD | What the system must do
User story | One slice of value
Acceptance criteria | When it counts as done
```

| Document | Answers | Written by | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BRD (business requirements document) | What the business needs and why | Business analyst with the business | Customers must be able to track an order without calling support |
| FRD (functional requirements document) | What the system must do | Analyst with the technical team | The system shall display order status updated within 5 minutes of a warehouse scan |
| User story | One slice of value, in a user's words | Product owner with the team | As a customer, I want to see my order status so that I do not need to phone support |

```ref
IIBA | BABOK Guide, requirements analysis and design definition | Interview line: BRD states the business need, FRD states system behaviour, stories state user value. Mention BABOK to show a business analysis background.
```


## What makes a requirement good

```cards
eye | Clear | One reading only. No hidden meaning. | Two teams build two different things.
check | Testable | If you cannot say how to test it, it is not yet a requirement. | You cannot prove it is done.
target | Necessary | Linked to a real business need. | Features nobody asked for.
link | Consistent and traceable | No conflicts with others, and you can trace it to its source. | Two requirements contradict each other.
```

## Your decision

Daniel wants "like a parcel app, with a map". Rahul says the order system has no location data.

```decision
? How do you handle the map request?
- Add "map tracking" to the requirements to keep the sponsor happy | The team commits to something the data cannot support, and the gap is found in month three.
- Tell Daniel the map is impossible and move on | You are probably right, but Daniel feels dismissed and raises it again at every steering meeting.
+ Record "map" as a separate, later requirement with a note that location data does not exist yet, and keep the first release to status and expected delivery date | The sponsor sees his idea is logged, the team builds what the data supports, and a future release has a clear starting point.
= Do not say yes to everything or no to everything. Write the idea down, state the constraint, and slice the first release to what is true today.
```

## Acceptance criteria: how you know it is done

Each story needs conditions that define done. A common format is Given, When, Then.

```accordion
Given a customer with a shipped order | When they open the tracking page, then they see the current status and expected delivery date.
Given an order with no shipment scan | When they open the page, then they see a clear "preparing" message.
```

```ref
Scrum | Scrum Guide, Definition of Done | Acceptance criteria belong to the story. Definition of Done applies to every item. Say the difference clearly.
PMI | Agile Practice Guide, user stories and acceptance criteria | Use for story format and how acceptance criteria prove a story is done.
```


## Requirements for AI features

AI makes drafting requirements quicker. Asking an AI to turn a business need into user stories is a good use of it. But AI features also need requirements that ordinary features do not.

```accordion
Accuracy | State a measurable standard, for example: at least 90 percent of answers rated correct by reviewers on a test set of 200 questions. The number is an example. Agree your own with the business.
Fallback to a human | Given a question the assistant cannot answer, when a customer asks it, then it says so and offers a person.
Data limits | Given a request that needs personal data the assistant must not see, when it is asked, then it declines and explains why.
```

```ai
AI can draft the story. You make it testable. If a requirement cannot be checked by a person with a clear pass or fail, send it back.
```

```ref
OWASP | ASVS (Application Security Verification Standard) | A source of testable security requirements for acceptance criteria on any feature.
NIST | AI RMF Map function | Write AI requirements for purpose, limits and human oversight, not only accuracy.
```

```cards
cpu | AI engineer | Turns requirements into evaluation cases and thresholds. | Requirements say answer accurately and stop there.
handshake | Forward deployed engineer | Collects real examples and edge cases from users. | Examples never reach the requirement set.
shield | AI security consultant | Adds misuse and privacy requirements. | Security written as a late non functional note.
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Turns limits, approvals and logging into requirements for agent behaviour. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Agent permissions left unspecified.
code | Traditional developers | Need clear interfaces and error behaviour for the AI parts. | Model output assumed to be exact.
```

```incident
# Requirement too vague to test (illustrative)
What happened | A requirement said the assistant must answer questions accurately. The team and the business each had a different idea of accurate.
Impact | Acceptance was disputed and go live was delayed.
Root cause | There were no acceptance criteria, no examples and no measured threshold.
Resolution | The team agreed example questions, an accuracy threshold, a rule for citing sources and a route to a human when unsure.
PM move | Write AI requirements as measurable acceptance criteria with examples, and agree them before build.
```

## Where requirements go wrong

```cards
alert | Vague words | "Fast", "easy", "modern" mean different things to different people. | The team and the sponsor disagree at acceptance.
file | No source | A requirement with no business need behind it cannot be defended or prioritised. | Pet features crowd out real needs.
clock | Frozen too early | In agile work, detail is added just in time, but the purpose must stay clear. | Either endless debate or building blind.
```

## Which one should I use?

Predictive projects lean on the BRD and FRD. Agile projects lean on user stories and acceptance criteria. Hybrid projects, like Lighthouse, often keep a short BRD for approval and manage the detail as stories.

## Check your understanding

```quiz
? Which of these is a testable requirement?
- The system should be fast
- The page must feel modern
+ Order status updates within 5 minutes of a warehouse scan
= It names a measurable condition you can check. The others cannot be tested.
```

```answer
? What is the difference between a BRD, an FRD and user stories, and when do you use each?
- They are the same thing with different names | They serve different readers and levels of detail.
- Always use user stories | Some contracts, regulators and vendors need fuller documents.
+ A BRD states the business need and outcomes for sponsors. An FRD describes what the system must do in detail for builders and testers. User stories capture needs in small testable pieces for iterative teams, with acceptance criteria. I choose by audience and contract: formal documents for fixed scope and regulated work, stories for agile delivery, and often both in hybrid projects. For AI features I add measurable acceptance criteria and example cases.
= Describe each by its reader.
= Say acceptance criteria are what make any of them testable.
= Mention AI acceptance criteria with examples. It shows current thinking.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
BRD: what and why. FRD: what the system does. Story: one slice of value
A requirement you cannot test is not yet a requirement
Acceptance criteria define done in Given, When, Then form
Slice the first release to what the data supports today
Match the document to the lifecycle: predictive, agile or hybrid
```
