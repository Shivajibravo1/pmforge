## Three documents, three jobs

Teams confuse these constantly. Knowing the difference prevents rework.

```flow
BRD | What the business needs and why
FRD | What the system must do
User story | One slice of value
Acceptance criteria | When it counts as done
```

| Document | Answers | Written by | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BRD (business requirements document) | What the business needs and why | Business analyst with the business | Customers must be able to track an order without calling support |
| FRD (functional requirements document) | What the system must do | Analyst with the technical team | The system shall display order status updated within 5 minutes of a warehouse scan |
| User story | One slice of value, in a user's words | Product owner with the team | As a customer, I want to see my order status so that I do not need to phone support |

## Good requirements are

```cards
eye | Clear | One reading, no hidden meaning.
check | Testable | If you cannot describe how to test it, it is not yet a requirement.
target | Necessary | Traceable to a business need.
link | Consistent and traceable | No conflicts with other requirements, linked to their source.
```

## Acceptance criteria

Each story needs conditions that define done. A common format is Given, When, Then:

```accordion
Given a customer with a shipped order | When they open the tracking page, then they see the current status and expected delivery date.
Given an order with no shipment scan | When they open the page, then they see a clear preparing message.
```

## Which to use

Predictive projects lean on BRD and FRD. Agile projects lean on user stories and acceptance criteria. Hybrid projects often keep a short BRD for approval and manage detail as stories.

```quiz
? Which statement is a testable requirement?
- The system should be fast
- The page must feel modern
+ Order status updates within 5 minutes of a warehouse scan
= It names a measurable condition you can check.
```

## Check yourself

```tip
Write one business requirement for your capstone, then split it into two user stories, each with two acceptance criteria.
```
