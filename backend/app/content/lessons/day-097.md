## "Will it pay for itself?"

The feasibility test has gone well enough to ask for the next stage of funding. Victoria wants a full AI business case for the support assistant, one that finance, legal and the board can all read. She wants to know if it will pay for itself, what could go wrong and how you will know.

```chat

Elena Rossi | CISO | And show the security and privacy costs. They are real.

```

Day 94 gave you the canvas and a first business case. This is the full version for a decision. A serious AI business case is built on pilot evidence, shows total cost over three years, includes governance and oversight and sets out the conditions under which you would stop.

```key
A credible AI business case is built on measured pilot results, shows total cost of ownership, separates cash savings from capacity released and includes stop criteria.
```

## Structure

```flow
Summary | The decision, the ask and the headline numbers
Problem | The baseline today, measured
Solution | What is proposed and the alternatives
Evidence | Pilot results on an evaluation set and with real users
Financials | Costs and benefits over three years in ranges
Risks | Technical, legal, ethical, people and vendor
Plan | Stages, gates and owners
Decision | The approval requested and the conditions
```

## Total cost of ownership

```cards
users | People | Build team, domain experts, reviewers and training. | Forgetting the reviewers.
coins | Usage and platform | Model calls, hosting, search, monitoring, security tooling. | Costs that grow with success.
database | Data work | Cleaning, labelling, maintaining sources. | Treating data as free.
shield | Governance and security | Reviews, testing, audits and incident readiness. | Cost shown as overhead and cut.
refresh | Ongoing improvement | Tuning, evaluation updates and retraining. | A project budget that ends at launch.
```

```ref
FinOps | Unit economics and total cost of ownership | Include build, run, monitoring, support and change. Run cost often exceeds build cost.
```


## Benefits, carefully

```accordion
Cash savings | Reduced spend, such as avoided outsourcing or overtime. Only count what budgets will actually change.
Capacity released | Time that staff can use elsewhere. Show how it will be used, and do not convert to cash unless staff numbers change.
Quality and risk | Fewer errors and incidents, measured against a baseline.
Revenue and retention | Only with a clear link and a way to measure.
Strategic | Skills, learning and options, shown separately with no figure.
```

## Stop criteria

```timeline
Gate 1 | Evaluation set meets quality and safety thresholds, or stop | warn
Gate 2 | Pilot users: resolution rate, satisfaction and escalation within range, or stop | warn
Gate 3 | Cost per resolved query below the limit, or redesign | warn
Gate 4 | Governance sign off by security, legal and the service owner | ok
```

```ref
PMI | PMBOK Guide, governance and stage gates | Set stop criteria before spending, not after. Say them in your business case.
```


## Your decision

The board wants the headline return on investment as one figure. The conservative case shows a payback in around two and a half years. The optimistic case shows under one year.

```decision
? How do you present it?
- Lead with the optimistic payback, to win approval | The first delay will look like failure, and credibility drops.
- Show only the conservative case, to be safe | Perhaps too cautious to support a decision, and it hides upside.
+ Present all three cases with the assumptions that distinguish them, recommend funding the next stage on the conservative case and show which measured results would move you toward the optimistic one | The board sees the range, understands the drivers and decides with open eyes.
= Show the range, name the drivers and base the decision on the conservative case.
```

## Risk and control view

```rag
Quality | A | Evaluation thresholds and sampling
Privacy and security | A | Controls and testing planned
Regulatory | A | Review of the classification and duties
Vendor dependence | A | Exit and fallback plan
Adoption | A | Staff involvement and training
Cost growth | A | Budgets, limits and alerts
```

## Vendor and exit

```cards
link | Lock in | Proprietary prompts, tools and data formats make leaving costly. | Discovering it when prices change.
file | Contract | Data use, retention, location, audit rights, price changes, support. | Standard terms accepted without review.
undo | Exit plan | A way to switch provider or return to human service. | No fallback if the provider fails.
```

```ref
NIST | SP 800-161 supply chain risk management | Plan exit: data return, portability and notice periods.
ISO | ISO/IEC 42001 | Third party AI use should be covered by your management system.
```
