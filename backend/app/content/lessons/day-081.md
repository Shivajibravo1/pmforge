## "Show me the security plan"

The Nimbus board has asked for one security plan covering the cloud platform and the assistant. Elena will own it. You will write it with her. The board wants to see risk, controls, cost and dates, in a form they can approve.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 9:15 AM
Daniel Okafor | Sponsor | The board asks one thing: are we secure enough to launch, and what will it take to stay that way.
Elena Rossi | CISO | I will not say "secure". I will say what we have done, what remains and what we have accepted.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | Give us the plan in backlog form, so we can deliver it.
```

A security plan is a delivery document, not a policy. It says what you will protect, which risks matter most, which controls reduce them, who owns each, what evidence you will keep and how you will review. A strong plan connects the threat model, the compliance obligations and the pipeline checks into one story.

```key
A security plan translates risk into owned, dated work. It should be short enough to be read and specific enough to be delivered.
```

## Structure

```cards
target | Scope and assets | What systems and data are covered, and what is critical. | Plans that cover everything and therefore nothing.
alert | Top risks | The ranked risks from the threat model and assessments. | Generic risk lists.
shield | Controls | Preventive, detective and responsive measures, mapped to risks. | Controls without owners or tests.
calendar | Roadmap | Dated actions with owners, in order of risk reduction. | Aspirations without dates.
file | Evidence and compliance | What records prove the controls work. | Controls that cannot be shown to an auditor.
eye | Monitoring and response | Detection, incident process and contacts. | A plan that stops at launch.
```

```ref
NIST | CSF 2.0 functions as a plan structure | Use Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover as chapters of the security plan.
CIS | Controls v8 Implementation Groups | Pick the group that matches organisation size and risk, then scope the plan.
```


## From risk to delivery

```flow
Risks | Threat model, vulnerability findings, compliance gaps
Controls | Choose measures that reduce each risk
Backlog | Create work items with acceptance criteria
Owners | Name a person and a date for each
Evidence | Define what proof will exist
Review | Monthly check and a board summary
```

## Your decision

The board asks for a security plan of one page. Elena prefers forty pages with every control.

```decision
? How do you reconcile?
- Give the board the forty pages | They will not read it, and the key decisions will be buried.
- Give only one page and no detail | The board cannot trace decisions, and delivery lacks guidance.
+ Write a one page board summary with top risks, status, decisions needed and dates, backed by a detailed plan and a backlog for delivery teams | Each audience gets what it needs, and the layers stay consistent.
= Layer the document: summary for decision makers, plan for owners, backlog for builders.
```

```incident
# Security plan ignored the AI assistant (illustrative)
What happened | A security plan covered networks, identity and the application. The AI assistant, its retrieval store and its tool access were not in it.
Impact | A review before launch found no owner, no controls and no monitoring for the AI parts.
Root cause | The plan was built from a template for conventional applications.
Resolution | The PM added an AI section with threats, controls, owners, cost and monitoring, and a board summary with residual risks.
PM move | Add an explicit AI section to the security plan.
```
