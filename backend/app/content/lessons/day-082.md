## "We delivered it. Now who runs it?"

Lighthouse's portal went live last month. Complaints arrive through three channels: email, phone and chat. Nobody agrees on priorities. Developers are pulled into daily questions. The service desk says it does not know which system owns which feature.

```chat
# Operations review, Monday, 4:00 PM
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | We have no list of services, no owners and no agreed response times.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | My team spends half its week answering support questions.
Victoria Hale | COO | The project is finished. Why does it feel like a mess?
```

Delivery is not the end of the story. Value is created when a service works for users over time. ITIL 4 is a widely used framework for managing services. Its central idea is the service value system, which describes how all the activities of an organisation combine to create value. A project manager should understand it, because the project hands over to it.

```key
ITIL is about co-creating value with customers, through services that are managed from demand to delivery, not about forms and tickets alone.
```

## The service value system

```arch
Guiding principles | Focus on value, start where you are, progress iteratively, collaborate, think holistically, keep it simple, optimise and automate
Governance | Direction and control from the organisation
Service value chain | Plan, improve, engage, design and transition, obtain or build, deliver and support
Practices | Sets of resources and skills, such as incident, change, problem and service level management
Continual improvement | Applied at every level
```

Check the official ITIL 4 publications for the current wording and the list of practices, because details are updated by the owner of the framework.

```ref
ITIL | ITIL 4 service value system: guiding principles, governance, service value chain, practices, continual improvement | Draw the system on a whiteboard. It is the core ITIL 4 interview answer.
```


## The service value chain

```flow
Plan | Strategy, portfolio and architecture
Improve | Continual improvement of everything
Engage | Understand needs and keep stakeholders involved
Design and transition | Design services and move them into use
Obtain or build | Acquire or create components
Deliver and support | Run the service and help users
```

```ref
ITIL | Service value chain activities: plan, improve, engage, design and transition, obtain or build, deliver and support | Show where projects live in the chain and where operations take over.
```


## Where projects meet services

```cards
rocket | Design and transition | Your project mostly lives here. | A project that ends before operations are ready.
users | Engage | Customers and users shape what is built and how it is supported. | A service no one asked for.
headset | Deliver and support | Run, fix and help. | Support added at the end.
refresh | Improve | Learn from incidents and feedback. | Lessons never applied.
```

## Key practices for a PM

```accordion
Service level management | Agree and measure what customers can expect.
Incident management | Restore service quickly when something breaks.
Problem management | Find and fix root causes.
Change enablement | Assess and authorise changes with risk in mind.
Service desk | A single point of contact for users.
Service catalogue | A clear list of services, owners and descriptions.
```

```ref

```


## Your decision

Developers want to hand the system to the service desk with a short email and move on. Priya says she cannot accept it without training and documentation.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Support the developers, because the project is complete | The service desk and customers pay for gaps that you could have closed.
- Block the handover until every document is perfect | The team and budget are stuck, and the service has no owner.
+ Agree a service acceptance checklist with operations, including owner, support hours, runbooks, training, monitoring and escalation, hold a short handover with named people, and keep project support through a defined hypercare period | Operations accepts a service they can run, and developers still have a clear exit.
= Acceptance criteria for operations are part of done. Agree them early.
```

## Service acceptance checklist

```checklist
Service owner and support team named
Service description and users documented
Support hours, contacts and escalation path
Runbooks and known issues
Monitoring and alerts active
Training given to service desk and operations
Service levels agreed
Backup, recovery and continuity tested
Handover meeting held and recorded
```

**ITIL and agile**

```cards
repeat | Complementary | ITIL stresses stable, managed services. Agile and DevOps stress fast change. | Treating them as rivals.
workflow | Lean ITIL | Use only the practices needed and automate them. | Heavy forms for every small change.
users | Shared goal | Value for customers, with safety. | Silos between delivery and operations.
```

## AI services need service management

```cards
brain | AI as a service | Needs an owner, service levels, a support route and a lifecycle. | An assistant with no named owner after launch.
chart | Quality as a service level | Accuracy, escalation rate and complaint rate are service measures. | Tracking only uptime.
refresh | Model lifecycle | Updates, retirement and retraining need change control. | Silent updates that alter behaviour.
```

```ai
Shift | An AI service belongs in the service catalogue like any other service, with extra fields for model and quality.
Example (illustrative) | An AI help assistant is live but absent from the service catalogue. The PM adds owner, data, model version, quality targets and a support route.
Use AI to | Draft catalogue entries and support articles.
Keep human | Owning the service and approving changes.
Risk | A shadow AI service nobody supports or reviews.
Control | Add owner, purpose, data used, model and version, service levels including quality measures, support route and review dates.
Interview line | I add each AI service to the catalogue with owner, data, model and version, quality service levels, support route and review dates.
```
