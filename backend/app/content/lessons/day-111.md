## "One pack for the board"

The assistant has launched in stages. Now the board audit committee asks a plain question: show us how you govern AI at Lighthouse. Victoria turns to you. She wants one pack that brings together everything produced so far, in a form that a director can read in twenty minutes and an auditor can follow in two hours.

```chat
# Message, Monday, 9:00 AM
Victoria Hale | COO | The audit committee meets in three weeks. I want an AI governance pack. Policy, roles, risk, evidence, incidents, and what we will do next.
Legal counsel | Legal | Include the regulatory mapping and the open items honestly.
Elena Rossi | CISO | And the security tests with dates.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | And what my team does when the assistant is wrong.
```

An AI governance pack is a structured set of documents that shows how an organisation decides, controls and monitors its use of AI. It is not a thick policy manual. It is a map: who is accountable, which systems exist, how risk is assessed, what evidence supports decisions and how problems are handled. It should let a reader move from a principle to a record in a few clicks.

```key
Governance is the answer to three questions: who decides, on what evidence, and what happens when things go wrong. The pack makes the answers visible.
```

## Contents

```cards
scale | Policy and principles | Short statement of purpose, boundaries and responsibilities. | A policy that nobody applies.
users | Roles and decision rights | Owner, risk owners, review board, escalation. | Committees with no named accountable person.
list | AI inventory | Every system with purpose, owner, data, provider and risk tier. | Shadow tools missing.
chart | Risk assessments | Register, impact assessments, ratings and decisions. | Assessments done once.
check | Evidence | Evaluation results, security tests, model cards, approvals. | Claims without proof.
alert | Incident and change | Incident procedures, review of events and change control. | Procedures never rehearsed.
```

## One page summary

| Question | Answer in the pack |
| --- | --- |
| Which AI systems do we run | Inventory with tier and owner |
| Who is accountable | Named owners and the review board |
| How do we decide to launch | Stage gates with required evidence |
| How do we know it works | Evaluation suite and monitoring results |
| What are the top risks | Ranked from the register with status |
| What went wrong | Incident log with actions |
| What is next | Roadmap with dates and owners |

## Your decision

Two weeks before the meeting, the review finds that one AI tool used by the marketing team is not in the inventory and processes customer lists.

```decision
? What do you do?
- Leave it out of the pack, since the board will not notice | You present an incomplete picture, and the audit will find it.
- Delay the pack until the tool is fully remediated | You miss the meeting, and the board loses visibility.
+ Add it to the inventory, assess it with Legal and Security, stop the data flow if there is no lawful basis, record the finding and the actions in the pack and tell the committee openly | Honest disclosure with action is the strongest evidence of governance.
= Show the gap and the response. A real finding handled well builds trust.
```

## AI and the pack

```cards

users | Human attestation | Owners sign each section. | Nobody takes responsibility.
```

```ai
Shift | Governance packs are relied on, so every claim needs evidence.
Example (illustrative) | AI flags claims in a governance pack with no evidence link. The PM finds two outdated figures and corrects them.
Use AI to | Check that claims have links to evidence.
Keep human | Verifying a sample by hand. A person opens the evidence behind several claims and confirms it supports them.
Risk | A polished pack with unsupported claims.
Control | Have AI flag unsupported claims, then verify a sample yourself.
Interview line | In a governance pack accuracy matters more than polish. I check claims against evidence and sample them by hand.
```

```incident
# Governance pack approved, then out of date (illustrative)
What happened | A board approved an AI governance pack. Two model updates later the pack still described the first model.
Impact | An internal review found decisions based on documents that no longer matched the system.
Root cause | The pack had no owner, no refresh trigger and no link to change control.
Resolution | The PM named owners, tied updates to model and prompt changes, added version numbers and a quarterly review, and put a one page summary on top.
PM move | Give the pack owners and a refresh trigger tied to change control.
```
