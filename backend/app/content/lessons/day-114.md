## "It was fine in March"

Six months after launch, a Lighthouse analyst notices that the assistant's accuracy on delivery questions has slipped. Nothing broke. No alert fired. The company changed courier partners, introduced new delivery options and customers began using phrases the system had never seen.

```chat
# Review, Thursday, 2:00 PM
Analyst | Data team | Accuracy on delivery questions was high in March. This month's sample shows a clear drop.
Rahul Mehta | Lead developer | The courier changed in June. Our documents still describe the old rules.
Priya Nair | Service desk lead | Customers are using the new terms. The assistant does not know them.
Victoria Hale | COO | Why did we find out from a spreadsheet and not from an alert?
```

AI systems degrade quietly. The world changes, the data changes and users change. Drift is the gradual change in the relationship between a system and its environment, so that performance falls even though the system itself is unchanged. Monitoring detects it. Human in the loop controls limit the harm while you do. Both are part of the operating model, not extras.

```key
Plan monitoring and human oversight before launch, and fund them as running costs. A system that nobody watches is a system that drifts.
```

## Types of drift

```cards
database | Data drift | The inputs change, such as new products, language or customer behaviour. | New terms the system never saw.
file | Content drift | Documents and policies change and the knowledge base is outdated. | Answers quoting last year's rules.
refresh | Model drift | The model version or provider behaviour changes. | A silent update that alters results.
users | Concept drift | The meaning of a good answer changes, for example new rules. | Old definition of correct.
```

## What to monitor

```accordion
Quality | Sampled correctness, citation accuracy, refusal and handover rates.
Safety and security | Blocked attacks, policy violations, leak tests and anomalies.
Fairness | Performance by language, region or customer group, where relevant.
Experience | Ratings, complaints, escalations, abandonment.
Operations | Latency, errors, availability.
Cost | Cost per resolved query and usage patterns.
Data | Freshness of documents, new terms and topics.
```

## A monitoring loop

```flow
Collect | Logs, ratings, samples and tickets
Analyse | Dashboards, thresholds and trends
Alert | When a measure crosses a limit
Investigate | Find the cause: data, content, model or prompt
Fix | Update sources, prompts or model through change control
Verify | Re run the evaluation set and compare
```

## Human in the loop

```cards
users | Review | A person approves outputs before they are used, for higher risk cases. | Rubber stamping at speed.
alert | Handover | The system passes uncertain or sensitive cases to a person. | A dead end for the customer.
star | Sampling | Staff review a random share to measure quality. | Samples that are too small or biased.
heart | Feedback | Staff and customers can flag errors easily, and see fixes. | Complaints that go nowhere.
```

**Design human oversight well**

```accordion
Meaningful | The person has time, information and authority to override.
Trained | Reviewers know the system's weaknesses and the policy.
Fatigue aware | Volume and layout should not encourage automatic approval.
Measured | Track override rates and reviewer agreement.
Accountable | Name who is responsible for decisions.
```

```ref
EU | AI Act human oversight for high risk systems | Human in the loop means a person with authority, competence and time to override.
```


## Your decision

Monitoring costs money and staff time. The sponsor suggests reducing the sampling to save cost, since the assistant has performed well for six months.

```decision
? What do you recommend?
- Reduce sampling by half, as the system seems stable | You reduce your ability to see the drift that has just happened.
- Increase sampling to everything | Costly and unneeded for low risk topics.
+ Keep sampling but tier it by risk: more for refunds, safety and new topics, less for stable low risk ones, add automatic alerts on key measures and review the plan quarterly | You focus effort where it protects most, with fewer surprises.
= Sample by risk, not evenly. Automate alerts for the main measures.
```

**Responding to drift**

```timeline
Day 1 | Alert or sample shows a drop, open an incident or problem record | warn
Day 2 | Identify the cause: outdated documents and new terms | info
Day 3 | Update documents, add test cases, adjust retrieval | ok
Day 4 | Re run evaluation, release to a small share | ok
Day 7 | Compare, widen, close the problem and record lessons | ok
```

## AI angle

```ai
Shift | Monitoring AI means watching drift and keeping people in the loop.
Example (illustrative) | Complaints cluster around a new product name. The team updates the knowledge base and adds the name to evaluation, and a named owner confirms the fix.
Use AI to | Cluster complaints and detect new terms.
Keep human | Reviewing the clusters and the proposed fixes. A person confirms that a pattern is real and the fix is safe.
Risk | Automatic detection treated as the answer.
Control | Treat detection as a signal and keep a named human accountable for the response.
Interview line | I monitor quality, not only uptime, with human review of samples and a named owner for each alert.
```

```cards
wrench | Agent harness engineer | Builds the logging, traces and alerts for quality, cost and tool calls. The term is not standardised, so confirm the scope. | Logs too thin to reproduce a failure.
cpu | AI engineer | Runs evaluations on fresh samples and tunes retrieval and prompts. | Evaluating only on the original test set.
compass | Agentic AI engineer | Defines which agent actions need human review and how they are sampled. | Review stops once the agent seems reliable.
bot | Forward deployed engineer | Brings user feedback from the customer site into the backlog. | Feedback stays in chat messages.
shield | AI security consultant | Watches for abuse patterns and leakage in logs. | Security review only at launch.
```

```incident
# Answers quoted last year's policy (illustrative)
What happened | The assistant kept quoting an old leave policy after HR had published a new one.
Impact | Staff received wrong guidance and complaints reached HR.
Root cause | The knowledge base had no owner or refresh process, and monitoring tracked uptime and response time, not answer accuracy.
Resolution | A content owner was named for each document set, refresh followed each policy change, a reviewer sampled answers weekly, and an alert flagged citations of outdated documents.
PM move | Add quality and content freshness to the dashboard, with an owner and a response time for each alert.
```

## What to remember

```checklist
AI systems drift as data, content and models change
Monitor quality, safety, fairness, experience, cost and data freshness
Alert on thresholds and investigate causes
Human in the loop must be meaningful and measured
Sample by risk and review the plan regularly
```
